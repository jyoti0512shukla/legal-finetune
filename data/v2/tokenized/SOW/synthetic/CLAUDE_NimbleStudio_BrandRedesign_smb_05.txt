ARTICLE 1 — PROJECT DESCRIPTION AND SCOPE

1.1 What we're doing. The Studio is going to redesign the Client's brand identity — logo system, color palette, typography, and core brand guidelines — and produce the asset library the Client needs to roll the new identity out across its website, packaging, and social channels. The goal is a brand system that the Client can use without coming back to us for every new asset.

1.2 What's in scope. The Studio will:

(a) Run a 2-hour brand discovery workshop with up to 5 of the Client's stakeholders to understand the current brand, the audience, and the strategic direction.

(b) Deliver three (3) initial logo concept directions for the Client to react to.

(c) Refine the chosen direction across two (2) rounds of revisions.

(d) Produce the final logo system in vector format (SVG, AI, EPS) plus rasterized exports (PNG, JPG) at the standard sizes for web, print, and social.

(e) Build out the supporting brand system: color palette (with hex, RGB, CMYK, and Pantone values), typography pairings (heading and body), and a core set of brand patterns and graphic elements.

(f) Produce a Brand Guidelines PDF (~25 pages) covering logo usage, clear-space rules, color, typography, imagery direction, voice and tone basics, and do's-and-don'ts.

(g) Produce starter templates for the Client's most common applications: business card, letterhead, email signature, social profile graphics, and a basic web header.

1.3 What's out of scope. The following things are not included. We're happy to do them as a separate Change Order, but they're not in this SOW:

(a) Anything beyond two rounds of revisions on the logo. After two rounds, more rounds are billable.

(b) Naming, taglines, brand strategy work, or brand voice writing beyond the basics in the Brand Guidelines.

(c) Full website design or development. The web header in Section 1.2(g) is a starter, not a full site.

(d) Photography, illustration, or video production.

(e) Trademark search, trademark filing, or any other legal work. The Client is responsible for confirming the new identity is clear to use.

(f) Print production, packaging dielines, or working with print vendors.

1.4 When we're working on this. We'll start on {{PROJECT_START_DATE}} and aim to wrap by {{PROJECT_END_DATE}}, subject to the Client doing its part on time (Article 5).

ARTICLE 2 — DELIVERABLES AND ACCEPTANCE

2.1 Deliverables. The Studio will deliver:

(a) Discovery Summary — a short written summary of the discovery workshop, including the agreed strategic direction. Target: end of Project Week 1.

(b) Logo Concepts — three initial logo concept directions in PDF presentation form. Target: end of Project Week 3.

(c) Refined Logo (Round 1) — refinements to the chosen direction. Target: end of Project Week 5.

(d) Refined Logo (Round 2) — second round of refinements. Target: end of Project Week 6.

(e) Final Logo Package — finalized logo system in all the formats listed in Section 1.2(d). Target: end of Project Week 7.

(f) Brand System Package — color palette, typography, brand patterns. Target: end of Project Week 9.

(g) Brand Guidelines PDF and Starter Templates. Target: end of Project Week 11.

2.2 How acceptance works. After we deliver something, the Client has five (5) business days to either accept it or send us a single consolidated written response listing what isn't working. If we don't hear back in five business days, we'll send a written reminder. After the reminder, if we still haven't heard back in another three (3) business days, the Deliverable is deemed accepted — we know the Client is busy, but we have to keep the project moving.

2.3 Cure. If the Client tells us something isn't working, we'll fix it and resend within five (5) business days at no extra cost. The fix is bounded by the original scope — we can't redo the logo direction at this stage.

2.4 Revision limits. The "two rounds of revisions" in Section 1.2(c) means two rounds of consolidated feedback after the initial concepts. Anything beyond that — including new logo directions, restarting from scratch, or adding new applications not in Section 1.2(g) — requires a Change Order.

ARTICLE 5 — CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES

5.1 What we need from the Client. To do our part, we need the Client to:

(a) Designate one decision-maker who can approve work on behalf of the Client. We can't run the project through a committee — one person needs to give us yes-or-no answers on the deliverables.

(c) Provide existing brand materials, customer-research summaries, and any brand-related materials in the Client's possession that would help us understand the starting point.

(d) Respond to deliverables and questions within five (5) business days. If something is going to be slower than that, we need a heads up so we can adjust the schedule.

5.2 Accuracy. The Client is responsible for the accuracy of any materials it gives us — including any text content, customer quotes, or competitive references. We'll rely on what the Client gives us without independent verification.

5.3 If the Client is late. If the Client takes longer than the five-business-day window to respond, the Project End Date moves out by the number of days the Client is late. We won't charge for the delay, but we also can't stop other client work to absorb it.

ARTICLE 6 — ASSUMPTIONS AND DEPENDENCIES

6.1 What we're assuming.

(a) The Client is open to a real redesign. If the Client wants a small refresh of the existing logo (rather than a redesign), the scope and fee are different — let us know and we'll do a Change Order.

(b) The Client has a single brand identity, not multiple sub-brands or product brands. Sub-brand work is a separate scope.

(c) The Client's existing brand materials and customer-research summaries are available to share with us within the first week.

(d) The two rounds of revisions in Section 2.4 will be sufficient to land on a final direction. Most of our projects land in two rounds; the few that don't go to a Change Order.

(e) The Client is the rightful owner of any reference materials it shares with us. We'll assume that and won't run independent IP checks.

6.2 If an assumption is wrong. If any of the above turns out not to be true, we'll let the Client know and we'll work out a Change Order together.
