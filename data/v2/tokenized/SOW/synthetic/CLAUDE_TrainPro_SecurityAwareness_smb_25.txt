ARTICLE 1 — PROJECT DESCRIPTION AND SCOPE

1.1 What you're getting

The Provider is a security awareness training vendor. The Customer has purchased the Provider's per-seat subscription. This SOW covers the Provider's standard rollout services to get the Customer's employees enrolled, the Customer's training campaigns scheduled, the Customer's reporting dashboard configured, and the Customer's first phishing simulation launched.

This is a standard click-through rollout. It is NOT custom consulting. The Provider's standard rollout playbook applies.

1.2 What's in scope

(a) Customer tenant provisioning on the Provider's training platform.

(b) Single sign-on (SSO) configuration with one (1) supported identity provider (Okta, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 / Azure AD — see Schedule A).

(c) Bulk import of the Customer's employee roster from the SSO provider, with department and manager attribution.

(d) Standard training campaign assignment using the Provider's pre-built training paths (Annual Security Awareness, Onboarding, Compliance Refresher).

(e) Configuration of the Customer's reporting dashboard.

(f) Launch of the Customer's first phishing simulation campaign using the Provider's standard phishing template library.

(g) One (1) live "rollout best practices" session (45 minutes, remote).

(h) Self-service access to the Provider's customer success knowledge base.

1.3 What's NOT in scope

(a) Anything beyond the standard rollout playbook.

(b) Custom training content development. The Customer uses the Provider's pre-built training library.

(c) Custom phishing template development. The Customer uses the Provider's pre-built phishing template library.

(d) Integration with any HR information system or LMS beyond the SSO provider's directory sync.

(e) Integration with any SIEM, SOAR, or security orchestration platform beyond the Provider's standard webhook events.

(f) Custom reporting beyond the Provider's standard reporting dashboard.

(g) Compliance attestation, audit support, or third-party risk management questionnaire response.

(h) Any work performed outside the engagement period.

(i) Any seat count above the Customer's per-seat subscription cap.

1.4 Engagement period

Start: {{PROJECT_START_DATE}}. End: {{PROJECT_END_DATE}}. The Engagement Period is approximately 3 weeks.

ARTICLE 2 — DELIVERABLES AND ACCEPTANCE

2.1 Deliverables

(a) Tenant Provisioning Confirmation — confirmation that the Customer's tenant is provisioned on the Provider's platform. Delivered: end of Project Week 1.

(b) SSO Integration Confirmation — confirmation that SSO with the Customer's identity provider is functional and the employee roster is imported. Delivered: end of Project Week 1.

(c) Training Campaign Assignment Confirmation — confirmation that the standard training campaigns are assigned to the imported employees. Delivered: end of Project Week 2.

(d) Reporting Dashboard Configuration Confirmation — confirmation that the standard reporting dashboard is configured. Delivered: end of Project Week 2.

(e) First Phishing Simulation Launch Confirmation — confirmation that the first phishing simulation has been launched. Delivered: end of Project Week 3.

(f) Rollout Best Practices Session — 45-minute remote session conducted with the Customer's security or HR team. Delivered: end of Project Week 3.

2.2 Acceptance

Each Deliverable is automatically accepted upon delivery via the Customer portal unless the Customer raises a written objection within three (3) business days. The objection must specifically identify why the Deliverable does not match what is described in Article 2.1.

2.3 Cure

If the Customer raises a valid objection, the Provider's rollout team will fix the issue within three (3) business days at no additional charge. The fix is bounded by what is described in Article 2.1.

2.4 No refunds for completed Deliverables

Deliverables that have been accepted (or deemed accepted under Section 2.2) are not subject to refund regardless of subsequent events.

ARTICLE 4 — KEY PERSONNEL

4.1 Rollout team

The Provider assigns the next available rollout specialist from its standard rotation. The Customer cannot request specific individuals or require any named specialist to remain on the engagement throughout. The Provider's rollout team is fungible.

4.2 No background checks beyond standard

The Provider's rollout team has completed the Provider's standard background checks. No additional background checks are required for this rollout.

4.3 Removal of personnel

The Customer may request a different specialist via the Customer portal; the Provider will assign a different specialist within five (5) business days.

ARTICLE 5 — CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES

5.1 What the Provider needs from the Customer

(a) Designate one Customer point of contact (typically the Customer's IT or security administrator).

(b) Provide SSO administrator access for the integration setup (see the SSO setup guide in the Customer portal).

(c) Confirm the Customer's training campaign preferences via the rollout wizard.

(d) Confirm the Customer's first phishing simulation parameters (template, target group, send window).

(e) Show up to the rollout best practices session.

(f) Respond to rollout-team messages in the Customer portal within 2 business days.

5.2 Accuracy

The Customer is responsible for the accuracy of the SSO configuration and the employee directory data. Errors caused by inaccurate Customer data or misconfigured SSO are not the Provider's responsibility.

5.3 Customer delays

If the Customer is more than 5 business days late on the rollout wizard or the SSO configuration, the Project End Date moves out by the same number of days. The Provider may charge the Customer at the Provider's standard re-engagement fee if the rollout is delayed more than 15 cumulative business days.

ARTICLE 6 — ASSUMPTIONS AND DEPENDENCIES

6.1 Assumptions

(a) The Customer uses one of the supported identity providers (Schedule A) and is on a current/supported version.

(b) The Customer has purchased a per-seat subscription with seats sufficient for the Customer's employee population. Additional seats above the subscription cap are billable.

(c) The Customer's employee directory in the identity provider is reasonably clean (employees have valid email addresses, valid manager/department attributes).

(d) The Customer's employees are willing and able to receive training emails and phishing simulation emails on their corporate email accounts.

(e) The Customer has the legal right to assign mandatory security awareness training to its employees (this is uncontroversial in most jurisdictions but the Customer should confirm under any applicable works council or labor law obligations).

(f) The Customer's email infrastructure does not block the Provider's training emails or phishing simulation emails (the Provider provides the IP allowlisting guide in the Customer portal).

6.2 Third-party dependencies

The Project depends on (a) the Customer's identity provider being accessible, and (b) the Provider's training platform being operational.

6.3 If an assumption is wrong

The Provider notifies the Customer through the Customer portal.
