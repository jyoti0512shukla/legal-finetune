ARTICLE 7 — CUSTOMER DATA, INFORMATION SECURITY, AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

7.1 Ownership of Customer Data. As between the Parties, Customer shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to Customer Data, including all Client Data referenced in identity records. Provider acknowledges that Customer Data is the exclusive property of Customer.

7.2 Permitted Uses. Provider shall use and disclose Customer Data only as necessary to provide the Services to Customer in accordance with this Agreement and only as permitted by applicable law. Provider shall not (a) use Customer Data for any purpose other than providing the Services; (b) sell, lease, or rent Customer Data to any third party; (c) use Customer Data to train any machine learning model, develop any commercial product, or contribute to any aggregated dataset; (d) mine Customer Data for analytics or insights for Provider's own purposes; or (e) disclose Customer Data to any third party other than as permitted in Section 7.10.

7.3 Regulation S-P Compliance. Provider acknowledges that Customer Data may include "non-public personal information" within the meaning of SEC Regulation S-P (17 C.F.R. § 248.30) and that Provider, in providing the Services, shall be subject to the obligations of a service provider under Regulation S-P. Provider shall implement and maintain administrative, technical, and physical safeguards designed to comply with such obligations.

7.4 SEC Cybersecurity Rule. The Parties acknowledge that Customer is subject to the SEC Cybersecurity Rule, which requires Customer to adopt and implement written cybersecurity policies and procedures, to oversee third-party service providers that have access to Customer's information systems or Customer Data, and to provide notice of significant cybersecurity incidents to the SEC and to clients within the time frames specified by the SEC. Provider shall (a) provide Customer with such information about Provider's cybersecurity program as Customer reasonably requires to comply with the SEC Cybersecurity Rule; (b) cooperate with Customer's annual cybersecurity risk assessment of Provider; (c) promptly notify Customer of any incident that could constitute a "significant cybersecurity incident" within the meaning of the SEC Cybersecurity Rule, with sufficient detail to allow Customer to make any required notification to the SEC and its clients; and (d) not object to any request by Customer to disclose information about a cybersecurity incident affecting the Platform to the SEC, FINRA, or any state securities regulator.

7.5 Regulator Examination. Provider acknowledges that Customer's use of the Platform is subject to examination by the SEC and other Regulators. Provider shall cooperate fully with any examination, audit, or inquiry by a Regulator with respect to Customer's use of the Platform, including by (a) making available to the Regulator (and to Customer upon Customer's request) such books, records, personnel, facilities, and information as the Regulator may reasonably request; (b) responding to Regulator inquiries within the time frames required by the Regulator; (c) providing copies of Provider's SOC 2 Type II reports, ISO 27001 certifications, and other relevant compliance reports as soon as available; and (d) permitting on-site examinations at Provider's facilities by the Regulator upon reasonable notice.

7.6 Customer Audit Rights. In addition to Customer's rights under Section 7.5, Customer shall have the right, upon at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice (or, in the case of a Regulator-driven audit, upon shorter notice as the Regulator requires), to audit Provider's compliance with this Agreement, the Service Level Agreement, the security obligations of this Article 7, and applicable laws. Customer may conduct such audit using internal audit personnel or a third-party auditor of Customer's choosing, subject to such auditor's execution of a reasonable confidentiality agreement.

7.7 Information Security Program. Provider shall maintain a documented information security program designed to comply with the SEC Cybersecurity Rule, Regulation S-P, the AICPA Trust Services Criteria, the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, and applicable industry standards. Provider's information security program shall include (a) administrative safeguards including written policies, employee training, background checks, and a designated chief information security officer; (b) physical safeguards including data center physical access controls; (c) technical safeguards including encryption of Customer Data in transit (TLS 1.2 or higher) and at rest (AES-256), strong cryptographic controls for password and credential storage, role-based access controls, multi-factor authentication for all privileged access, and continuous network monitoring; (d) annual third-party penetration testing of the Platform; (e) maintenance of SOC 2 Type II certification and ISO 27001 certification throughout the Term; (f) annual independent assessment of the information security program; and (g) a documented incident response plan tested annually.

7.8 Security Incident Notification. Provider shall notify Customer's designated security contact in writing of any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to, acquisition of, use of, disclosure of, modification of, or destruction of Customer Data (a "Security Incident") without undue delay and in any event within twenty-four (24) hours of Provider's discovery of such Security Incident. The notification shall include all information then available regarding the nature of the Security Incident, the categories and approximate number of records affected, the technical and operational measures Provider has taken or proposes to take to address the Security Incident, and the contact information for Provider's incident response lead. Provider shall provide Customer with regular updates on the Security Incident at intervals no less frequent than every twelve (12) hours until the incident is resolved.

7.9 Cooperation in Breach Response. Provider shall cooperate fully with Customer in Customer's investigation, response, and notification efforts with respect to any Security Incident, including by (a) providing forensic data and incident response personnel, (b) participating in Customer-led incident response calls, (c) providing such information as Customer reasonably requires to determine whether the incident constitutes a "significant cybersecurity incident" under the SEC Cybersecurity Rule, (d) reimbursing Customer for the reasonable costs of breach notifications, credit monitoring, and forensic investigation to the extent the Security Incident was caused by Provider's breach of this Agreement, and (e) cooperating with any Regulator inquiry arising from the Security Incident.

7.10 Subprocessors. Provider shall not engage any Subprocessor to process Customer Data without Customer's prior written approval, which Customer may withhold or condition in its sole discretion based on the Subprocessor's risk profile, security posture, regulatory status, or geographic location. Provider shall maintain a list of approved Subprocessors at a Customer-accessible Customer Trust Portal. For each new or replacement Subprocessor, Provider shall (a) provide Customer with at least sixty (60) days' prior written notice, (b) provide Customer with the Subprocessor's most recent SOC 2 Type II report, (c) provide Customer with a written explanation of the Subprocessor's role and the type of Customer Data the Subprocessor will process, and (d) not engage the Subprocessor until Customer has provided written approval. Customer may object to any new or replacement Subprocessor on any reasonable ground.

7.11 Permitted Disclosures. Provider may disclose Customer Data only (a) to its employees, contractors, and approved Subprocessors who have a need to know to provide the Services and who are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as those set forth in this Agreement; (b) to a Regulator as permitted under Section 7.5; or (c) as required by court order or applicable law, in which case Provider shall (to the extent legally permitted) give Customer prompt notice to allow Customer to seek a protective order.

7.12 Data Residency. Provider shall store and process all Customer Data within data centers physically located in the contiguous United States. Provider shall not transfer Customer Data outside of the United States, and shall not permit any Subprocessor to process Customer Data from any location outside of the United States, without Customer's prior written consent.

7.13 Data Return and Deletion. Upon expiration or termination of this Agreement, Provider shall, at Customer's election, (a) make Customer Data available for export by Customer in a commercially reasonable, machine-readable format for a period of one hundred eighty (180) days at no additional charge; or (b) permanently delete Customer Data from Provider's production systems within thirty (30) days, with backup deletion to follow within ninety (90) days.
