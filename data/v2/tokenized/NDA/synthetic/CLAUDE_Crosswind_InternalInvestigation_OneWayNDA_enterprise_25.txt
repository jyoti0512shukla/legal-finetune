NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT FOR ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED INTERNAL INVESTIGATION

This NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} with its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} ("Company"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, a {{CUSTOMER_STATE}} {{CUSTOMER_ENTITY_TYPE}} with its principal place of business at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} ("Investigation Vendor"). Company and Investigation Vendor are each referred to herein as a "Party" and collectively as the "Parties."

WHEREAS, Company has retained Pemberton & Wells LLP, its outside legal counsel ("Outside Counsel"), to investigate certain allegations relating to potentially improper payments made by Company personnel in connection with overseas business operations (the "Subject Matter") and to provide Company with legal advice in connection therewith;

WHEREAS, Outside Counsel has engaged Investigation Vendor to provide forensic accounting, document review, and investigative support services in connection with Outside Counsel's representation of Company on the Subject Matter, with the intent that Investigation Vendor's services be performed at the direction of Outside Counsel and that Investigation Vendor's communications, analyses, and deliverables be protected by the attorney-client privilege and the work-product doctrine;

WHEREAS, Company recognizes that the Subject Matter implicates obligations under the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act of 1977, as amended (the "FCPA"), and that Company may, in its sole discretion, elect to make a voluntary self-disclosure to the U.S. Department of Justice or the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, in which case the conduct of the investigation must be calibrated to preserve such options;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing and the mutual covenants set forth herein, the Parties, intending to be legally bound, hereby agree as follows:

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Confidential Information" means all non-public information disclosed by or on behalf of Company or Outside Counsel to Investigation Vendor or any of its Representatives in connection with the Purpose, in whatever form or medium, including without limitation: (a) documents, communications, records, and data produced by Company personnel for the investigation; (b) interviews, witness statements, and notes prepared in connection with the investigation; (c) Investigation Vendor's analyses, work papers, draft and final reports, and other deliverables; (d) the existence and scope of the investigation; (e) the identities of individuals who are or may become subjects, witnesses, or interviewees in the investigation; and (f) any preliminary or final findings or conclusions reached by Investigation Vendor or Outside Counsel.

1.2 "Privileged Materials" means any portion of Confidential Information that is intended to be protected by the attorney-client privilege or the attorney work-product doctrine, including all communications between Investigation Vendor and Outside Counsel, all of Investigation Vendor's analyses, work papers, deliverables, and all interview memoranda prepared by Outside Counsel or Investigation Vendor.

1.3 "Purpose" means Investigation Vendor's provision of forensic accounting, document review, and investigative support services to Outside Counsel for the purpose of assisting Outside Counsel in providing legal advice to Company concerning the Subject Matter, and nothing else.

1.4 "Representatives" means Investigation Vendor's directors, officers, employees, and (to the extent expressly approved in advance in writing by Outside Counsel) independent contractors and outside consultants, in each case who have a bona fide need to know Confidential Information for the Purpose and who are bound by written obligations of confidentiality and non-use no less protective than those set forth herein. For the avoidance of doubt, individual Company employees who are interviewed by Outside Counsel as part of the investigation are not "Representatives" of either Party for purposes of this Agreement.

1.5 "Trade Secret" means any Confidential Information that qualifies as a trade secret under the Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016 or the laws of the State of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}} or any other applicable law.

ARTICLE 2 — PURPOSE AND PRIVILEGE PRESERVATION

2.1 Narrow Purpose. Investigation Vendor shall use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and for no other purpose. Investigation Vendor shall not use Confidential Information for Investigation Vendor's marketing, training, research, business development, or any other purpose.

2.2 Direction by Outside Counsel. Investigation Vendor's engagement is directed by Outside Counsel. All assignments, requests for analysis, and review of deliverables shall be coordinated through Outside Counsel. Investigation Vendor shall direct all written deliverables to Outside Counsel rather than directly to Company personnel, except where Outside Counsel expressly directs otherwise in writing. Investigation Vendor shall not provide any oral or written report directly to Company management or board members except in the presence of, or with the express prior authorization of, Outside Counsel.

2.3 Privilege Markings. Investigation Vendor shall mark all written deliverables, draft and final reports, work papers, and analyses prepared in connection with the Purpose as "Privileged and Confidential — Attorney Work Product — Prepared at the Direction of Outside Counsel" or with comparable markings.

2.4 Upjohn Warnings. Investigation Vendor acknowledges that Outside Counsel will provide individual Company employee interviewees with appropriate Upjohn warnings prior to interviews, advising such interviewees that Outside Counsel represents Company and not the individual employee, and that Outside Counsel may share interview content with Company management. Investigation Vendor's notes of interviews shall reflect Outside Counsel's mental impressions and shall be treated as opinion work product.

2.5 No Reverse Engineering. Investigation Vendor shall not reverse engineer, disassemble, or attempt to derive the composition or structure of any proprietary system, tool, or methodology disclosed by Company in connection with the Purpose.

ARTICLE 3 — CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

3.1 Obligations. Investigation Vendor shall: (a) hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence; (b) use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose as directed by Outside Counsel; (c) disclose Confidential Information only to its Representatives who have a bona fide need to know in connection with the Purpose and who are bound by written obligations of confidentiality and non-use no less protective than those set forth herein; and (d) protect Confidential Information using at least the degree of care Investigation Vendor uses for its own most sensitive confidential information, but no less than a reasonable degree of care.

3.2 No Voluntary Disclosure. Investigation Vendor shall not voluntarily disclose any Privileged Materials, in whole or in part, to any third party — including any government authority, regulator, journalist, other investigation vendor, or any other person — without the prior express written consent of both Outside Counsel and Company.

3.3 Responsibility for Representatives. Investigation Vendor shall be fully responsible for any breach of this Agreement by any of its Representatives.

3.4 Incident Reporting. Investigation Vendor shall notify Outside Counsel and Company in writing immediately, and in any event within twelve (12) hours, after becoming aware of any actual or suspected unauthorized use, disclosure, loss, or exposure of Confidential Information, and shall cooperate fully with Outside Counsel and Company in investigating and remediating any such incident.

ARTICLE 4 — EXCLUSIONS FROM CONFIDENTIALITY

4.1 Exclusions. The obligations in Article 3 shall not apply to any information that Investigation Vendor can demonstrate by competent written evidence: (a) was in the public domain at the time of disclosure or subsequently entered the public domain through no breach of this Agreement; (b) was in Investigation Vendor's possession free of any obligation of confidence prior to disclosure; (c) was independently developed by Investigation Vendor without use of or reference to any Confidential Information; or (d) was rightfully received from a third party without any obligation of confidence to Company or Outside Counsel.

4.2 Privileged Materials Never Excluded. Privileged Materials shall not fall within any of the exclusions in Section 4.1 solely by virtue of any subsequent public disclosure of the underlying Subject Matter or by virtue of any Company voluntary self-disclosure.

ARTICLE 5 — COMPELLED DISCLOSURE AND REGULATORY ELECTIONS

5.1 Legal Process. In the event that Investigation Vendor or any of its Representatives is requested or required by any court, governmental authority, or other legal process to disclose any Confidential Information, Investigation Vendor shall, to the extent legally permissible, immediately notify Outside Counsel and Company so they may seek a protective order or other appropriate remedy and may assert applicable privileges and protections.

5.2 Assertion of Privilege. If Investigation Vendor is legally compelled to disclose any Privileged Materials, Investigation Vendor shall assert the attorney-client privilege and the work-product doctrine, cooperate with Outside Counsel and Company in protecting privilege, and disclose only the minimum portion of Privileged Materials legally required to be disclosed.

5.3 Company FCPA Self-Disclosure Carve-Out. Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement, Company reserves the absolute right, in its sole discretion and at the direction of Outside Counsel, to (a) make a voluntary self-disclosure to the U.S. Department of Justice, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, or any foreign regulator concerning the Subject Matter, including disclosure of any portion of the investigation findings and supporting Confidential Information; and (b) cooperate with any such authority by providing access to interview memoranda, deliverables, or other Privileged Materials. Investigation Vendor shall reasonably cooperate with any such voluntary disclosure or cooperation effort upon Outside Counsel's request.

ARTICLE 6 — RETURN OR DESTRUCTION

6.1 Return on Engagement Conclusion. Upon the conclusion of the engagement or at any time upon Outside Counsel's written request, Investigation Vendor shall, at Outside Counsel's election, return or destroy all Confidential Information, together with all copies, extracts, and derivative materials, and shall certify such return or destruction in writing within thirty (30) business days.

6.2 Litigation Hold. If a litigation hold is in effect with respect to any Confidential Information, Investigation Vendor shall preserve such Confidential Information and shall not return or destroy it until Outside Counsel notifies Investigation Vendor in writing that the litigation hold has been released.

6.3 Retention Exceptions. Investigation Vendor may retain (a) one archival copy of Confidential Information in the custody of its outside legal counsel, solely for the purpose of establishing Investigation Vendor's compliance with this Agreement; and (b) Confidential Information contained in automatic, tamper-resistant system backups. Any retained Confidential Information shall remain subject to this Agreement for so long as it is retained.

ARTICLE 7 — NO LICENSE OR OWNERSHIP TRANSFER

7.1 Ownership Reserved. All Confidential Information shall remain the sole and exclusive property of Company. All Privileged Materials shall be deemed work product of Outside Counsel prepared for Company. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed as granting Investigation Vendor, by implication, estoppel, or otherwise, any license or other right in any patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or other intellectual property right of Company or Outside Counsel. Investigation Vendor's proprietary methodologies, work paper templates, and analytic tools shall remain the exclusive property of Investigation Vendor, subject to the restrictions of this Agreement.

ARTICLE 8 — TERM AND SURVIVAL

8.1 Term. This Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue in full force and effect for a period of {{TERM_YEARS}} years thereafter, unless earlier terminated upon {{NOTICE_DAYS}} days' prior written notice in the event of a material uncured breach.

8.2 Survival. Investigation Vendor's obligations of confidentiality and non-use with respect to Confidential Information shall survive indefinitely. With respect to Privileged Materials, the obligations shall survive indefinitely and without limitation. With respect to any Trade Secret, the obligations shall survive for so long as such information remains a Trade Secret under applicable law.

ARTICLE 9 — EQUITABLE REMEDIES

9.1 Irreparable Harm. Investigation Vendor acknowledges that any breach of this Agreement, particularly any compromise of privilege or any unauthorized disclosure of Confidential Information related to the Subject Matter, may cause Company irreparable harm for which monetary damages would be inadequate.

9.2 Injunctive Relief. Company shall be entitled to seek specific performance, injunctive relief, and other equitable relief in any court of competent jurisdiction, without the necessity of posting a bond or other security and without the necessity of proving actual damages, in addition to any other remedies available at law or in equity.

9.3 Cumulative Remedies. Remedies are cumulative and in addition to any other rights and remedies available at law, in equity, or under any other agreement.

ARTICLE 10 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

10.1 Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, without regard to conflict of laws principles.

10.2 Venue. Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, for any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement.

10.3 Entire Agreement. This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties with respect to the subject matter hereof.

10.4 Amendments. No amendment of this Agreement shall be effective unless in writing and signed by both Parties (and acknowledged by Outside Counsel).

10.5 Assignment. Investigation Vendor may not assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Company. Company may assign this Agreement freely.

10.6 Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force.

10.7 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts.
