BID TEAM NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT

[PRIME CONTRACTOR PAPER — CUI HANDLING]

THIS BID TEAM NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} with its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Prime Contractor"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, a {{CUSTOMER_STATE}} {{CUSTOMER_ENTITY_TYPE}} with its principal place of business at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Teaming Partner"). The Prime Contractor and the Teaming Partner are each referred to herein as a "Party" and collectively as the "Parties."

WHEREAS, the Prime Contractor is preparing a proposal in response to a solicitation by the United States Department of Defense for a classified information technology modernization procurement (the "Procurement");

WHEREAS, the Prime Contractor intends to form a bid team that may include the Teaming Partner, and for such purpose the Prime Contractor and the Teaming Partner may need to exchange Confidential Information that includes Controlled Unclassified Information ("CUI") and Covered Defense Information ("CDI") as those terms are defined in the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement ("DFARS");

WHEREAS, the Prime Contractor's handling of CUI and CDI is subject to NIST Special Publication 800-171, DFARS 252.204-7012, the International Traffic in Arms Regulations ("ITAR"), and the Export Administration Regulations ("EAR"), and the Teaming Partner's access to any such information must be consistent with those regulatory requirements;

WHEREAS, the Procurement Integrity Act (41 U.S.C. § 2102) restricts the handling of contractor bid or proposal information during the conduct of a federal procurement, and both Parties desire to ensure that their activities under this Agreement comply with such restrictions;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing recitals and the mutual covenants set forth herein, and for other good and valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged, the Parties, intending to be legally bound, hereby agree as follows:

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed by one Party (the "Disclosing Party") to the other Party (the "Receiving Party") in connection with the Purpose, in whatever form or medium, including without limitation: (a) the Prime Contractor's proposal strategy, pricing assumptions, staffing plans, and technical approach; (b) the Teaming Partner's proposed scope of work, pricing, and technical capabilities; (c) any CUI, CDI, Export-Controlled Information, or classified information made available by the Prime Contractor; (d) any contractor bid or proposal information (as defined in 41 U.S.C. § 2101(6)) shared in connection with the Procurement; and (e) the existence and content of the Parties' teaming discussions.

1.2 "CUI" means Controlled Unclassified Information as defined in 32 CFR Part 2002.

1.3 "CDI" means Covered Defense Information as defined in DFARS 252.204-7012.

1.4 "Export-Controlled Information" means any technical data or technology subject to ITAR or EAR.

1.5 "Purpose" means the Parties' good-faith evaluation of whether to form a bid team for the Procurement, and (if such team is formed) the Parties' joint preparation and submission of a proposal in response to the Procurement. The Purpose does not include any use of Confidential Information for any other proposal, any other federal procurement, any commercial procurement, or any activity unrelated to the Procurement.

1.6 "Representatives" means each Party's directors, officers, and employees who: (a) have a demonstrated need to know the specific Confidential Information in connection with the Purpose; (b) have been individually identified in writing to the Disclosing Party (or, in the case of broad general-disclosure sessions, approved as a category by the Disclosing Party); (c) in the case of CUI and CDI, are United States persons (as defined in 22 CFR § 120.15); and (d) are bound by written obligations of confidentiality and non-use no less protective than those set forth herein. Subcontractors, consultants, and third parties are not "Representatives" without the Disclosing Party's specific prior written approval.

1.7 "United States person" has the meaning set forth in 22 CFR § 120.15 and, for purposes of this Agreement, includes lawful permanent residents and certain protected individuals, but does not include any person who is not authorized under ITAR or EAR to receive the specific category of Export-Controlled Information at issue.

ARTICLE 2 — PURPOSE

2.1 Narrow Purpose. Each Receiving Party shall use the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information solely for the Purpose. Without limiting the foregoing, Confidential Information may not be used: (a) to prepare or support any other bid, proposal, or response to any other procurement; (b) to obtain a competitive advantage against the Disclosing Party in any other matter; or (c) to inform the Receiving Party's commercial pricing, staffing, or product decisions for any business other than the Procurement.

2.2 Procurement Integrity Act Acknowledgment. Each Party acknowledges that the Procurement Integrity Act restricts the disclosure, receipt, and use of contractor bid or proposal information and source selection information. Each Party represents that it has implemented policies and training to ensure compliance with the Procurement Integrity Act, and each Party agrees that no contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information received from the other Party will be disclosed to any person who does not have a lawful need to know in connection with the Procurement.

2.3 No Reverse Engineering. Neither Party shall reverse engineer, disassemble, or otherwise attempt to derive the composition, structure, or methods of operation of any tangible item of Confidential Information, including any prototype hardware, classified sample, or source code made available for inspection during the Purpose.

ARTICLE 3 — CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION AND CUI HANDLING

3.1 General Obligations. The Receiving Party shall: (a) hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence; (b) use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose; (c) disclose Confidential Information only to its Representatives in accordance with this Agreement; and (d) protect Confidential Information using at least the degree of care the Receiving Party uses for its own most sensitive confidential information, but no less than a reasonable degree of care.

3.2 CUI and CDI Safeguarding. The Receiving Party shall handle CUI and CDI in accordance with NIST SP 800-171 and DFARS 252.204-7012, shall maintain the security controls required by those sources, and shall limit access to CUI and CDI to Representatives who are United States persons.

3.3 Export Control Compliance. No Export-Controlled Information shall be disclosed by the Disclosing Party to the Receiving Party, or re-disclosed by the Receiving Party, except in compliance with ITAR and EAR. The Receiving Party shall not export, re-export, or transfer any Export-Controlled Information to any non-U.S. person without the prior written consent of the Disclosing Party and the required U.S. government authorizations.

3.4 Facility and Personnel Clearance Flow-Down. Where access to any Confidential Information requires a facility security clearance or personnel security clearance, the Receiving Party represents that it holds the required clearance at the required level, and that it has and will maintain personnel security procedures consistent with the National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual ("NISPOM"). The Receiving Party shall flow down the clearance and safeguarding requirements of this Agreement to any approved Representatives.

3.5 Responsibility for Representatives. The Receiving Party shall be fully responsible for any breach of this Agreement by any of its Representatives.

3.6 Incident Reporting. The Receiving Party shall notify the Disclosing Party's security office in writing within seventy-two (72) hours after discovering any actual or suspected cyber incident, unauthorized access, or spillage involving Confidential Information, consistent with the reporting timeline required by DFARS 252.204-7012. Such notification shall include the information required by DFARS 252.204-7012, and the Receiving Party shall preserve and protect relevant media and cooperate fully with any Disclosing Party or DoD incident response activities.

ARTICLE 4 — EXCLUSIONS FROM CONFIDENTIALITY

4.1 Exclusions. The obligations in Article 3 shall not apply to any information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate by competent written evidence: (a) was in the public domain at the time of disclosure or subsequently entered the public domain through no breach of this Agreement; (b) was in the Receiving Party's possession, free of any obligation of confidence, prior to disclosure; (c) was independently developed without use of or reference to Confidential Information; or (d) was rightfully received from a third party without any obligation of confidence to the Disclosing Party.

4.2 CUI and CDI Never Excluded. Notwithstanding Section 4.1, CUI, CDI, Export-Controlled Information, and contractor bid or proposal information are not eligible for the exclusions in Section 4.1 unless the Receiving Party can demonstrate that the specific item of information is no longer controlled or restricted under applicable law.

ARTICLE 5 — COMPELLED DISCLOSURE

5.1 Legal Process. In the event that the Receiving Party is requested or required by any court, governmental authority, or other legal process to disclose Confidential Information, the Receiving Party shall promptly, and to the extent legally permissible, notify the Disclosing Party so that the Disclosing Party may, at its sole cost, seek a protective order or other appropriate remedy.

5.2 Limitation. If a protective order is not obtained and the Disclosing Party does not waive compliance, the Receiving Party may disclose only the portion of Confidential Information legally required to be disclosed and shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain confidential treatment. Non-disclosed portions shall continue to be treated as confidential.

5.3 DoD and Agency Requests. Any request or inquiry from DoD, any contracting officer, any agency inspector general, or any agency attorney that relates to Confidential Information of the Disclosing Party shall be promptly communicated by the Receiving Party to the Disclosing Party before the Receiving Party responds, to the extent legally permissible.

ARTICLE 6 — RETURN OR DESTRUCTION

6.1 Return on Request. Upon the Disclosing Party's written request, or upon the earlier of (a) a determination by either Party not to proceed with the teaming arrangement, (b) the submission of the proposal to the Procurement if the Receiving Party is not included as a bid team member, or (c) the conclusion of the Procurement without award to the bid team, the Receiving Party shall return or, at the Disclosing Party's election, destroy all Confidential Information in its possession, together with all copies, extracts, and derivative materials. The Receiving Party shall execute a certificate of destruction confirming the foregoing within ten (10) business days.

6.2 Retention Exceptions. The Receiving Party may retain (a) one archival copy in the custody of its outside legal counsel for compliance purposes and (b) Confidential Information contained in automatic, tamper-resistant system backups. Retained Confidential Information remains subject to this Agreement.

ARTICLE 7 — NO LICENSE OR OWNERSHIP TRANSFER

7.1 Ownership Reserved. All Confidential Information shall remain the sole and exclusive property of the Disclosing Party. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed as granting the Receiving Party, by implication, estoppel, or otherwise, any license or other right in any patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or other intellectual property right of the Disclosing Party. This Agreement does not create any joint venture, partnership, or agency relationship between the Parties, and does not obligate either Party to include the other as a member of its bid team.

ARTICLE 8 — TERM AND SURVIVAL

8.1 Term. This Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue in full force and effect for a period of {{TERM_YEARS}} years thereafter, unless earlier terminated upon {{NOTICE_DAYS}} days' prior written notice by either Party.

8.2 Survival. Notwithstanding any expiration or termination, the Receiving Party's obligations of confidentiality and non-use shall survive for ten (10) years after the date of expiration or termination, and with respect to any Trade Secret, for so long as the information remains a Trade Secret under applicable law. With respect to CUI, CDI, Export-Controlled Information, and classified information, the Receiving Party's obligations shall survive for so long as such information remains controlled under applicable law.

ARTICLE 9 — EQUITABLE REMEDIES

9.1 Irreparable Harm. Each Party acknowledges that any breach of this Agreement may cause the Disclosing Party irreparable harm for which monetary damages alone would be inadequate.

9.2 Injunctive Relief. The Disclosing Party shall be entitled, in addition to any other remedies available at law or in equity, to seek specific performance, injunctive relief, and other equitable relief in any court of competent jurisdiction to prevent or restrain any breach or threatened breach of this Agreement, without the necessity of posting a bond or other security and without the necessity of proving actual damages.

9.3 Cumulative Remedies. The rights and remedies of the Disclosing Party under this Agreement are cumulative and in addition to, and not in lieu of, any other rights and remedies.

ARTICLE 10 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

10.1 Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the Commonwealth of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, without regard to its conflict of laws principles. To the extent any provision of this Agreement conflicts with any federal statute, regulation, or executive order applicable to federal contracting or the handling of CUI, CDI, classified information, or Export-Controlled Information, such federal law shall govern.

10.2 Venue. Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, for any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement.

10.3 Entire Agreement. This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties with respect to the subject matter hereof and supersedes all prior understandings.

10.4 Amendments. No amendment, modification, or waiver of any provision of this Agreement shall be effective unless in writing and signed by both Parties.

10.5 Assignment. Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party. Any purported assignment in violation of this Section shall be void.

10.6 Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect.

10.7 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts, each of which shall be deemed an original, and all of which together shall constitute one and the same instrument.
