This DPA is part of the parent agreement above. It governs how the Processor handles personal data submitted to the Processor's LLM / generative AI platform on behalf of the Controller. When this DPA conflicts with the parent agreement on personal-data matters, this DPA wins. Otherwise the parent agreement controls.

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

Standard meanings from EU GDPR, UK GDPR, and CCPA / CPRA apply.

"Customer Inputs" means prompts, files, and other data the Controller submits to the LLM API.

"Customer Outputs" means the text, images, or other content the LLM generates in response to Customer Inputs.

"Customer Personal Data" means any Personal Data contained in Customer Inputs or Customer Outputs.

ARTICLE 2 — RELATIONSHIP OF THE PARTIES

2.1 The Controller is the controller. The Processor is the processor.

2.2 The Processor processes Customer Personal Data only on the Controller's documented instructions: the parent agreement, this DPA, and the Controller's API calls.

2.3 If the Processor thinks an instruction would break the law, the Processor will tell the Controller before performing it.

2.4 No model training on Customer Personal Data. Critical: the Processor will not use Customer Inputs, Customer Outputs, or any Customer Personal Data to train, fine-tune, or evaluate any model — whether the Processor's foundation model, the Processor's other models, or any third-party model — without the Controller's express written consent. This is the Processor's default for the API; it is not a feature toggle, it is a contractual commitment.

2.5 No human review of Customer Inputs or Customer Outputs except (a) by automated abuse / safety systems that operate on a no-retention basis and that do not produce data used for any other purpose, or (b) on the Controller's express written consent for a specific human review activity.

3.2 Nature and purpose: {{PROCESSING_PURPOSE}}.

3.3 Categories of Personal Data: {{DATA_CATEGORIES}}.

3.4 Categories of Data Subjects: {{DATA_SUBJECT_CATEGORIES}}.

3.5 The Controller represents that it has a lawful basis for the processing under Applicable Data Protection Law and that it has provided fair-processing notices to its end users about the use of LLM / AI services.

3.6 No special categories without express consent. The Controller agrees not to submit special category data within the meaning of Article 9 GDPR (including health, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, genetic data, biometric data, or data concerning sex life or sexual orientation) to the LLM API without the Controller's express written authorization to do so.

ARTICLE 4 — PROCESSOR OBLIGATIONS

4.1 Processing limitation. The Processor processes Customer Personal Data only as the Controller instructs and only to provide the LLM API services.

4.2 No model training. As stated in Section 2.4, no model training without express Controller consent.

4.3 Personnel access. People at the Processor with access to Customer Personal Data are subject to confidentiality obligations and access is need-to-know only.

4.4 No sale or sharing under CCPA / CPRA.

4.5 Inference logs. The Processor's logs of LLM inference requests are retained only as long as necessary for operational debugging and abuse prevention, and in any event no longer than thirty (30) days, after which they are deleted automatically.

ARTICLE 5 — TECHNICAL AND ORGANIZATIONAL MEASURES

5.1 The Processor implements the TOMs in Annex II. TLS 1.2+ in transit. AES-256 at rest. SSO + MFA for personnel access. Annual SOC 2 Type II. Annual ISO 27001 surveillance. Annual third-party penetration testing.

5.2 LLM-specific safeguards. Prompt and completion data are stored encrypted with per-Controller encryption context. Inference traffic is segregated from training infrastructure. Model weights and Customer Personal Data are stored in separate environments.

5.3 BCDR plan with stated RTO/RPO and annual testing.

ARTICLE 6 — SUB-PROCESSORS

6.1 General authorization. The Controller authorizes sub-processors subject to this Article 6.

6.2 Current list at the Processor's trust center and in Annex III.

6.3 The Processor will give the Controller at least 30 days' written notice before adding or replacing a sub-processor.

6.4 The Controller may object on reasonable data protection grounds within 30 days. If unresolved within another 30 days, the Controller may terminate the affected portion of the parent agreement without penalty.

6.5 Each sub-processor under written agreement with data protection terms at least as protective as this DPA, including the no-model-training commitment in Section 2.4 (any sub-processor that operates a model component cannot use Customer Personal Data for model training without the Controller's express consent).

6.6 Primary liability for sub-processor acts and omissions.

ARTICLE 7 — DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

7.1 Given the architecture of the LLM API (Customer Inputs and Customer Outputs are stored only briefly and are linked to API keys, not directly to Data Subjects), the Processor's ability to respond to per-Data-Subject requests is limited. The Controller is responsible for managing Data Subject requests at the application layer (the Controller's own application that calls the LLM API).

7.2 The Processor will, on the Controller's request, delete inference logs associated with a specific time window and a specific Controller API key, in support of the Controller's response to a Data Subject erasure request.

7.3 The Processor will not respond to Data Subjects directly.

ARTICLE 8 — PERSONAL DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION

8.1 The Processor will notify the Controller within {{BREACH_NOTIFICATION_HOURS}} hours after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Customer Personal Data.

8.2 The notification will include the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects affected, the categories and approximate number of Personal Data records affected, the contact point at the Processor, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.

8.3 The Processor will cooperate with the Controller in investigating, remediating, and notifying any Supervisory Authority and affected Data Subjects.

8.4 The Processor maintains a record of all Personal Data Breaches.

ARTICLE 9 — DATA PROTECTION IMPACT ASSESSMENTS

9.1 The Processor will help the Controller with Data Protection Impact Assessments, including providing documentation about the LLM platform architecture, the no-training commitment, the TOMs, and the sub-processors.

9.2 The Controller is responsible for the DPIA itself, including any DPIA required for the use of generative AI under Applicable Data Protection Law.

ARTICLE 10 — INTERNATIONAL TRANSFERS

10.1 EU SCCs (2021/914) incorporated, Module 2 and Module 3, general sub-processor authorization (Option 2), Irish governing law and venue.

10.2 UK IDTA incorporated for UK transfers.

10.3 The Processor maintains a current Transfer Impact Assessment.

10.4 The Controller may select EU-only or US-only inference regions via the Processor's region selector, in which case the Processor will route inference traffic exclusively through the selected region.

ARTICLE 11 — AUDIT RIGHTS

11.1 The Controller can audit on at least {{AUDIT_NOTICE_DAYS}} days' notice and no more than once per twelve (12) month period.

11.2 The Processor will share its current SOC 2 Type II report at no charge. Where the SOC 2 report adequately covers the audit scope, the Controller agrees to accept it instead of an on-site audit.

11.3 The Processor will also make available its model card, its no-training commitment attestation, and any third-party AI red team assessment summaries the Processor has commissioned.

11.4 Audits won't expose other customers' inference data, the Processor's proprietary model weights, or trade secrets.

ARTICLE 12 — DELETION OR RETURN OF CUSTOMER PERSONAL DATA

12.1 When the services end, the Processor will delete all Customer Personal Data (which, given the LLM platform's architecture, primarily consists of inference logs and any stored Customer Inputs and Customer Outputs).

12.2 Inference logs are automatically deleted within thirty (30) days per Section 4.5; on termination, the Processor will accelerate this deletion to occur within ten (10) days of termination.

12.3 Certification of deletion on request.

12.4 No model retention. The Processor will not retain any model weights or model components that have been trained or fine-tuned on Customer Personal Data, since (per Section 2.4) no such training has occurred without Controller consent.

ARTICLE 13 — TERM AND TERMINATION

This DPA runs for the term of the parent agreement plus the wind-down period. Articles 8, 11, and 12 survive termination.

ARTICLE 14 — INCORPORATION OF THE PARENT AGREEMENT AND ORDER OF PRECEDENCE

This DPA is part of the parent agreement. In any conflict, this DPA wins on personal-data matters, and the no-training commitment in Section 2.4 controls over any contrary provision in the parent agreement. Counterparts allowed.
