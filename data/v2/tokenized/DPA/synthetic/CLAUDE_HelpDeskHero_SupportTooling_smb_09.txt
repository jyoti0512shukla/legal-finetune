ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

We use the standard meanings from the EU GDPR, the UK GDPR, and the CCPA / CPRA. "Personal Data" means anything that identifies a person. "Controller Personal Data" means Personal Data that the Processor processes on the Controller's behalf, including data about the Controller's end-user customers who interact with the Controller's support team via the Processor's platform. "Sub-processor" means anyone the Processor uses to help process Controller Personal Data.

ARTICLE 2 — RELATIONSHIP OF THE PARTIES

2.1 The Controller is the controller. The Processor is the processor. Sub-processors are sub-processors.

2.2 The Processor processes Controller Personal Data only on documented Controller instructions: the parent agreement, this DPA, and the Controller's standard configuration of the support platform.

2.3 If the Processor thinks an instruction would break the law, the Processor will tell the Controller before doing it.

2.4 No use for product analytics or AI training without express Controller consent. Aggregated, de-identified analytics about platform usage (not Controller Personal Data) is permitted.

ARTICLE 3 — SCOPE OF PROCESSING

3.1 Processing details are in Annex I.

3.2 Nature and purpose: {{PROCESSING_PURPOSE}}.

3.3 Categories of Personal Data: {{DATA_CATEGORIES}}.

3.4 Categories of Data Subjects: {{DATA_SUBJECT_CATEGORIES}}.

3.5 The Controller represents that it has a lawful basis for the processing under Applicable Data Protection Law and that it has provided fair-processing notices to its end users.

ARTICLE 4 — PROCESSOR OBLIGATIONS

4.1 Process only on instructions. Personnel access is need-to-know only with confidentiality undertakings. No sale or sharing under CCPA / CPRA. No use for the Processor's marketing.

ARTICLE 5 — TECHNICAL AND ORGANIZATIONAL MEASURES

5.1 The Processor implements the TOMs in Annex II.

5.2 TLS 1.2+ in transit. AES-256 at rest.

5.3 Annual SOC 2 Type II. Annual ISO 27001 surveillance. Quarterly internal vulnerability scans. Annual third-party penetration testing.

5.4 Documented business continuity and disaster recovery plan with stated RTO/RPO and annual testing.

ARTICLE 6 — SUB-PROCESSORS

6.1 General authorization. The Controller authorizes sub-processors subject to this Article 6.

6.2 Current list at the Processor's trust center and in Annex III.

6.3 At least 30 days' written notice for additions or replacements (via in-product notification, email, or trust center update).

6.4 The Controller may object on reasonable data protection grounds within 30 days. If the Parties cannot resolve the objection within 30 days, the Controller may terminate the affected portion of the parent agreement without penalty.

6.5 Each sub-processor under written agreement with data protection terms at least as protective as this DPA. The Processor remains primarily liable for sub-processor acts and omissions.

ARTICLE 7 — DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

7.1 The Processor provides the Controller with self-service tools in the platform to retrieve, search, and delete Personal Data on a per-Data-Subject basis.

7.2 If the Processor receives a Data Subject request directly, it forwards the request to the Controller within 5 business days.

ARTICLE 8 — PERSONAL DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION

8.1 The Processor will notify the Controller within {{BREACH_NOTIFICATION_HOURS}} hours after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Controller Personal Data.

8.2 The notification will include the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects affected, the categories and approximate number of Personal Data records affected, the contact point at the Processor, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.

8.3 The Processor will cooperate with the Controller in investigating, remediating, and notifying any Supervisory Authority and affected Data Subjects.

8.4 The Processor maintains a record of all Personal Data Breaches.

ARTICLE 9 — DATA PROTECTION IMPACT ASSESSMENTS

9.1 The Processor will help the Controller with Data Protection Impact Assessments and prior consultations with Supervisory Authorities.

9.2 The Controller is responsible for the DPIA itself; the Processor's role is supportive.

ARTICLE 10 — INTERNATIONAL TRANSFERS

10.1 EU SCCs (2021/914) incorporated for transfers from the EEA, Module 2 and Module 3, general sub-processor authorization (Option 2), Irish governing law and venue, Annexes referencing this DPA's Annexes I, II, and III.

10.2 UK IDTA incorporated for UK transfers with the same parameters.

10.3 The Processor maintains a current Transfer Impact Assessment.

ARTICLE 11 — AUDIT RIGHTS

11.1 The Controller can audit on at least {{AUDIT_NOTICE_DAYS}} days' notice and no more than once per twelve (12) month period.

11.2 The Processor will share its current SOC 2 Type II report at no charge. Where the SOC 2 report adequately covers the audit scope, the Controller agrees to accept it instead of an on-site audit.

11.3 Audits won't expose other customers' data or the Processor's trade secrets.

ARTICLE 12 — DELETION OR RETURN OF PERSONAL DATA

12.1 When services end, the Processor will delete or return all Controller Personal Data at the Controller's choice and delete all existing copies, unless retention is required by law.

12.2 Certification of deletion on request.

12.3 Backup or archival copies retained per the Processor's retention schedule remain subject to this DPA's confidentiality and security obligations.

12.4 Deletion is completed within 30 days after the end of services.

ARTICLE 13 — TERM AND TERMINATION

This DPA runs for the term of the parent agreement plus the wind-down period. Articles 8, 11, and 12 survive termination.

ARTICLE 14 — INCORPORATION OF THE PARENT AGREEMENT AND ORDER OF PRECEDENCE

This DPA is part of the parent agreement. In any conflict, this DPA wins on personal-data matters; the parent agreement wins on commercial matters, IP, liability, governing law, and dispute resolution. Counterparts allowed.
