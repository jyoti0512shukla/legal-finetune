ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Applicable Privacy Law" means, collectively, (a) the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the NAIC Privacy of Consumer Financial and Health Information Regulation (Model Regulation 672), (b) the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (Model Law 668) and any state adoption (including 23 NYCRR Part 500 for New York-licensed insurers), (c) HIPAA (where the Insurer offers health insurance), (d) the CCPA / CPRA, (e) state insurance laws and regulations, and (f) any other privacy law applicable to the Processing under the Agreement.

1.2 "Insurer Personal Data" means any Personal Data of the Insurer's policyholders, applicants, claimants, beneficiaries, and other Data Subjects Processed by the Service Provider on behalf of the Insurer.

1.3 "Nonpublic Personal Information" or "NPI" has the meaning in the NAIC Privacy Regulation and includes all financial and health information about Data Subjects.

ARTICLE 2 — RELATIONSHIP OF THE PARTIES

2.1 Roles. The Insurer is the Controller. The Service Provider is the Processor.

2.2 Documented Instructions. The Service Provider shall Process Insurer Personal Data only on documented instructions from the Insurer.

2.3 No Secondary Use. The Service Provider shall not (a) sell, share, or disclose Insurer Personal Data to any third party, (b) use Insurer Personal Data for the Service Provider's own commercial purposes, (c) use Insurer Personal Data to train AI / ML models without the Insurer's express prior written consent, or (d) combine Insurer Personal Data with personal data from any other source.

2.4 Cooperation with State Insurance Regulators. The Service Provider shall cooperate with state insurance regulators (including state insurance departments) in connection with any examination or investigation relating to the Insurer's compliance with insurance laws.

ARTICLE 3 — SCOPE OF PROCESSING

3.1 Subject Matter and Duration. The subject matter is the provision of the services described in the Agreement.

3.2 Nature and Purpose. {{PROCESSING_PURPOSE}}.

3.3 Categories of Personal Data. {{DATA_CATEGORIES}}.

3.4 Categories of Data Subjects. {{DATA_SUBJECT_CATEGORIES}}.

ARTICLE 4 — SERVICE PROVIDER OBLIGATIONS

4.1 Compliance with NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law. The Service Provider shall maintain a written information security program that meets the requirements of the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (Model Law 668) as adopted by the Insurer's domiciliary state, including (a) designating a qualified individual responsible for the information security program, (b) conducting periodic risk assessments, (c) implementing risk management controls, (d) overseeing third-party service providers, and (e) maintaining an incident response plan.

4.2 Personnel. Personnel with access to Insurer Personal Data have completed background checks, confidentiality undertakings, and annual training.

4.3 No Sale or Secondary Use. As set forth in Section 2.3.

4.4 Cooperation with HIPAA Where Applicable. If the Insurer offers health insurance products, the Service Provider shall comply with HIPAA as a Business Associate to the extent applicable, and the Parties shall execute a separate Business Associate Agreement on the Insurer's standard form.

ARTICLE 5 — TECHNICAL AND ORGANIZATIONAL MEASURES

5.1 Information Security Program. The Service Provider's information security program meets the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law and is documented in Annex II.

5.2 Encryption. TLS 1.2+ in transit. AES-256 at rest.

5.3 Annual SOC 2 Type II audit. Annual ISO 27001 certification. Annual third-party penetration testing. Quarterly internal vulnerability scans.

5.4 Documented BCDR plan with stated RTO/RPO and annual testing.

ARTICLE 6 — SUB-PROCESSORS

6.1 Specific Authorization Required.

6.2 Notice and Consent. At least sixty (60) days' written notice for any new or replacement Sub-processor.

6.3 Sub-processor Obligations equivalent to those imposed on the Service Provider.

6.4 Primary Liability for Sub-processor Acts and Omissions.

ARTICLE 7 — DATA SUBJECT / CONSUMER RIGHTS

7.1 The Service Provider shall provide reasonable assistance to the Insurer in fulfilling the Insurer's GLBA privacy notice obligations and the Insurer's CCPA / CPRA consumer rights obligations.

7.2 If the Service Provider receives a Data Subject request directly, the Service Provider shall forward it to the Insurer within five (5) business days.

ARTICLE 8 — INCIDENT NOTIFICATION

8.1 The Service Provider shall notify the Insurer within {{BREACH_NOTIFICATION_HOURS}} hours after discovering any unauthorized access to, use of, or disclosure of Insurer Personal Data.

8.2 The notification shall include the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects affected, the categories and approximate number of Personal Data records affected, the contact point at the Service Provider, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.

8.3 NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law Notification. The Service Provider shall cooperate with the Insurer in fulfilling the Insurer's notification obligations under the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (which require notification to the state insurance commissioner within 72 hours of determining a cybersecurity event has occurred).

8.4 Records of all incidents.

ARTICLE 9 — DATA PROTECTION IMPACT ASSESSMENTS / RISK ASSESSMENTS

9.1 The Service Provider shall provide reasonable assistance to the Insurer in conducting risk assessments under the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law and any other applicable state insurance regulation.

9.2 The Insurer is responsible for the risk assessment itself.

ARTICLE 10 — DATA LOCATION

10.1 US Data Residency. Insurer Personal Data shall be Processed and stored exclusively in the United States. The Service Provider shall not transfer Insurer Personal Data outside of the United States without the Insurer's prior written consent.

10.2 Data Residency Designation. Insurer Personal Data is hosted in: {{DATA_RESIDENCY_REGION}}.

ARTICLE 11 — AUDIT RIGHTS AND REGULATORY ACCESS

11.1 Insurer Audit Right. On at least {{AUDIT_NOTICE_DAYS}} days' notice and no more than once per twelve (12) month period (except in connection with an incident or a regulatory event).

11.2 State Insurance Regulator Access. State insurance regulators shall have audit rights as required by state insurance law and the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law. The Service Provider shall cooperate fully at no additional charge.

11.3 Third-Party Audit Reports. Annual SOC 2 Type II report, ISO 27001 certificate, and any state-mandated security assessments available at no charge.

11.4 Scope Limitations. Audits shall not require disclosure of other customers' data or trade secrets, but the Service Provider shall not assert such limitations to obstruct any state insurance regulator examination.

ARTICLE 12 — DELETION OR RETURN OF INSURER PERSONAL DATA

12.1 End of Provision of Services. At the Insurer's choice, delete or return all Insurer Personal Data, subject to retention requirements under state insurance records retention law.

12.2 Records Retention. The Service Provider shall retain Insurer Personal Data for the period required by the Insurer's records retention schedule and applicable state insurance law.

12.3 Certification of deletion on request.

12.4 Wind-Down Period. Deletion or return is completed within thirty (30) days following the end of services or the expiration of the records retention period.

ARTICLE 13 — TERM AND TERMINATION

13.1 Term. This DPA runs for the term of the Agreement plus the records retention period.

13.2 Survival. Articles 8, 11, and 12 survive termination.

ARTICLE 14 — INCORPORATION OF THE PARENT AGREEMENT AND ORDER OF PRECEDENCE

14.1 Incorporation. This DPA is part of the Agreement.

14.2 Order of Precedence. This DPA controls on personal-data matters; the Agreement controls on all other matters not modified by this DPA.

14.3 Counterparts allowed.
