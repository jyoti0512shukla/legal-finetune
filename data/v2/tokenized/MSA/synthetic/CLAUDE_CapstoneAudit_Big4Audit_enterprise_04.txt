FINANCIAL STATEMENT AUDIT MASTER ENGAGEMENT AGREEMENT

This Financial Statement Audit Master Engagement Agreement (this "Agreement") is made and entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date"), by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Auditor"), having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}}, and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, a {{CUSTOMER_STATE}} {{CUSTOMER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Company"), having its principal place of business at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}}. The Auditor and the Company may each be referred to as a "Party" and collectively as the "Parties."

The Parties acknowledge that the Auditor will audit the consolidated financial statements of the Company and its subsidiaries in accordance with the standards of the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board ("PCAOB"). The Parties further acknowledge that this Agreement is subject to the auditor independence rules of the United States Securities and Exchange Commission and the PCAOB, and that nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to impair the Auditor's independence with respect to the Company. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any applicable rule, standard, or regulation governing the Auditor's independence or professional responsibilities, the rule, standard, or regulation shall control.

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Affiliate" means any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with another entity.

1.2 "Audit Committee" means the audit committee of the Board of Directors of the Company.

1.3 "Background IP" means any Intellectual Property Rights owned or licensable by a Party prior to the Effective Date or independently developed outside of any Engagement Letter.

1.4 "Company Materials" means any books, records, financial information, contracts, schedules, working papers, electronic data, and other materials provided by the Company to the Auditor in connection with the performance of the Services.

1.5 "Engagement Letter" means a written letter executed by both Parties in respect of a specific audit period or specific services, which incorporates this Agreement by reference and which sets forth the scope, fees, and timing for the relevant audit or services.

1.6 "Intellectual Property Rights" means all intellectual property and proprietary rights of any kind anywhere in the world.

1.7 "PCAOB" means the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board.

1.8 "Permitted Non-Audit Services" means non-audit services that the Audit Committee has pre-approved and that are permitted under the auditor independence rules of the SEC and the PCAOB.

1.9 "Prohibited Services" means services that are prohibited by the auditor independence rules of the SEC and the PCAOB, including without limitation bookkeeping; financial information systems design and implementation; appraisal or valuation services, fairness opinions, or contribution-in-kind reports; actuarial services; internal audit outsourcing services; management functions or human resources; broker-dealer, investment adviser, or investment banking services; legal services; expert services unrelated to the audit; and any other service that the PCAOB or the SEC determines to be impermissible.

1.10 "Public Company Audit" means the audit of the Company's annual consolidated financial statements and internal control over financial reporting in accordance with PCAOB standards.

1.11 "Services" means the audit and assurance services to be performed by the Auditor for the Company under one or more Engagement Letters.

1.12 "Working Papers" means the audit working papers (including testing documentation, analytical procedures, conclusions, and supporting evidence) prepared by the Auditor in the course of performing the Services. Under PCAOB and AICPA standards, the Working Papers are the property of the Auditor.

ARTICLE 2 — SCOPE OF SERVICES

2.1 Engagement Letters. The Auditor shall provide audit and assurance Services to the Company only pursuant to an Engagement Letter executed by both Parties. Each Engagement Letter shall describe in reasonable detail (a) the scope of the audit or assurance work; (b) the financial statements or subject matter to be audited; (c) the audit period; (d) the responsible audit partner; (e) the Fees; (f) the timing for the engagement; and (g) any engagement-specific terms.

2.2 Order of Precedence. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and an Engagement Letter, this Agreement shall control as to the matters expressly addressed in this Agreement, except that the Engagement Letter may expressly modify any provision solely as it applies to that specific engagement.

2.3 PCAOB Standards. The Public Company Audit shall be performed in accordance with the standards of the PCAOB. The Auditor's report on the Company's annual consolidated financial statements and internal control over financial reporting shall be in the form prescribed by PCAOB standards.

2.4 No Assurance Beyond Standards. The Auditor's audit is designed to obtain reasonable, but not absolute, assurance that the financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether caused by error or fraud. There are inherent limitations in any audit, including the possibility that material misstatements may exist that are not detected by the audit, including misstatements arising from collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, or management override of controls.

2.5 Auditor Independence. Throughout the Term, the Auditor shall maintain its independence from the Company in accordance with the auditor independence rules of the SEC and the PCAOB. The Auditor shall not perform any Prohibited Services for the Company or any Affiliate of the Company. The Auditor shall not perform any non-audit services for the Company without the prior written approval of the Audit Committee, which approval shall be sought in accordance with the Audit Committee's pre-approval policies and the auditor independence rules.

2.6 Communications with the Audit Committee. The Auditor shall communicate directly with the Audit Committee on matters required to be communicated by PCAOB standards, including significant accounting policies and practices, critical audit matters, significant deficiencies and material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, instances of fraud or illegal acts, disagreements with management, and other matters required to be communicated to the Audit Committee.

ARTICLE 3 — DELIVERABLES AND ACCEPTANCE

3.1 Audit Deliverables. The Auditor shall deliver to the Company and the Audit Committee (a) the Auditor's report on the Company's annual consolidated financial statements; (b) the Auditor's report on the Company's internal control over financial reporting; (c) the management letter, if any; (d) any written communications required by PCAOB standards; and (e) any other deliverables specified in the Engagement Letter.

3.2 No Acceptance Mechanic for Audit Reports. The Parties acknowledge that the Auditor's reports reflect the Auditor's professional judgment and are not subject to acceptance by the Company. The Company may not require the Auditor to issue any report that, in the Auditor's professional judgment, is not warranted by the audit work performed and the audit evidence obtained.

ARTICLE 4 — FEES AND PAYMENT

4.1 Fees. The Company shall pay the Auditor the Fees set forth in each Engagement Letter. The Engagement Letter shall specify whether Fees are billed on a fixed-fee, time-and-materials, or other basis.

4.2 Annual Aggregate Reference. The aggregate annual Fees payable by the Company across all then-active Engagement Letters as of the Effective Date is approximately {{ANNUAL_FEE_AMOUNT}}, included for reference only.

4.3 Audit Committee Pre-Approval. Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, no Fees shall be paid to the Auditor for any service that has not been pre-approved by the Audit Committee in accordance with the Audit Committee's pre-approval policies.

4.4 Out-of-Scope Work. Any work performed at the Company's request that is outside the scope of an Engagement Letter shall require an amendment to the Engagement Letter and prior approval by the Audit Committee.

4.5 Expenses. Reasonable travel and out-of-pocket expenses incurred by the Auditor in connection with the Services shall be reimbursed by the Company at cost without markup, accompanied by reasonable documentation, and subject to the Company's then-current expense policies.

4.6 Payment Terms. All undisputed invoices shall be due and payable within forty-five (45) days of the date of invoice.

4.7 Disputed Invoices. The Company may withhold payment of any disputed amount until the dispute is resolved. The Auditor shall not impose any late fee, interest charge, or work stoppage with respect to any amount in good-faith dispute. The Auditor shall not, however, suspend or delay the issuance of any audit report on account of a fee dispute, given the regulatory and reporting obligations of the Company.

4.8 Taxes. Fees are exclusive of all taxes other than taxes imposed on the Auditor's net income.

ARTICLE 5 — TERM AND TERMINATION

5.1 Initial Term. This Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue for a period of {{TERM_YEARS}} years, unless earlier terminated in accordance with Article 13. The Parties acknowledge that the engagement of the Auditor for any specific audit period is subject to the annual ratification of the Audit Committee and the shareholders.

5.2 Renewal. This Agreement shall automatically renew for successive one (1) year renewal terms unless either Party provides written notice of non-renewal at least {{NOTICE_DAYS}} days prior to the expiration of the then-current term. The Parties acknowledge that any renewal of an Engagement Letter for a Public Company Audit is subject to the Audit Committee's annual review and the shareholders' annual ratification of the Auditor's appointment.

ARTICLE 6 — KEY PERSONNEL AND PARTNER ROTATION

6.1 Engagement Partner. Each Engagement Letter shall identify the lead audit engagement partner who is responsible for the engagement. The Parties acknowledge that under PCAOB Rule 3526 and Rule 3527, the lead audit partner is subject to a five-year rotation requirement, with a five-year time-out period before the partner may again serve as lead audit partner for the Company.

6.2 Concurring Partner. Each Engagement Letter for a Public Company Audit shall also identify the concurring audit engagement partner, who shall conduct the engagement quality review required by PCAOB Auditing Standard 1220. The concurring partner is also subject to the five-year rotation requirement.

6.3 Replacement of Engagement Partner. The Auditor shall not replace the lead audit partner or the concurring partner identified in an Engagement Letter without notice to the Audit Committee. In any case where rotation is required by PCAOB rules, the Auditor shall propose the new lead engagement partner to the Audit Committee with sufficient lead time to allow for the Audit Committee's review and approval.

6.4 Engagement Team. The Auditor shall staff each Engagement with appropriately qualified personnel (partners, managers, senior associates, and staff associates). The Auditor shall ensure that all personnel performing the Services have undergone the training and supervision required by PCAOB standards.

6.5 Background Checks. The Auditor shall ensure that all personnel performing the Services have undergone background investigations and meet the Auditor's hiring standards.

6.6 Non-Solicitation of Personnel. The Parties acknowledge that the auditor independence rules of the SEC and the PCAOB impose certain "cooling-off" restrictions on the Company's hiring of former Auditor personnel into financial reporting oversight roles. The Company shall comply with such restrictions and shall consult with the Auditor before extending an offer of employment to any current or recent Auditor personnel.

ARTICLE 7 — COMPANY MATERIALS, AUDIT EVIDENCE, AND CONFIDENTIALITY

7.1 Company Cooperation. The Company shall provide the Auditor with access to such Company Materials, personnel, facilities, and information as the Auditor reasonably requires to perform the Services in accordance with PCAOB standards. The Company's failure to provide reasonable cooperation may result in delays, additional Fees, scope limitations, or, in extreme cases, the Auditor's inability to issue a clean audit opinion.

7.2 Management Representations. The Company shall provide the Auditor with management representation letters as required by PCAOB standards in connection with each Public Company Audit.

7.3 Working Papers. The Working Papers prepared by the Auditor in the course of performing the Services are the property of the Auditor and shall be retained by the Auditor in accordance with PCAOB Rule 3100 and the Auditor's records retention policies. The Auditor shall make the Working Papers available to the PCAOB, the SEC, the Internal Revenue Service, and other governmental authorities as required by law.

7.4 Access to Working Papers by the Company. The Auditor shall make the Working Papers available to the Company and its other auditors (such as a successor auditor or a Federal Reserve System examiner) only as required or permitted by law and the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, and only on terms reasonably acceptable to the Auditor.

7.5 Confidentiality of Company Information. The Auditor shall hold all Company Materials and other non-public information about the Company in strict confidence and shall use such information solely for the purpose of performing the Services. The Auditor shall not disclose such information to any third party except (a) to its own personnel who have a need to know in connection with the audit, (b) to the PCAOB, the SEC, or other regulatory authorities as required by law or PCAOB standards, (c) to a successor auditor as required or permitted by the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, or (d) as the Company may otherwise authorize in writing.

7.6 Data Security. The Auditor shall implement and maintain reasonable administrative, physical, and technical safeguards designed to protect Company Materials against unauthorized access, including encryption in transit and at rest, role-based access controls, and a documented information security program reviewed annually.

7.7 Breach Notification. The Auditor shall notify the Company in writing of any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to or disclosure of Company Materials without undue delay and in any event within seventy-two (72) hours of discovery.

ARTICLE 8 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS

8.1 Background IP. As between the Parties, each Party shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to its respective Background IP. The Auditor's Background IP includes the Auditor's audit methodology, software tools, training materials, and proprietary databases.

8.2 Audit Reports. The Auditor's reports prepared in connection with a Public Company Audit are owned by the Auditor, but the Company shall have the right to use, reproduce, and distribute such reports as part of the Company's required public filings (including filings with the SEC) and in connection with the Company's communications with shareholders, lenders, regulators, and other stakeholders. The Auditor shall consent in writing to the inclusion of its report in the Company's filings, in accordance with the requirements of the SEC and the PCAOB.

8.3 Working Papers. As provided in Section 7.3, the Working Papers are the property of the Auditor.

8.4 No Implied Licenses. Except for the limited rights expressly granted in this Article 8, neither Party grants any rights or licenses to the other Party.

ARTICLE 9 — CONFIDENTIALITY

9.1 Auditor's Confidentiality Duty. The Auditor acknowledges that it owes the Company a duty of confidentiality under the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct. The Auditor shall comply with both the contractual obligations in this Article 9 and its obligations under the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, and in the event of any conflict, the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct shall control.

9.2 Confidentiality Obligations. The Auditor shall (a) hold the Company's Confidential Information in strict confidence; (b) use such Confidential Information solely for the purposes of performing the Services; (c) not disclose such Confidential Information to any third party except as permitted by Section 7.5; and (d) protect such Confidential Information using the same degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of similar importance.

9.3 Compelled Disclosure. The Auditor may disclose Confidential Information to the extent required by court order or applicable law (including subpoenas issued by the SEC, the PCAOB, or other regulators), provided that the Auditor gives the Company prompt written notice (where legally permitted).

9.4 Survival. The Auditor's confidentiality obligations shall survive any expiration or termination of this Agreement indefinitely or for as long as required by the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct.

ARTICLE 10 — REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES

10.1 Mutual Warranties. Each Party represents and warrants that (a) it is duly organized and in good standing; (b) it has the corporate authority to enter into this Agreement; and (c) this Agreement constitutes a binding obligation enforceable against it.

10.2 Auditor Warranties. The Auditor represents, warrants, and covenants that (a) the Auditor is duly registered with the PCAOB and is in good standing; (b) the Services shall be performed in accordance with PCAOB standards; (c) the Auditor shall maintain its independence from the Company in accordance with the auditor independence rules of the SEC and the PCAOB throughout the Term; (d) the personnel performing the Services are duly qualified and meet the experience requirements of PCAOB standards; (e) the Auditor is not currently the subject of any PCAOB disciplinary action that materially affects its ability to perform under this Agreement; and (f) the Auditor shall maintain professional liability insurance coverage of not less than One Hundred Million United States Dollars ($100,000,000) per claim throughout the Term.

10.3 Company Warranties. The Company represents and warrants that (a) it has the authority to engage the Auditor; (b) the Audit Committee has pre-approved the Services in accordance with the auditor independence rules; (c) Company Materials provided to the Auditor are accurate and complete in all material respects; and (d) the Company shall not request the Auditor to perform any Prohibited Services or to issue any audit opinion that is not warranted by the audit work performed.

10.4 Disclaimer. EXCEPT FOR THE EXPRESS WARRANTIES SET FORTH IN THIS ARTICLE 10, THE SERVICES ARE PROVIDED ON THE BASIS OF THE AUDITOR'S PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT. THE AUDITOR DOES NOT WARRANT THAT THE AUDIT WILL DETECT ALL MATERIAL MISSTATEMENTS, ALL INSTANCES OF FRAUD, OR ALL SIGNIFICANT DEFICIENCIES IN INTERNAL CONTROL.

ARTICLE 11 — INDEMNIFICATION

11.1 Auditor Indemnity. The Auditor shall defend the Company against any third-party claim alleging that the Auditor's performance of the Services caused harm to such third party as a result of the Auditor's negligence, willful misconduct, or breach of this Agreement, and the Auditor shall indemnify the Company for any damages, settlement amounts, and reasonable attorneys' fees finally awarded against the Company with respect to such claim. The Parties acknowledge that the auditor independence rules limit the scope of indemnification arrangements between an audit client and its auditor, and this Section 11.1 shall be interpreted and applied consistently with such rules.

11.2 Company Indemnity Limited by Auditor Independence Rules. The Parties acknowledge that the SEC's auditor independence rules and PCAOB Rule 3526 prohibit certain indemnification arrangements between an audit client and its auditor, including any provision that would indemnify the Auditor against liability arising from the Auditor's own negligence, willful misconduct, or fraud. Accordingly, the Company shall not indemnify the Auditor against any such liability, and any provision of this Agreement that would purport to do so is hereby disclaimed and shall be unenforceable.

11.3 Indemnification Procedure. The Party seeking indemnification shall (a) provide prompt written notice; (b) grant the indemnifying Party sole control over the defense and settlement (subject to a no-bad-settlement-without-consent right); and (c) provide reasonable cooperation at the indemnifying Party's expense.

ARTICLE 12 — LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

12.1 Aggregate Cap. EXCEPT AS PROVIDED IN SECTION 12.3, IN NO EVENT SHALL THE AGGREGATE LIABILITY OF THE AUDITOR ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT OR ANY ENGAGEMENT LETTER, WHETHER IN CONTRACT, TORT (INCLUDING NEGLIGENCE), OR ANY OTHER LEGAL OR EQUITABLE THEORY, EXCEED THE TOTAL FEES PAID OR PAYABLE BY THE COMPANY TO THE AUDITOR UNDER THE AFFECTED ENGAGEMENT LETTER DURING THE {{LIABILITY_CAP_MONTHS}} MONTHS IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING THE EVENT GIVING RISE TO LIABILITY. THE PARTIES ACKNOWLEDGE THAT THE SEC'S AUDITOR INDEPENDENCE RULES MAY LIMIT THE ENFORCEABILITY OF LIABILITY CAPS BETWEEN AN AUDIT CLIENT AND ITS AUDITOR, AND THIS SECTION 12.1 SHALL BE INTERPRETED AND APPLIED ACCORDINGLY.

12.2 Exclusion of Indirect Damages. EXCEPT AS PROVIDED IN SECTION 12.3, IN NO EVENT SHALL EITHER PARTY BE LIABLE TO THE OTHER PARTY FOR ANY INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, SPECIAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, EXEMPLARY, OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES, ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT.

12.3 Exceptions to Limitations. The limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 shall not apply to (a) the Auditor's obligations under Article 11 (subject to the auditor independence limitations in Section 11.2); (b) any breach of Article 9 (Confidentiality); (c) any liability arising from a Party's gross negligence, willful misconduct, or fraud; (d) any liability that, under the SEC's auditor independence rules or PCAOB rules, cannot be limited or excluded; or (e) any liability that, under applicable law, cannot be limited or excluded.

12.4 Basis of the Bargain. The limitations of liability are an essential element of the bargain between the Parties, subject to the auditor independence rules.

ARTICLE 13 — TERMINATION

13.1 Company Termination. The Company may terminate this Agreement, or any Engagement Letter, at any time upon written notice to the Auditor, subject to the requirement that the dismissal of an auditor be reported by the Company on Form 8-K and any other applicable SEC reporting requirements.

13.2 Auditor Resignation. The Auditor may resign from any Engagement upon written notice to the Audit Committee. The Auditor's resignation shall be reported on Form 8-K as required by SEC rules.

13.3 Termination for Material Breach. Either Party may terminate this Agreement for material breach upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice if the other Party fails to cure the breach within the notice period.

13.4 Termination for Loss of Auditor Independence. The Company shall terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the Auditor loses its independence from the Company in a manner that the SEC's auditor independence rules do not permit to be cured. The Company shall also terminate this Agreement immediately if the Auditor becomes the subject of a final, non-appealable PCAOB disciplinary action that materially affects the Auditor's ability to perform under this Agreement.

13.5 Termination for Insolvency. Either Party may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the other Party becomes insolvent, makes a general assignment for the benefit of creditors, or files a voluntary petition in bankruptcy.

13.6 Effects of Termination. Upon expiration or termination: (a) the Auditor shall promptly cease performance of the Services under the affected Engagement Letter; (b) the Auditor shall make Working Papers available to a successor auditor as required or permitted by the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct; (c) the Company shall pay the Auditor for all Fees that have accrued through the effective date of termination; and (d) the provisions that by their nature are intended to survive (including Articles 1, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, and 14) shall so survive.

13.7 Form 8-K Reporting. The Parties shall cooperate to ensure that any reportable event arising from the termination of this Agreement (including any disagreement on accounting matters) is reported on Form 8-K accurately and in accordance with SEC rules.

ARTICLE 14 — GOVERNING LAW AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION

14.1 Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, without regard to its conflict-of-laws principles.

14.2 Venue and Jurisdiction. Any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

14.3 Informal Dispute Resolution. Before initiating any litigation, the Parties shall attempt in good faith to resolve any dispute through direct negotiation between executives of each Party with authority to resolve the dispute, and the Audit Committee shall be involved in any such resolution.

14.4 Waiver of Jury Trial. EACH PARTY HEREBY WAIVES ANY RIGHT IT MAY HAVE TO A TRIAL BY JURY IN ANY LEGAL PROCEEDING ARISING OUT OF OR RELATING TO THIS AGREEMENT, TO THE EXTENT PERMITTED BY APPLICABLE LAW.

ARTICLE 15 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

15.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement, together with all Engagement Letters, constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties with respect to the subject matter hereof.

15.2 Amendments. No amendment shall be effective unless in writing executed by both Parties.

15.3 Severability. If any provision is held to be unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force.

15.4 Waiver. No failure or delay by either Party in exercising any right shall operate as a waiver thereof.

15.5 Notices. Formal notices shall be in writing and delivered to the addresses set forth in the applicable Engagement Letter.

15.6 Independent Contractors. The Auditor is an independent contractor of the Company and is not an employee, agent, or partner of the Company.

15.7 Subcontractors. The Auditor shall not engage subcontractors or non-network member firms to perform any portion of the Services without the prior written consent of the Audit Committee.

15.8 Network Firms. The Parties acknowledge that the Auditor may, in accordance with PCAOB standards, use the work of other member firms within its global network in connection with the audit of the Company's foreign operations. Such network firms shall be subject to the Auditor's quality control system and shall be supervised by the Auditor in accordance with PCAOB standards.

15.9 Assignment. Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party, which the Company's consent shall require Audit Committee approval.

15.10 Force Majeure. Neither Party shall be liable for delays caused by events outside its reasonable control (other than payment obligations and the Auditor's regulatory reporting obligations).

15.11 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts.

15.12 Headings. Headings are for convenience of reference only.
