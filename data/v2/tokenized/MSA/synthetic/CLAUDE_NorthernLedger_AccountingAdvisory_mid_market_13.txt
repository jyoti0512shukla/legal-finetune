MASTER ACCOUNTING, TAX, AND ADVISORY SERVICES AGREEMENT

This Master Accounting, Tax, and Advisory Services Agreement (this "Agreement") is made and entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date"), by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Firm"), having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}}, and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, a {{CUSTOMER_STATE}} {{CUSTOMER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Client"), having its principal place of business at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}}.

This Agreement is a master agreement only. The specific accounting, tax, or advisory services to be performed (and which specific Firm professionals are engaged for which services) shall be set forth in one or more Engagement Letters executed under this Agreement. Each Engagement Letter shall be matched to a specific service line — tax compliance, tax advisory, audit, review, compilation, financial statement attestation, or business advisory — and shall specify the scope, fees, schedule, and any service-line-specific terms. The Firm acknowledges that it is subject to IRS Circular 230, the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, the rules of the {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}} State Board of Accountancy, and applicable state and federal law governing the practice of public accounting.

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Affiliate" means any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with another entity.

1.2 "AICPA Code" means the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct.

1.3 "Background IP" means any Intellectual Property Rights owned or licensable by a Party prior to the Effective Date or independently developed outside of any Engagement Letter, including the Firm's tax research databases, audit methodologies, advisory frameworks, and proprietary tools.

1.4 "Circular 230" means the Treasury Department regulations governing practice before the Internal Revenue Service, 31 C.F.R. Subtitle A, Part 10.

1.5 "Client Records" means the original books, records, financial statements, supporting documents, and other materials provided by the Client to the Firm in connection with the Services.

1.6 "Engagement Letter" means a written letter executed by both Parties in respect of a particular service line, which incorporates this Agreement by reference and which sets forth the scope of the engagement, the responsible Firm partner, the Fees, the schedule, and any service-line-specific terms.

1.7 "Services" means the accounting, tax, audit, attestation, and advisory services to be performed by the Firm for the Client under one or more Engagement Letters.

1.8 "Working Papers" means the workpapers, audit programs, analytical procedures, and other documentation prepared by the Firm in the course of performing the Services. Under AICPA standards, the Working Papers are the property of the Firm.

ARTICLE 2 — SCOPE OF SERVICES

2.1 Engagement Letters. The Firm shall provide Services to the Client only pursuant to an Engagement Letter executed by both Parties for each service line.

2.2 Service Lines. The Firm may provide the following types of services under this Agreement, each of which shall require its own Engagement Letter and shall be subject to the applicable professional standards: (a) tax compliance services (tax return preparation), (b) tax advisory services (tax planning, tax controversy, transfer pricing), (c) financial statement audit services, (d) financial statement review or compilation services, (e) business advisory services (operations consulting, valuation, transaction support), and (f) any other services agreed in an Engagement Letter.

2.3 Order of Precedence. As described in the preamble. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any applicable rule of professional conduct (including Circular 230, the AICPA Code, or state board of accountancy rules), the rule of professional conduct shall control.

2.4 No Audit / MAS Conflict. To the extent the Firm performs financial statement audit services for the Client, the Firm shall not provide any non-attest service that would impair the Firm's independence under the AICPA Code. The Parties shall review proposed engagement combinations against the AICPA independence rules before executing any conflicting Engagement Letter.

2.5 Standard of Performance. The Firm shall perform the Services in accordance with the applicable professional standards (Generally Accepted Auditing Standards, Statements on Standards for Tax Services, AICPA Statements on Standards for Accounting and Review Services, the AICPA Code, and applicable state board of accountancy rules) and the standards of the public accounting profession.

2.6 Client Cooperation. The Client shall provide the Firm with timely access to Client Records, personnel, and information as the Firm reasonably requires to perform the Services. The Firm's ability to perform the Services on schedule depends on the Client's cooperation.

ARTICLE 3 — DELIVERABLES AND ACCEPTANCE

3.1 Deliverables. The Firm shall deliver the deliverables specified in each Engagement Letter, which may include tax returns, audit reports, review reports, compilation reports, financial statements, advisory reports, tax memos, and other professional deliverables.

3.2 No Acceptance Mechanic for Professional Reports. The Parties acknowledge that the Firm's professional reports reflect the Firm's professional judgment and are not subject to acceptance by the Client. The Client may not require the Firm to issue any report that, in the Firm's professional judgment, is not supported by the work performed and the evidence obtained.

3.3 Tax Return Acceptance. The Client shall review each tax return prepared by the Firm before signing and filing. The Client is responsible for the contents of any tax return signed and filed by the Client.

3.4 Audit and Review Reports. Audit and review reports shall be issued in the form prescribed by AICPA Statements on Auditing Standards or Statements on Standards for Accounting and Review Services, as applicable.

ARTICLE 4 — FEES AND PAYMENT

4.1 Fees. The Client shall pay the Firm the Fees set forth in each Engagement Letter. Fees may be calculated on a time-and-materials, fixed-fee, milestone-based, or other basis as set forth in the Engagement Letter.

4.2 Annual Aggregate Reference. The aggregate annual Fees payable by the Client across all then-active Engagement Letters as of the Effective Date is approximately {{ANNUAL_FEE_AMOUNT}}, included for reference only.

4.3 Out-of-Scope Work. Any work performed at the Client's request that is outside the scope of an Engagement Letter shall require an amendment to the Engagement Letter or a new Engagement Letter.

4.4 Tax Controversy. Where the Firm is engaged to represent the Client in any tax controversy (such as an IRS examination, appeal, or tax court proceeding), the Engagement Letter shall describe the scope of the controversy work and the Fees.

4.5 Expenses. Reasonable, pre-approved travel and out-of-pocket expenses shall be reimbursed at cost without markup, accompanied by reasonable documentation.

4.6 Payment Terms. All undisputed invoices shall be due and payable within thirty (30) days of the date of invoice.

4.7 Disputed Invoices. The Client shall notify the Firm of any disputed amount within fifteen (15) business days and shall pay all undisputed amounts on time.

4.8 Taxes. Fees are exclusive of all sales, use, or other transaction taxes other than taxes imposed on the Firm's net income.

ARTICLE 5 — TERM AND TERMINATION

5.1 Initial Term. This Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue for a period of {{TERM_YEARS}} years, unless earlier terminated in accordance with Article 13.

5.2 Renewal. This Agreement shall automatically renew for successive one (1) year renewal terms unless either Party provides written notice of non-renewal at least {{NOTICE_DAYS}} days prior to the expiration of the then-current term.

5.3 Engagement Letter Term. Each Engagement Letter shall continue until the relevant engagement is completed or until the Engagement Letter is terminated under Article 13.

ARTICLE 6 — ENGAGEMENT PARTNER AND STAFFING

6.1 Engagement Partner. Each Engagement Letter shall identify the Firm partner responsible for the engagement. The engagement partner shall remain with the engagement except in case of death, disability, departure from the Firm, or rotation as required by applicable professional standards.

6.2 Independence Confirmations. For each financial statement audit Engagement Letter, the Firm shall confirm in writing that the Firm and the engagement team are independent of the Client in accordance with AICPA Code rules and applicable state board of accountancy rules. The Firm shall promptly notify the Client of any change in independence status.

6.3 Background Checks. The Firm shall ensure that all personnel performing the Services have undergone background investigations consistent with the Firm's standard practice and shall be subject to the Firm's quality control system.

6.4 Removal of Personnel on Client Request. The Client may, at any time and for any reason that does not violate applicable employment law, request that the Firm remove any individual personnel from the engagement. The Firm shall comply promptly.

6.5 Non-Solicitation. During the Term and for a period of twelve (12) months thereafter, the Client shall not solicit or hire any partner, manager, or senior associate of the Firm who performed Services for the Client, except through general public employment advertising.

ARTICLE 7 — CLIENT RECORDS, WORKING PAPERS, AND CONFIDENTIALITY

7.1 Client Records. As between the Parties, the Client shall retain ownership of Client Records. Upon completion of the relevant engagement or upon the Client's earlier written request, the Firm shall return Client Records to the Client (or to a successor accountant, as the Client directs), in accordance with the AICPA Code.

7.2 Working Papers. As provided in Section 1.8, the Working Papers prepared by the Firm in the course of performing the Services are the property of the Firm. The Firm shall retain Working Papers in accordance with the Firm's records retention policy and applicable professional standards.

7.3 Access to Working Papers. The Firm shall make Working Papers available to the Client only as required or permitted by the AICPA Code and applicable professional standards. The Firm shall make Working Papers available to a successor accountant of the Client only as permitted by the AICPA Code on file transfers between accountants.

7.4 Confidentiality of Client Information. The Firm shall hold all Client Information in strict confidence and shall use such information solely for the purpose of performing the Services. The Firm shall not disclose Client Information to any third party except (a) to its personnel who have a need to know, (b) to the IRS or any state taxing authority in the course of representing the Client in a tax matter, (c) to a successor accountant as permitted by the AICPA Code, or (d) as required by court order or applicable law. The Firm acknowledges that under IRC § 7216 it may not use or disclose tax return information without the Client's written consent.

7.5 Data Security. The Firm shall implement and maintain reasonable safeguards to protect Client Information, including encryption in transit and at rest, role-based access controls, multi-factor authentication, and a documented information security program.

7.6 Breach Notification. The Firm shall notify the Client of any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to Client Information within seventy-two (72) hours of discovery.

ARTICLE 8 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS

8.1 Background IP. As between the Parties, each Party shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to its respective Background IP. The Firm's Background IP includes the Firm's tax research databases, audit programs, advisory methodologies, and proprietary tools.

8.2 Reports and Tax Returns. Tax returns, audit reports, review reports, compilation reports, advisory reports, and other professional deliverables prepared by the Firm specifically for the Client are owned by the Client, subject to the Firm's right to retain copies in accordance with the AICPA Code and applicable records retention requirements, and subject to the Firm's right to use the underlying Background IP for other clients.

8.3 Working Papers. As provided in Section 7.2, the Working Papers are the property of the Firm.

8.4 No Reuse of Client Confidential Information. The Firm shall not reuse Client Confidential Information for the benefit of any other client.

8.5 No Implied Licenses. Except for the limited rights expressly granted in this Agreement, neither Party grants any rights or licenses to the other Party.

ARTICLE 9 — CONFIDENTIALITY

9.1 Confidentiality Obligations. The Firm shall hold Client Information in strict confidence, in accordance with both this Agreement and the AICPA Code. The Firm shall comply with both the contractual obligations in this Article 9 and the Firm's obligations under the AICPA Code, and in the event of any conflict, the AICPA Code shall control.

9.2 Compelled Disclosure. The Firm may disclose Confidential Information to the extent required by court order or applicable law, with prompt notice to the Client where legally permitted.

9.3 Survival. The Firm's confidentiality obligations shall survive any expiration or termination of this Agreement indefinitely or for as long as required by the AICPA Code and IRC § 7216.

ARTICLE 10 — REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES

10.1 Mutual Warranties. Each Party represents and warrants that it has the corporate authority to enter into this Agreement.

10.2 Firm Warranties. The Firm represents, warrants, and covenants that (a) the Firm and its CPAs are duly licensed and in good standing in the relevant states; (b) the Services shall be performed in accordance with applicable professional standards; (c) the Firm is not currently the subject of any disciplinary action by the {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}} State Board of Accountancy or the AICPA that materially affects its ability to perform under this Agreement; (d) the Firm shall comply with Circular 230, the AICPA Code, the state board of accountancy rules, IRC § 7216, and other applicable federal, state, and local laws; and (e) the Firm shall maintain professional liability (errors and omissions) insurance coverage of not less than Five Million United States Dollars ($5,000,000) per claim throughout the Term.

10.3 Client Warranties. The Client represents and warrants that (a) the Client has the authority to engage the Firm; (b) the Client Records provided to the Firm are accurate and complete in all material respects to the best of the Client's knowledge; and (c) the Client shall not request the Firm to perform any service that would violate Circular 230, the AICPA Code, or any other professional standard.

10.4 Disclaimer. EXCEPT FOR THE EXPRESS WARRANTIES SET FORTH IN THIS ARTICLE 10, THE SERVICES ARE PROVIDED ON THE BASIS OF THE FIRM'S PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT. THE FIRM DOES NOT WARRANT ANY SPECIFIC OUTCOME, ANY SPECIFIC DECISION OF THE INTERNAL REVENUE SERVICE OR ANY OTHER TAXING AUTHORITY, OR ANY SPECIFIC RESULT FROM THE CLIENT'S IMPLEMENTATION OF THE FIRM'S ADVICE.

ARTICLE 11 — INDEMNIFICATION

11.1 Firm Indemnity. The Firm shall defend the Client against any third-party claim arising from the Firm's gross negligence, willful misconduct, breach of this Agreement, or violation of the AICPA Code or other applicable professional standards in the course of performing the Services.

11.2 Client Indemnity. The Client shall defend the Firm against any third-party claim arising from (a) any inaccuracy or incompleteness in Client Records or Client Information provided to the Firm; (b) the Client's misuse of any Firm deliverable in violation of applicable law; or (c) the Client's failure to follow the Firm's professional advice in connection with a matter giving rise to liability.

11.3 Indemnification Procedure. The Party seeking indemnification shall (a) provide prompt written notice; (b) grant the indemnifying Party sole control over the defense and settlement (subject to a no-bad-settlement-without-consent right); and (c) provide reasonable cooperation at the indemnifying Party's expense.

ARTICLE 12 — LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

12.1 Aggregate Cap. EXCEPT AS PROVIDED IN SECTION 12.3, IN NO EVENT SHALL THE AGGREGATE LIABILITY OF THE FIRM ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT OR ANY ENGAGEMENT LETTER, WHETHER IN CONTRACT, TORT (INCLUDING NEGLIGENCE), OR ANY OTHER LEGAL OR EQUITABLE THEORY, EXCEED THE GREATER OF (A) THE TOTAL FEES PAID OR PAYABLE BY THE CLIENT TO THE FIRM IN RESPECT OF THE AFFECTED ENGAGEMENT LETTER DURING THE {{LIABILITY_CAP_MONTHS}} MONTHS IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING THE EVENT GIVING RISE TO LIABILITY, OR (B) THE LIMITS OF THE FIRM'S PROFESSIONAL LIABILITY INSURANCE COVERAGE.

12.2 Exclusion of Indirect Damages. EXCEPT AS PROVIDED IN SECTION 12.3, IN NO EVENT SHALL EITHER PARTY BE LIABLE TO THE OTHER PARTY FOR ANY INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, SPECIAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, EXEMPLARY, OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES, ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT.

12.3 Exceptions to Limitations. The limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 shall not apply to (a) any breach of Article 9 (Confidentiality); (b) any breach of Article 7 (Client Records, Working Papers, and Confidentiality); (c) any liability arising from a Party's gross negligence, willful misconduct, or fraud; (d) any liability arising from the Firm's violation of Circular 230, the AICPA Code, IRC § 7216, or other applicable professional or tax law; or (e) any liability that, under applicable law, cannot be limited.

12.4 Basis of the Bargain. The limitations of liability are an essential element of the bargain between the Parties.

ARTICLE 13 — TERMINATION

13.1 Client Termination at Any Time. The Client may terminate this Agreement, or any Engagement Letter, at any time and for any reason upon written notice to the Firm. The Parties acknowledge that the Client has the right under the AICPA Code to discharge the Firm at any time.

13.2 Firm Termination. The Firm may withdraw from any engagement only as permitted by the AICPA Code and applicable professional standards, and shall provide the Client with reasonable notice and reasonable cooperation in transferring the matter to a successor accountant.

13.3 Termination for Material Breach. Either Party may terminate this Agreement for material breach upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice if the other Party fails to cure the breach within the notice period.

13.4 Termination for Insolvency. Either Party may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the other Party becomes insolvent or files for bankruptcy.

13.5 Effects of Termination. Upon termination: (a) the Firm shall promptly cease performance of the Services; (b) the Firm shall return Client Records in accordance with Section 7.1; (c) the Client shall pay the Firm for all Fees that have accrued through the effective date of termination; (d) the Firm shall provide reasonable cooperation with the Client's successor accountant in accordance with the AICPA Code; and (e) the provisions that by their nature are intended to survive shall so survive.

14.2 Venue and Jurisdiction. Any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

15.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement, together with all Engagement Letters, constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties.

15.2 Amendments. No amendment shall be effective unless in writing executed by both Parties.

15.3 Severability. If any provision is held to be unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force.

15.4 Waiver. No failure or delay in exercising any right shall operate as a waiver thereof.

15.5 Notices. Formal notices shall be in writing and delivered to the addresses set forth in the applicable Engagement Letter.

15.6 Independent Contractors. The Firm is an independent contractor of the Client.

15.7 Subcontractors. The Firm shall not subcontract any of the Services to any third party (other than its network of accountancy firm members) without the Client's prior written consent.

15.8 Assignment. Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party.

15.9 Force Majeure. Neither Party shall be liable for delays caused by events outside its reasonable control (other than payment obligations).

15.10 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts.

15.11 Headings. Headings are for convenience of reference only.
