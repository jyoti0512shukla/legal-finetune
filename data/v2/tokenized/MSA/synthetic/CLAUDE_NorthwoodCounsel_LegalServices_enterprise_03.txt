OUTSIDE COUNSEL ENGAGEMENT AND MASTER LEGAL SERVICES AGREEMENT

This Outside Counsel Engagement and Master Legal Services Agreement (this "Agreement") is made and entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date"), by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Firm"), having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}}, and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, a {{CUSTOMER_STATE}} {{CUSTOMER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Client"), having its principal place of business at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}}. The Firm and Client may each be referred to as a "Party" and collectively as the "Parties."

The Parties acknowledge that this Agreement governs the provision of legal services by the Firm to Client and that the Firm's representation of Client is subject to the rules of professional conduct applicable to attorneys, including without limitation the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct as adopted in the jurisdictions in which the Firm and its attorneys are licensed to practice. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed as a waiver of any obligation imposed on the Firm by such rules. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any applicable rule of professional conduct, the rule of professional conduct shall control.

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Affiliate" means any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with another entity.

1.2 "Background IP" means any Intellectual Property Rights owned or licensable by a Party prior to the Effective Date or independently developed outside of any Engagement Letter, including the Firm's research databases, briefs, opinions, forms, templates, methodologies, and proprietary know-how.

1.3 "Client Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed by Client to the Firm in connection with the Firm's representation of Client, including without limitation Client's business strategy, financial information, customer information, intellectual property, regulatory communications, and any information protected by the attorney-client privilege or the attorney work product doctrine.

1.4 "Engagement" means a discrete legal matter that the Firm is engaged to handle for Client under an Engagement Letter executed pursuant to this Agreement.

1.5 "Engagement Letter" means a written letter executed by both Parties that describes a particular Engagement, including the scope of representation, the responsible Firm partner, the Fees, the budget, and any matter-specific terms. Each Engagement Letter shall be incorporated by reference into this Agreement.

1.6 "Intellectual Property Rights" means all intellectual property and proprietary rights of any kind anywhere in the world, including patents, copyrights, moral rights, trademarks, trade secrets, know-how, database rights, and similar rights.

1.7 "Privileged Materials" means any materials that are protected by the attorney-client privilege or the attorney work product doctrine.

1.8 "Work Product" means all written advice, opinions, briefs, motions, memoranda, contracts, and other tangible legal work product prepared by the Firm specifically for Client in the course of an Engagement, in each case excluding Background IP.

ARTICLE 2 — SCOPE OF SERVICES

2.1 Engagement Letters. The Firm shall provide legal services to Client only pursuant to an Engagement Letter executed by both Parties. Each Engagement Letter shall describe in reasonable detail (a) the scope of the representation; (b) the responsible Firm partner; (c) the staffing model and the rates of the timekeepers expected to work on the matter; (d) the Fees and the basis on which they are calculated; (e) the budget for the matter; (f) any conflict-of-interest considerations identified at the time of engagement; and (g) any matter-specific terms.

2.2 Order of Precedence. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and an Engagement Letter, this Agreement shall control as to the matters expressly addressed in this Agreement, except that the Engagement Letter may expressly modify any provision solely as it applies to that specific Engagement.

2.3 No General Engagement. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed as a general engagement of the Firm for all of Client's legal needs. The Firm represents Client only in the specific Engagements set forth in executed Engagement Letters.

2.4 Standard of Performance. The Firm shall provide legal services with the diligence, skill, and judgment ordinarily exercised by attorneys of similar standing practicing in the same field of law in the same jurisdiction, and in accordance with the applicable rules of professional conduct.

2.5 Conflict Checks. Before accepting any new Engagement, the Firm shall conduct a conflict check in accordance with its standard procedures and the applicable rules of professional conduct. If the Firm identifies any actual or potential conflict of interest, the Firm shall promptly notify Client and shall not proceed with the Engagement without Client's informed written consent or the resolution of the conflict in accordance with the applicable rules of professional conduct.

2.6 Ethical Walls. Where the Firm identifies a potential conflict that may be addressed through the use of an ethical wall (information barrier), the Firm shall implement and maintain such ethical wall in accordance with the applicable rules of professional conduct, and shall describe the ethical wall to Client in writing.

ARTICLE 3 — DELIVERABLES AND ACCEPTANCE

3.1 Delivery of Work Product. The Firm shall deliver Work Product to Client in accordance with the schedule set forth in the applicable Engagement Letter or as otherwise agreed by the Parties.

3.2 No Acceptance Mechanic for Legal Advice. The Parties acknowledge that legal advice is inherently judgmental and that the formal acceptance and cure process described in many commercial services agreements is not appropriate for legal services. Client may, however, request revisions to any Work Product, and the Firm shall use commercially reasonable efforts to address Client's comments at no additional charge where the requested revisions are within the original scope of the Engagement.

3.3 Out-of-Scope Work. Any work performed at Client's request that is outside the scope of the original Engagement Letter shall require an amendment to the Engagement Letter or a new Engagement Letter, and shall be billed at the rates set forth in the amended or new Engagement Letter.

ARTICLE 4 — FEES AND PAYMENT

4.1 Fees. Client shall pay the Firm the Fees set forth in each Engagement Letter. Fees may be billed on the basis of (a) hourly rates of the Firm's timekeepers, (b) fixed fees, (c) contingent fees (where permitted by applicable rules of professional conduct and expressly agreed in the Engagement Letter), (d) blended rates, or (e) any other basis the Parties agree to in the Engagement Letter.

4.2 Annual Aggregate Reference. The aggregate annual Fees payable by Client across all then-active Engagement Letters as of the Effective Date is approximately {{ANNUAL_FEE_AMOUNT}}, included for reference only.

4.3 Rate Adjustments. The Firm's standard hourly rates may be adjusted annually. The Firm shall provide Client with at least sixty (60) days' prior written notice of any rate adjustment. Rate adjustments shall not exceed five percent (5%) per year above the rates in effect during the immediately preceding year, except where Client expressly agrees in writing to a higher adjustment.

4.4 Expenses. Reasonable expenses incurred by the Firm in connection with each Engagement (including filing fees, court reporter fees, expert witness fees, travel, and reproduction costs) shall be billed to Client at cost without markup, accompanied by reasonable documentation. Any single expense in excess of Two Thousand Five Hundred United States Dollars ($2,500) shall require Client's prior written approval.

4.5 Billing Detail. Each invoice shall include a detailed description of the services performed, the timekeeper performing the services, the time spent (in tenths of an hour), and the expenses incurred. Client may request additional billing detail at any time, and the Firm shall provide such detail at no additional charge.

4.6 Payment Terms. All undisputed invoices shall be due and payable within forty-five (45) days of the date of invoice.

4.7 Disputed Invoices. Client may withhold payment of any disputed amount until the dispute is resolved. The Firm shall not impose any late fee, interest charge, or work stoppage with respect to any amount in good-faith dispute.

4.8 Outside Counsel Guidelines. Client may, by written notice to the Firm, require the Firm to comply with Client's then-current outside counsel guidelines, which may address matters such as staffing, billing format, expense reimbursement, and reporting. The Firm shall comply with such guidelines to the extent consistent with the applicable rules of professional conduct.

4.9 Audit Right. Client shall have the right, upon at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice and not more than once per twelve-month period, to audit the Firm's records (including time records and supporting documentation) for any Engagement billed on a time-and-materials basis. Such audit shall be conducted in a manner that does not compromise the attorney-client privilege of any other client of the Firm. If any audit reveals an overcharge in excess of three percent (3%), the Firm shall reimburse Client for the reasonable costs of the audit in addition to refunding the overcharged amounts.

4.10 Taxes. Fees are exclusive of all taxes other than taxes imposed on the Firm's net income.

ARTICLE 5 — TERM AND TERMINATION

5.1 Initial Term. This Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue for a period of {{TERM_YEARS}} years, unless earlier terminated in accordance with Article 13.

5.2 Renewal. This Agreement shall automatically renew for successive one (1) year renewal terms unless either Party provides written notice of non-renewal at least {{NOTICE_DAYS}} days prior to the expiration of the then-current term.

5.3 Engagement Letter Term. Each Engagement Letter shall continue in effect until the Engagement is completed, the Engagement Letter is terminated by mutual agreement, or the Engagement Letter is terminated pursuant to Article 13.

ARTICLE 6 — KEY PERSONNEL AND STAFFING

6.1 Responsible Partner. Each Engagement Letter shall identify the Firm partner who is responsible for the Engagement. The responsible partner shall remain with the Engagement for the duration of the matter, except as provided in Section 6.2.

6.2 Replacement of Responsible Partner. The Firm shall not replace the responsible partner identified in an Engagement Letter without Client's prior written consent, except in the case of (a) the death or disability of the partner, (b) the partner's departure from the Firm, or (c) the resolution of a conflict of interest that requires reassignment under the applicable rules of professional conduct. In any such case, the Firm shall propose a replacement of comparable seniority and expertise within fifteen (15) days, and Client shall have the right to interview the proposed replacement.

6.3 Staffing. The Firm shall staff each Engagement with the appropriate mix of partners, associates, and paralegals as set forth in the Engagement Letter. The Firm shall not assign any timekeeper not identified in the Engagement Letter to bill more than ten percent (10%) of the total time on the Engagement without Client's prior written consent.

6.4 Removal of Personnel on Client Request. Client may, at any time and for any reason that does not violate applicable employment law, request that the Firm remove any individual timekeeper from an Engagement. The Firm shall comply with such request promptly.

6.5 Non-Solicitation of Personnel. During the Term and for a period of twelve (12) months thereafter, Client shall not solicit or hire any partner or associate of the Firm who performed services for Client; provided, however, that this restriction shall not apply to (a) general public employment advertising not directed at Firm personnel, or (b) any individual whose employment with the Firm has terminated for at least six (6) months prior to the date of solicitation.

ARTICLE 7 — CLIENT MATERIALS, PRIVILEGE, AND CONFIDENTIALITY

7.1 Ownership of Client Files. As between the Parties, Client shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to its files, records, documents, and other materials. The Firm shall maintain the Client file in accordance with the applicable rules of professional conduct and shall, upon Client's request, transfer the Client file to Client or to substitute counsel.

7.2 Privilege Preservation. The Firm shall take all reasonable steps to preserve the attorney-client privilege and the attorney work product doctrine with respect to all communications and materials prepared in the course of the Firm's representation of Client. The Firm shall not waive any privilege without Client's prior written consent.

7.3 Communications with the Firm. Communications between Client (including its officers, employees, and agents) and the Firm in connection with the representation are confidential and, to the extent the law so provides, protected by the attorney-client privilege. The Firm shall not disclose any such communications to any third party except as permitted by Client or as required by applicable rules of professional conduct.

7.4 Use of Client Materials. The Firm shall use Client Materials and Client Confidential Information solely for the purpose of performing the Engagement. The Firm shall not use Client Confidential Information for the benefit of any other client, for any commercial purpose other than the Engagement, or for any purpose that would create a conflict of interest.

7.5 Data Security. The Firm shall implement and maintain reasonable administrative, physical, and technical safeguards designed to protect Client Materials and Client Confidential Information against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, or destruction. Such safeguards shall include encryption of Client Materials and Client Confidential Information in transit and at rest, role-based access controls, and a documented information security program reviewed annually.

7.6 Breach Notification. The Firm shall notify Client in writing of any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to or disclosure of Client Materials or Client Confidential Information without undue delay and in any event within seventy-two (72) hours of discovery, in accordance with the Firm's obligations under the applicable rules of professional conduct.

7.7 Return of Client Files. Upon the conclusion of an Engagement or upon Client's earlier written request, the Firm shall return the Client file to Client or to substitute counsel as Client may direct, in accordance with the applicable rules of professional conduct on retention of client files.

ARTICLE 8 — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS

8.1 Background IP. As between the Parties, each Party shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to its respective Background IP. The Firm's Background IP includes the Firm's brief banks, opinion banks, form contracts, research databases, and proprietary methodologies.

8.2 Ownership of Work Product. Work Product prepared by the Firm specifically for Client in the course of an Engagement shall be owned by Client, subject to the Firm's right to retain copies for its own files in accordance with the applicable rules of professional conduct on retention of client files. The Firm hereby assigns to Client all right, title, and interest in and to such Work Product, to the extent assignable.

8.3 License to Background IP in Work Product. To the extent that any Work Product incorporates the Firm's Background IP, the Firm hereby grants to Client a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide license to use such Background IP solely as embedded in the Work Product and solely for Client's own internal use in the matter for which the Work Product was prepared.

8.4 No Reuse Without Consent. The Firm shall not reuse Client Confidential Information or any Work Product specific to Client in connection with any other client matter without Client's prior written consent. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Firm may use general legal knowledge, skills, and experience that its attorneys gain in the course of representing Client, provided that no Client Confidential Information is disclosed.

8.5 No Implied Licenses. Except for the limited rights expressly granted in this Article 8, neither Party grants any rights or licenses to the other Party.

ARTICLE 9 — CONFIDENTIALITY

9.1 Firm's Confidentiality Duty. The Firm acknowledges that, in addition to its obligations under this Article 9, it owes Client a duty of confidentiality under the applicable rules of professional conduct. The Firm shall comply with both the contractual obligations in this Article 9 and its obligations under the applicable rules of professional conduct, and in the event of any conflict, the rules of professional conduct shall control.

9.2 Confidentiality Obligations. The Firm shall (a) hold Client Confidential Information in strict confidence; (b) use Client Confidential Information solely for the purposes of the relevant Engagement; (c) not disclose Client Confidential Information to any third party except to its attorneys and staff who have a need to know and who are bound by the rules of professional conduct or by similar confidentiality obligations; and (d) protect Client Confidential Information using the same degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of similar importance.

9.3 Compelled Disclosure. The Firm may disclose Client Confidential Information to the extent required by court order or applicable law, subject to the Firm's obligations under the applicable rules of professional conduct, provided that the Firm gives Client prompt written notice of the requirement and cooperates with Client's efforts to obtain a protective order.

9.4 Survival. The Firm's confidentiality obligations shall survive any expiration or termination of this Agreement indefinitely or for as long as required by the applicable rules of professional conduct.

ARTICLE 10 — REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES

10.1 Mutual Warranties. Each Party represents and warrants that (a) it is duly organized and in good standing; (b) it has the corporate or partnership authority to enter into this Agreement; and (c) this Agreement constitutes a binding obligation enforceable against it in accordance with its terms.

10.2 Firm Warranties. The Firm represents, warrants, and covenants that (a) the legal services shall be performed with the diligence, skill, and judgment described in Section 2.4; (b) the timekeepers performing the legal services are duly licensed and in good standing in the relevant jurisdictions; (c) the Firm is not currently the subject of any disciplinary action that materially affects its ability to perform under this Agreement; (d) the Firm shall comply with the applicable rules of professional conduct in performing the legal services; and (e) the Firm shall maintain professional liability (errors and omissions) insurance coverage of not less than Twenty-Five Million United States Dollars ($25,000,000) per claim throughout the Term.

10.3 Client Warranties. Client represents and warrants that it has the authority to engage the Firm and that the materials it provides to the Firm in connection with each Engagement do not infringe any third-party rights.

10.4 Disclaimer. EXCEPT FOR THE EXPRESS WARRANTIES SET FORTH IN THIS ARTICLE 10, THE LEGAL SERVICES ARE PROVIDED ON THE BASIS OF THE FIRM'S PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT. THE FIRM DOES NOT WARRANT ANY SPECIFIC LEGAL OUTCOME, ANY SPECIFIC DECISION OF ANY COURT OR REGULATORY BODY, OR ANY SPECIFIC RESULT FROM CLIENT'S IMPLEMENTATION OF THE FIRM'S ADVICE.

ARTICLE 11 — INDEMNIFICATION

11.1 Firm Indemnity. The Firm shall defend Client and its officers, directors, employees, and agents (each a "Client Indemnified Party") against any third-party claim, suit, action, or proceeding (each a "Claim") alleging that the Firm's performance of the legal services caused harm to such third party as a result of the Firm's negligence, willful misconduct, breach of the applicable rules of professional conduct, or breach of this Agreement. The Firm shall indemnify each Client Indemnified Party for any damages, settlement amounts, and reasonable attorneys' fees finally awarded against such Client Indemnified Party with respect to such Claim.

11.2 Client Indemnity. Client shall defend the Firm against any third-party Claim alleging that materials Client provided to the Firm infringe any Intellectual Property Right of such third party.

11.3 Indemnification Procedure. The Party seeking indemnification shall (a) provide prompt written notice; (b) grant the indemnifying Party sole control over the defense and settlement (subject to a no-bad-settlement-without-consent right); and (c) provide reasonable cooperation at the indemnifying Party's expense.

ARTICLE 12 — LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

12.1 Aggregate Cap. EXCEPT AS PROVIDED IN SECTION 12.3, IN NO EVENT SHALL THE AGGREGATE LIABILITY OF THE FIRM ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT OR ANY ENGAGEMENT LETTER, WHETHER IN CONTRACT, TORT (INCLUDING NEGLIGENCE), OR ANY OTHER LEGAL OR EQUITABLE THEORY, EXCEED THE GREATER OF (A) THE TOTAL FEES PAID OR PAYABLE BY CLIENT TO THE FIRM IN RESPECT OF THE AFFECTED ENGAGEMENT DURING THE {{LIABILITY_CAP_MONTHS}} MONTHS IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING THE EVENT GIVING RISE TO LIABILITY, OR (B) THE LIMITS OF THE FIRM'S PROFESSIONAL LIABILITY INSURANCE COVERAGE.

12.2 Exclusion of Indirect Damages. EXCEPT AS PROVIDED IN SECTION 12.3, IN NO EVENT SHALL EITHER PARTY BE LIABLE TO THE OTHER PARTY FOR ANY INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, SPECIAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, EXEMPLARY, OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES, ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT.

12.3 Exceptions to Limitations. The limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 shall not apply to (a) any breach of Article 9 (Confidentiality); (b) any breach of Article 7 (Client Materials, Privilege, and Confidentiality); (c) any liability arising from a Party's gross negligence, willful misconduct, or fraud; (d) any liability arising from a violation of the applicable rules of professional conduct that the rules expressly prohibit being limited; or (e) any liability that, under applicable law, cannot be limited or excluded.

12.4 Basis of the Bargain. The limitations of liability are an essential element of the bargain between the Parties.

ARTICLE 13 — TERMINATION

13.1 Client Termination at Any Time. Client may terminate this Agreement, or any Engagement Letter, at any time and for any reason upon written notice to the Firm. The Parties acknowledge that Client has the right under the applicable rules of professional conduct to discharge the Firm at any time, with or without cause.

13.2 Firm Termination. The Firm may withdraw from any Engagement only as permitted by the applicable rules of professional conduct, and shall provide Client with reasonable notice of withdrawal and reasonable cooperation in transferring the matter to substitute counsel.

13.3 Termination for Material Breach. Either Party may terminate this Agreement for material breach upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice if the other Party fails to cure the breach within the notice period.

13.4 Termination for Insolvency. Either Party may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the other Party becomes insolvent or files for bankruptcy.

13.5 Effects of Termination. Upon expiration or termination: (a) the Firm shall promptly cease performance of legal services under the affected Engagement Letter; (b) the Firm shall transfer the Client file to Client or to substitute counsel as Client directs; (c) Client shall pay the Firm for all Fees that have accrued through the effective date of termination, subject to any reduction permitted by the applicable rules of professional conduct in connection with a Firm withdrawal; and (d) the provisions that by their nature are intended to survive (including Articles 1, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, and 14) shall so survive, in addition to the Firm's continuing obligations under the applicable rules of professional conduct.

ARTICLE 15 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

15.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement, together with all Engagement Letters, constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties with respect to the subject matter hereof.

15.2 Amendments. No amendment shall be effective unless in writing executed by both Parties.

15.3 Severability. If any provision is held to be unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force.

15.4 Waiver. No failure or delay by either Party in exercising any right shall operate as a waiver thereof.

15.5 Notices. Formal notices shall be in writing and delivered to the addresses set forth in the applicable Engagement Letter.

15.6 Independent Contractors. The Firm is an independent contractor of Client. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed as creating an employment, partnership, joint venture, or agency relationship between the Firm and Client.

15.7 Subcontractors and Co-Counsel. The Firm shall not engage co-counsel or any other third-party attorneys to perform any portion of the legal services without Client's prior written consent. The Firm shall remain primarily responsible for the performance of any such co-counsel.

15.8 Assignment. Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party.

15.9 Force Majeure. Neither Party shall be liable for delays caused by events outside its reasonable control (other than payment obligations).

15.10 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts.

15.11 Headings. Headings are for convenience of reference only.
