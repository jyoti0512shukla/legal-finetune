ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Affiliate" means any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with another entity, including any hospital, clinic, ambulatory surgery center, physician practice, or other healthcare facility owned or operated by a Party.

1.2 "AKS" means the federal Anti-Kickback Statute, 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b), and the regulations promulgated thereunder.

1.3 "BAA" means the Business Associate Agreement attached hereto as Exhibit A and executed simultaneously with this Agreement.

1.4 "Background IP" means any Intellectual Property Rights owned or licensable by a Party prior to the Effective Date or independently developed outside of any Statement of Work.

1.5 "Engagement" means a discrete consulting engagement performed by Consultant for the Health System pursuant to an executed Statement of Work.

1.6 "Health System Materials" means any data, information, documents, records, and other materials provided by the Health System to Consultant in connection with the performance of any Statement of Work, including without limitation Protected Health Information.

1.7 "HIPAA" means the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, as amended by the HITECH Act, and the regulations promulgated thereunder at 45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164.

1.8 "Intellectual Property Rights" means all intellectual property and proprietary rights of any kind anywhere in the world.

1.9 "Key Personnel" means the specific individual personnel of Consultant identified as Key Personnel on a Statement of Work.

1.10 "Protected Health Information" or "PHI" has the meaning set forth in 45 C.F.R. § 160.103.

1.11 "Services" means the consulting services to be performed by Consultant for the Health System under one or more Statements of Work, including strategy consulting, regulatory compliance consulting, revenue cycle consulting, clinical operations consulting, and population health consulting.

1.12 "Stark Law" means Section 1877 of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. § 1395nn) and the regulations promulgated thereunder.

1.13 "Statement of Work" or "SOW" means a written document executed by both Parties that describes a particular Engagement.

1.14 "Work Product" means all Deliverables and other work product, materials, ideas, concepts, designs, code, documentation, and inventions created, conceived, or first reduced to practice by or on behalf of Consultant in the course of performing any Engagement, in each case excluding Background IP.

ARTICLE 2 — SCOPE OF SERVICES

2.1 Engagement of Services. Consultant shall provide the Services described in each Statement of Work executed by the Parties from time to time during the Term.

2.2 Statements of Work. Each Statement of Work shall describe (a) the scope of the Services; (b) the Deliverables; (c) the Key Personnel assigned; (d) the Fees and the basis on which they are calculated; (e) the schedule; (f) the acceptance criteria; and (g) any project-specific terms.

2.3 Order of Precedence. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and a Statement of Work, this Agreement shall control as to the matters expressly addressed in this Agreement, except as the Statement of Work expressly modifies as it applies to that specific engagement. The BAA shall control over both this Agreement and any Statement of Work with respect to the handling of PHI.

2.4 Standard of Performance. Consultant shall perform the Services in a professional and workmanlike manner, in accordance with the standards of the healthcare consulting industry, and in compliance with all applicable healthcare laws and regulations.

2.5 Healthcare Compliance Posture. Each Statement of Work shall be structured to comply with the AKS, the Stark Law, the federal False Claims Act, and applicable state law. Without limiting the foregoing: (a) Fees shall be set in advance, shall be consistent with fair market value for the Services, and shall not be determined in any manner that takes into account the volume or value of any referrals or other business generated between the Parties; (b) the scope of the Services shall be commercially reasonable and shall not exceed what is reasonably necessary to accomplish the legitimate business purpose of the Engagement; and (c) the Services shall be commercially reasonable.

2.6 Personal Services Safe Harbor. The Parties intend that this Agreement and each Statement of Work satisfy the personal services and management contracts safe harbor under the AKS at 42 C.F.R. § 1001.952(d), to the extent applicable, and the personal services exception under the Stark Law at 42 C.F.R. § 411.357(d), to the extent applicable. Each Statement of Work shall (a) be in writing and signed by both Parties; (b) specify the Services to be performed; (c) cover all of the Services Consultant will provide to the Health System; (d) have a term of not less than one (1) year (subject to the termination provisions of this Agreement); (e) set forth the aggregate Services contracted for that do not exceed those reasonably necessary to accomplish the commercially reasonable business purpose; (f) provide for compensation set in advance at fair market value; and (g) cover Services that are not in violation of state or federal law.

2.7 Cooperation. The Health System shall provide Consultant with such Health System Materials, personnel access, and information as Consultant reasonably requires to perform the Services, subject to the BAA and applicable confidentiality protections.

ARTICLE 3 — DELIVERABLES AND ACCEPTANCE

3.1 Delivery. Consultant shall deliver each Deliverable in accordance with the schedule and in the format set forth in the applicable Statement of Work.

3.2 Acceptance Period. The Health System shall have a period of twenty (20) business days following delivery of each Deliverable to review the Deliverable and notify Consultant in writing of (a) acceptance, or (b) rejection accompanied by a detailed description of the alleged non-conformity.

3.3 Cure of Non-Conformity. If the Health System rejects a Deliverable, Consultant shall use commercially reasonable efforts to correct the non-conformity within fifteen (15) business days at no additional charge.

3.4 Deemed Acceptance. If the Health System fails to provide written acceptance or rejection within the Acceptance Period, the Deliverable shall be deemed accepted.

ARTICLE 4 — FEES AND PAYMENT

4.1 Fees. The Health System shall pay Consultant the Fees set forth in each Statement of Work. Fees shall be set in advance and shall be consistent with fair market value for the Services.

4.2 Annual Aggregate Reference. The aggregate annual Fees payable by the Health System across all then-active Statements of Work as of the Effective Date is approximately {{ANNUAL_FEE_AMOUNT}}, included for reference only.

4.3 Fair Market Value Documentation. Consultant shall maintain documentation supporting the fair market value of the Fees set forth in each Statement of Work, and shall make such documentation available to the Health System upon request to support the Health System's compliance with the AKS and Stark Law.

4.4 No Volume- or Value-Based Compensation. Notwithstanding anything to the contrary, no portion of the Fees shall be determined in any manner that takes into account the volume or value of any referrals between the Parties or any other business generated between the Parties or with their respective Affiliates.

4.5 Expenses. Reasonable, pre-approved travel and out-of-pocket expenses shall be reimbursed at cost without markup, accompanied by reasonable documentation, and consistent with the Health System's vendor expense policy.

4.6 Payment Terms. All undisputed invoices shall be due and payable within forty-five (45) days of the date of invoice.

4.7 Disputed Invoices. The Health System may withhold payment of any disputed amount until the dispute is resolved.

4.8 Audit Right. The Health System shall have the right to audit Consultant's records for any Services billed on a time-and-materials basis, upon at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice and not more than once per twelve-month period.

4.9 Sunshine Act Reporting. To the extent any payment to Consultant or any individual Consultant personnel constitutes a "transfer of value" reportable by the Health System under the Open Payments program (Sunshine Act), the Parties shall cooperate in good faith to ensure proper reporting.

4.10 Taxes. Fees are exclusive of all taxes other than taxes imposed on Consultant's net income.

ARTICLE 6 — KEY PERSONNEL AND CONFLICTS

6.1 Key Personnel. Each Statement of Work shall identify the Key Personnel of Consultant who are committed to perform the Services.

6.2 No Replacement of Key Personnel Without Consent. Consultant shall not replace Key Personnel without the Health System's prior written consent, except in the case of death, disability, or departure from Consultant.

6.3 Healthcare Industry Conflicts. Consultant acknowledges that the Health System operates in a competitive market and that Consultant may have or develop relationships with other healthcare providers. Consultant shall (a) disclose to the Health System at the time of each Engagement any current consulting relationship between Consultant and any healthcare provider that competes with the Health System in the same geographic market or service line; (b) implement reasonable safeguards (including ethical walls and personnel separation) to protect the Health System's Confidential Information from disclosure to or use for the benefit of any competing healthcare provider; and (c) refrain from using any Health System-specific data, methodologies, or insights developed during an Engagement for the benefit of any competing healthcare provider during the Term of this Agreement and for a period of two (2) years thereafter.

6.4 Exclusivity Carve-Out. Notwithstanding the foregoing, Consultant is not granted exclusivity with respect to any service line of the Health System, and the Health System may engage other consultants for similar or related services. Likewise, Consultant is not prohibited from providing services to other healthcare providers (subject to the safeguards in Section 6.3).

6.5 Background Checks. Consultant shall ensure that all personnel performing the Services have undergone background investigations reasonably acceptable to the Health System and have completed HIPAA training before commencing work.

6.6 Non-Solicitation of Personnel. During the Term and for a period of twelve (12) months thereafter, neither Party shall solicit or hire any Key Personnel of the other Party who performed Services in connection with this Agreement; provided this restriction shall not apply to general public employment advertising.

ARTICLE 7 — HEALTH SYSTEM MATERIALS, PHI, AND CONFIDENTIALITY

7.1 Ownership of Health System Materials. As between the Parties, the Health System shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to Health System Materials, including all PHI.

7.2 Business Associate Status. To the extent Consultant accesses, uses, or discloses PHI in performing the Services, Consultant is a "business associate" of the Health System under HIPAA, and the BAA attached as Exhibit A governs Consultant's handling of PHI.

7.3 Permitted Uses. Consultant shall use Health System Materials and PHI only as necessary to perform the Services and only as permitted by the BAA, this Agreement, and applicable law. Consultant shall not (a) use Health System Materials for any other purpose, (b) sell Health System Materials, (c) use Health System Materials to develop any commercial product or train any artificial intelligence model, or (d) disclose Health System Materials to any third party except as permitted by Section 7.6.

7.4 Minimum Necessary. Consultant shall request, use, and disclose only the minimum necessary Health System Materials required to perform the Services, in accordance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule.

7.5 Security Safeguards. Consultant shall implement and maintain administrative, physical, and technical safeguards designed to comply with the HIPAA Security Rule (45 C.F.R. Part 164, Subpart C), including encryption of Health System Materials in transit (TLS 1.2 or higher) and at rest (AES-256), role-based access controls, multi-factor authentication for privileged access, annual third-party penetration testing, and a documented information security program reviewed annually.

7.6 Permitted Disclosures. Consultant may disclose Health System Materials only (a) to Consultant personnel who have a need to know in connection with the Services and have been trained on HIPAA, or (b) as required by court order or law (in which case Consultant shall give the Health System prompt notice).

7.7 Breach Notification. Consultant shall notify the Health System in writing of any "Breach" of "Unsecured PHI" (as defined under 45 C.F.R. § 164.402) without undue delay and in any event within the timeframe set forth in the BAA, which shall not exceed seventy-two (72) hours of Consultant's discovery of such Breach.

7.8 Right to Audit. Once per calendar year, upon at least sixty (60) days' prior written notice, the Health System shall have the right to audit Consultant's compliance with Article 7 and the BAA.

7.9 Return of Health System Materials. Upon completion of the relevant Engagement, Consultant shall return or destroy all Health System Materials in its possession or control, except to the extent retention is required by law, in which case the retained Health System Materials shall remain subject to the obligations of this Agreement and the BAA.

8.4 Aggregate Industry Benchmarks. Consultant may use de-identified, aggregated industry benchmarks derived from its work across multiple healthcare clients, provided that such benchmarks do not identify the Health System and do not include any PHI or Health System Confidential Information.

8.5 No Implied Licenses. Except for the limited rights expressly granted, neither Party grants any rights or licenses to the other Party.

ARTICLE 9 — CONFIDENTIALITY

9.2 Health System Confidential Information includes Health System Materials, PHI, the Health System's strategic plans, financial information, payor contract terms, and any information about regulatory inquiries or surveys.

9.3 Compelled Disclosure. The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to the extent required by court order or applicable law, with prompt notice to the Disclosing Party where legally permitted.

9.4 Survival. The obligations in this Article 9 shall survive any expiration or termination of this Agreement for a period of seven (7) years.

ARTICLE 13 — TERMINATION

13.1 Termination for Material Breach. Either Party may terminate this Agreement, or any Statement of Work, upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice if the other Party commits a material breach and fails to cure such breach within the notice period.

13.2 Termination for Insolvency. Either Party may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the other Party becomes insolvent, makes a general assignment for the benefit of creditors, files a voluntary petition in bankruptcy, or has an involuntary petition filed against it that is not dismissed within sixty (60) days.

13.3 Termination for Loss of Compliance. The Health System may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if (a) Consultant becomes excluded from any federal or state healthcare program; (b) Consultant becomes the subject of a final, non-appealable enforcement action under HIPAA, the AKS, the Stark Law, the federal False Claims Act, or any other applicable healthcare law; or (c) any government regulator with jurisdiction over the Health System directs the Health System to terminate its relationship with Consultant.

13.4 Effects of Termination. Upon expiration or termination: (a) Consultant shall promptly cease performance of the Services; (b) Consultant shall deliver to the Health System all completed Work Product through the effective date of termination; (c) Consultant shall return Health System Materials in accordance with Section 7.9; (d) the Health System shall pay Consultant for all undisputed Fees that have accrued; (e) each Party shall return or destroy the other Party's Confidential Information; and (f) the provisions that by their nature are intended to survive termination shall so survive.

13.5 Wind-Down Assistance. Following termination, Consultant shall provide reasonable wind-down assistance for a period of up to ninety (90) days to facilitate the orderly transfer of in-progress Work Product, knowledge transfer, and transition to a successor consultant.
