EXECUTIVE EMPLOYMENT AGREEMENT

This EXECUTIVE EMPLOYMENT AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} (the "Company"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual domiciled in Dallas County, Texas (the "Executive"). The Company and the Executive are collectively referred to as the "Parties."

WHEREAS, the Company is engaged in the midstream petroleum business, with operations primarily in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford shale plays;

WHEREAS, the Executive previously led private-equity-backed midstream petroleum portfolio companies and is, as of the Effective Date, indirectly vested in equity of the Company through a rollover transaction in which a portion of the Executive's prior consideration was reinvested in the equity of the Company (the "Rollover Equity");

WHEREAS, the Company desires to employ the Executive as its President and Chief Executive Officer to lead the Company's growth strategy in the midstream petroleum sector;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing premises and the mutual covenants set forth herein, the Parties, intending to be legally bound, agree as follows:

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 "Cause" means the Executive's: (a) conviction of, or plea of guilty or nolo contendere to, a felony or any crime involving fraud or moral turpitude; (b) willful misappropriation of Company funds; (c) willful and material breach of this Agreement after written notice and a thirty-day cure opportunity; or (d) material and willful violation of the Company's code of business conduct, after notice and opportunity to cure.

1.2 "Change in Control" has the meaning customarily given to that term in private-equity-backed midstream petroleum portfolio company employment agreements, including without limitation the sale of more than 50% of the equity or substantially all of the assets of the Company.

1.3 "Good Reason" means a material adverse change in the Executive's title or duties, a material reduction in Base Salary or target Annual Bonus opportunity, or a material relocation of the Executive's principal place of employment more than 50 miles from {{VENUE_CITY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, in each case subject to a 60-day notice and 30-day cure mechanism.

ARTICLE 2 — POSITION AND DUTIES

2.1 Position. The Company shall employ the Executive as {{EMPLOYEE_TITLE}}, reporting to the {{EMPLOYEE_REPORTS_TO}}.

2.2 Duties. The Executive shall perform the duties customarily performed by the chief executive of a midstream petroleum company of comparable size, including overall strategic direction, capital allocation, regulatory affairs, and operational oversight of the Company's gathering, processing, and transportation assets.

2.3 Family Office Carve-Out. The Executive may continue to manage the Executive's family office and personal investments in non-competing energy assets, including investments that the Executive has previously disclosed to the Company in writing, so long as such activities do not (a) materially interfere with the Executive's duties to the Company or (b) involve any business that directly competes with the Company in the Permian Basin or Eagle Ford gathering and processing assets.

2.4 Place of Employment. The Executive's principal place of employment shall be the Company's offices in {{VENUE_CITY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

ARTICLE 3 — TERM

3.1 Initial Term. The Executive's employment shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue for an initial term of {{TERM_YEARS}} years, automatically renewing for successive one-year terms unless either Party provides at least {{NOTICE_DAYS}} days' prior written notice of non-renewal.

ARTICLE 4 — COMPENSATION AND BENEFITS

4.1 Base Salary. The Company shall pay the Executive an annual base salary of {{EMPLOYEE_BASE_SALARY}} (the "Base Salary"), reviewed annually by the Compensation Committee.

4.2 Annual Bonus. The Executive shall be eligible for an annual cash bonus with a target of {{EMPLOYEE_TARGET_BONUS_PCT}}% of Base Salary, based on Company financial performance against objectives set jointly with the Compensation Committee.

4.3 Initial Equity Grant. The Company shall grant the Executive an initial equity award with a grant-date fair value of {{EMPLOYEE_INITIAL_EQUITY}}, vesting in equal annual installments over five years with full acceleration on a Change in Control.

4.4 Rollover Equity. The Rollover Equity is fully vested as of the Effective Date and is not subject to any service-based forfeiture under this Agreement.

4.5 Welfare and Retirement Benefits. The Executive shall participate in the Company's standard executive benefit plans on terms no less favorable than those provided to other senior executives.

4.6 Vacation. The Executive shall be entitled to five (5) weeks of paid vacation per calendar year.

4.7 Indemnification and D&O Insurance. The Company shall indemnify the Executive to the fullest extent permitted by Texas law and shall maintain D&O insurance coverage.

ARTICLE 5 — TERMINATION AND SEVERANCE

5.1 Termination Events. The Executive's employment may be terminated as follows: (a) automatically upon death; (b) by the Company on the Executive's Disability; (c) by the Company for Cause; (d) by the Company without Cause; (e) by the Executive for Good Reason; or (f) by the Executive without Good Reason on 60 days' notice.

5.2 Accrued Obligations. Upon any termination, the Company shall pay the Accrued Obligations.

5.3 Severance for Termination Without Cause or for Good Reason. Subject to the Executive's execution and non-revocation of a release of claims, the Executive shall be entitled to: (a) a lump sum equal to two (2) times Base Salary plus two (2) times target Annual Bonus, paid within fifteen (15) days after the release becomes irrevocable; (b) twenty-four (24) months of COBRA continuation; and (c) accelerated vesting of all unvested time-vesting equity.

5.4 Section 280G. If any payment to the Executive would be subject to the Section 4999 excise tax, the aggregate payments shall be reduced to the extent necessary to avoid the excise tax only if such reduction would result in a greater after-tax amount.

5.5 No Mitigation. The Executive is not required to mitigate damages.

ARTICLE 6 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS

6.1 Acknowledgment of Texas Non-Compete Statute. The Executive and the Company acknowledge that under Texas Business and Commerce Code Section 15.50, a non-competition covenant is enforceable if it is ancillary to or part of an otherwise enforceable agreement and contains reasonable limits on geographic area, scope of activity, and time. The Parties intend that the covenants in this Article 6 satisfy those requirements.

6.2 Non-Competition. During the Executive's employment and for a period of twelve (12) months after the date of termination of the Executive's employment for any reason, the Executive shall not, within the Permian Basin or Eagle Ford shale geographic operating areas of the Company, accept employment with or hold any direct or indirect ownership interest greater than 1% in any business that competes with the Company in the gathering, processing, or transportation of crude oil or natural gas.

6.3 Non-Solicitation of Employees. During the Executive's employment and for twelve (12) months after termination, the Executive shall not directly solicit for employment any senior employee of the Company with whom the Executive worked or had material contact during the twelve months preceding termination, subject to a general-solicitation carve-out.

6.4 Non-Solicitation of Customers. During the Executive's employment and for twelve (12) months after termination, the Executive shall not directly solicit any midstream customer of the Company with whom the Executive had material contact for the purpose of providing competing midstream services.

6.5 Non-Disparagement. The Executive shall not, during or after employment, make any public statement that disparages the Company or its officers, directors, or employees.

6.6 Tolling. The post-termination restricted period shall be tolled day-for-day for any period of breach.

6.7 Reasonableness. The Executive acknowledges that the geographic and substantive restrictions are reasonable and necessary to protect the Company's confidential information and customer relationships, in accordance with Texas law.

ARTICLE 7 — CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION AND IP

7.1 Confidentiality. The Executive shall hold in strict confidence all non-public information of the Company.

7.2 IP Assignment. The Executive assigns to the Company all inventions and works of authorship created during employment that relate to the Company's business.

7.3 DTSA Whistleblower Notice. Pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), the Executive is notified that the Executive cannot be held liable under any federal or state trade secret law for the disclosure of a trade secret made in confidence to a federal, state, or local government official or to an attorney solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law.

ARTICLE 8 — CHANGE IN CONTROL

8.1 Definition. "Change in Control" has the meaning set forth in Section 1.2.

8.2 Double-Trigger Severance. If, within the period beginning ninety (90) days before and ending twenty-four (24) months after a Change in Control, the Executive's employment is terminated by the Company without Cause or by the Executive for Good Reason, the Executive shall be entitled to enhanced severance equal to three (3) times Base Salary plus three (3) times target Annual Bonus, plus full acceleration of all outstanding equity awards.

ARTICLE 9 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION (BINDING ARBITRATION)

9.1 Binding Arbitration. Except as provided in Section 9.5, any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement, or the Executive's employment with the Company, shall be resolved by final and binding arbitration administered by JAMS in {{VENUE_CITY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, in accordance with the JAMS Employment Arbitration Rules and Procedures then in effect. The arbitration shall be conducted by a single arbitrator selected in accordance with the JAMS Rules.

9.2 Confidentiality of Arbitration. The arbitration proceeding and any award rendered therein shall remain confidential, except as may be required by law or as necessary to enforce the award.

9.3 Allocation of Costs. The Company shall bear the JAMS administrative fees and the arbitrator's fees. Each Party shall bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, except that the prevailing Party in any arbitration shall be entitled to recover its reasonable attorneys' fees and costs from the non-prevailing Party.

9.4 Waiver of Jury Trial. EACH PARTY HEREBY IRREVOCABLY WAIVES ANY RIGHT TO A TRIAL BY JURY.

9.5 Equitable Relief Carve-Out. Notwithstanding Section 9.1, either Party may seek temporary or preliminary injunctive relief in any court of competent jurisdiction in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, to enforce the restrictive covenants in Article 6 or the confidentiality provisions in Article 7, pending the outcome of the arbitration.

9.6 Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.

ARTICLE 10 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

10.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties.

10.2 Amendment. This Agreement may be amended only by a writing signed by both Parties.

10.3 Assignment. The Executive may not assign this Agreement. The Company may assign this Agreement to any successor.

10.4 Survival. Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, and 9 shall survive the termination of the Executive's employment.

10.5 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, the Parties have executed this Agreement as of the Effective Date.
