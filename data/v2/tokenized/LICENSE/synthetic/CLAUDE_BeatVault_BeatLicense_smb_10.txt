1.4 New Song. The original song the Artist creates by recording vocals on top of the Beat is the "New Song." The New Song consists of (a) the Beat (still owned by the Producer) and (b) the Artist's vocal performance and lyrics (owned by the Artist).

ARTICLE 3 — RESTRICTIONS

3.1 No standalone Beat distribution. The Artist cannot distribute the Beat as a standalone instrumental track. The Beat may only be distributed as part of the New Song with the Artist's vocals on top.

3.2 No re-licensing the Beat to other artists. The Artist cannot license, sell, or transfer the Beat to any other artist or producer. If the Artist's friend wants to record a different song on the same Beat, that friend must purchase a separate license from the Producer.

3.3 No use as instrumental track in commercials or sync. The standard beat license does NOT include synchronization rights for use in commercials, films, TV shows, or video games. If a brand wants to use the New Song in a commercial, the brand must obtain a separate sync license from the Producer (and a master use license, since the master sound recording of the Beat is owned by the Producer).

3.4 No modifications to the Beat. The Artist cannot edit, remix, or significantly modify the Beat itself. The Artist may make minor edits for arrangement (looping, trimming for length, fade in/out) but cannot change the underlying composition. Major modifications require a separate written agreement.

3.5 Producer credit. The Artist will credit the Producer as the producer of the Beat in the song metadata, on the cover art, and in the streaming platform credits. The credit format is: "Produced by {{PROVIDER_NAME}}."

3.6 No derogatory content. The Artist cannot use the Beat in a New Song that contains content the Producer would reasonably find derogatory of the Producer, the Producer's brand, or the Producer's other customers.

ARTICLE 4 — FEES AND PAYMENT

4.1 License fee. The Artist pays the upfront license fee shown on the Producer's order. The fee depends on the exclusivity tier (non-exclusive is the lowest tier; exclusive is the highest).

4.2 Streaming royalties. Streaming royalties from digital streaming services (the Artist's payouts from Spotify, Apple Music, etc.) belong to the Artist. The Producer is entitled to:
- a producer royalty equal to {{LICENSE_ROYALTY_RATE}} of the Artist's net streaming revenue from the New Song, if the Artist generates more than 50,000 streams per month from the New Song
- a separate publishing royalty for the Producer's authorship of the underlying composition, collected through the Producer's PRO (BMI / ASCAP / SESAC) — the Artist will register the New Song with the Producer as 50% co-writer of the publishing share

4.3 Mechanical royalties. Mechanical royalties for downloads and physical copies of the New Song are split 50/50 between the Producer (for the Beat composition) and the Artist (for the lyrics).

4.4 Unit cap for non-exclusive license. The non-exclusive beat license is capped at 10,000 distributed units (combined streaming + downloads + physical sales). Once the New Song crosses 10,000 units, the Artist must upgrade to the exclusive license tier or stop distributing the New Song.

4.5 Sync fee separate. As stated in Section 3.3, sync uses (commercials, film, TV, video games) require a separate sync license and a separate sync fee.

4.6 Currency and payment. All payments in United States Dollars. The Artist's upfront license fee is paid via the Producer's online store at the time of purchase.
