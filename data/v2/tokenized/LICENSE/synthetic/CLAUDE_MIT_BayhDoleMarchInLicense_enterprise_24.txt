PATENT LICENSE AGREEMENT — UNIVERSITY TECH TRANSFER WITH BAYH-DOLE MARCH-IN PROVISIONS

This PATENT LICENSE AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} ({{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}}) (the "University"), acting through its Office of Technology Transfer, and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, a {{CUSTOMER_STATE}} {{CUSTOMER_ENTITY_TYPE}} ({{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}}) (the "Licensee"). The University licenses to the Licensee certain patent rights resulting from federally funded research at the University, subject to the requirements of the Bayh-Dole Act (35 U.S.C. §§ 200-212) and the implementing regulations at 37 C.F.R. Part 401, including the U.S. government's reserved license, march-in rights, and the U.S. manufacturing preference.

This Agreement specifically addresses Bayh-Dole compliance in greater detail than the University's standard license, given the political and academic visibility of the underlying federally funded research and the heightened scrutiny that the University and the Licensee may face.

RECITALS

WHEREAS, the inventions claimed in the Licensed Patents identified on Schedule A (the "Licensed Patents") were made in whole or in part with funding from the U.S. government under the funding agreements identified on Schedule B (the "Funding Agreements") and are therefore "subject inventions" within the meaning of 35 U.S.C. § 201(e);

WHEREAS, the University has elected to retain title to the Licensed Patents under 35 U.S.C. § 202(a), has timely disclosed the inventions to the U.S. funding agency under 35 U.S.C. § 202(c)(1), has timely filed for patent protection under 35 U.S.C. § 202(c)(3), and has otherwise complied with the Bayh-Dole Act in establishing the University's title to the Licensed Patents;

WHEREAS, the U.S. government has reserved a non-exclusive, non-transferable, irrevocable, paid-up license to practice or have practiced the Licensed Patents for or on behalf of the U.S. government throughout the world, and has reserved march-in rights under 35 U.S.C. § 203;

WHEREAS, the Licensee wishes to obtain an exclusive license under the Licensed Patents to develop and commercialize Licensed Products and is willing to undertake the diligence, manufacturing, and reporting obligations necessary for the University and the Licensee to comply with Bayh-Dole;

NOW, THEREFORE, the Parties agree as follows.

ARTICLE 3 — DILIGENCE AND COMMERCIALIZATION OBLIGATIONS

3.1 Commercially Reasonable Efforts. The Licensee shall use commercially reasonable efforts to bring the Licensed Patents to Practical Application as that term is used in 35 U.S.C. § 203(a)(1) and the Bayh-Dole Act, including by developing, obtaining regulatory approval for (where applicable), and commercializing one or more Licensed Products within the Field and Territory.

3.2 Development Plan. The Licensee shall provide the University with a detailed development plan, attached as Schedule D, identifying the Licensee's planned development activities, target dates, key milestones, and estimated timeline to first commercial sale.

3.3 Annual Diligence Report. The Licensee shall provide the University with an annual Diligence Report by February 15 of each year, describing the Licensee's progress against the development plan, the Licensee's investment of resources in the Licensed Patents, the status of any clinical trials or regulatory submissions, and any expected delays or revisions to the development plan. The University shall be permitted to share the Diligence Report with the relevant Funding Agencies.

3.4 Diligence Milestones. The Licensee shall achieve the milestones set forth in Schedule E by the dates specified. If the Licensee fails to meet a milestone by the applicable deadline, the University may, at the University's option, (a) grant the Licensee an extension on terms negotiated in good faith, (b) convert the exclusive license to non-exclusive, or (c) terminate this Agreement under Article 9.

3.5 No Suppression. The Licensee shall not suppress, withhold, or fail to commercialize the Licensed Patents for the purpose of protecting any other product, technology, or business interest of the Licensee. Any such suppression would constitute a breach of this Agreement and a basis for the University to seek the Licensor to invoke its march-in rights under Section 11.2.

ARTICLE 4 — U.S. MANUFACTURING PREFERENCE (37 C.F.R. § 401.14(i))

4.1 U.S. Manufacturing Requirement. For each Licensed Product (or other product produced through the use of the Licensed Patents) that is sold in the United States, the Licensee shall manufacture such product substantially in the United States, in accordance with 35 U.S.C. § 204 and 37 C.F.R. § 401.14(i).

4.2 Waiver of U.S. Manufacturing Requirement. The University may seek a waiver of the U.S. manufacturing preference from the Funding Agency in accordance with 37 C.F.R. § 401.14(i)(2) if (a) reasonable but unsuccessful efforts have been made to grant licenses on similar terms to potential licensees that would be likely to manufacture substantially in the United States, or (b) under the circumstances domestic manufacture is not commercially feasible. The Licensee shall reasonably cooperate with the University in any such waiver application.

4.3 Compliance Reporting. The Licensee shall include in each annual Diligence Report a description of the Licensee's compliance with the U.S. manufacturing preference, including the locations at which Licensed Products are manufactured, the percentage of total manufacturing that occurs in the United States, and the rationale (if any) for non-U.S. manufacturing.

ARTICLE 5 — FINANCIAL TERMS

5.1 Upfront Fee. The Licensee shall pay the University an upfront license fee of {{LICENSE_UPFRONT_FEE}} within sixty (60) days of the Effective Date.

5.2 Annual Maintenance Fee. The Licensee shall pay the University an annual maintenance fee of {{LICENSE_MIN_ANNUAL_ROYALTY}} on each anniversary of the Effective Date until the first commercial sale of a Licensed Product.

5.3 Running Royalty. The Licensee shall pay the University a running royalty equal to {{LICENSE_ROYALTY_RATE}} on Net Sales of Licensed Products in the Territory.

5.4 Minimum Annual Royalty. After first commercial sale, the Licensee shall pay the University a minimum annual royalty of {{LICENSE_MIN_ANNUAL_ROYALTY}}, creditable against running royalties.

5.5 Sublicense Income Sharing. The Licensee shall pay the University a percentage of all sublicense income (upfront fees, milestones, royalties, and other consideration received from sublicensees), as set forth in Schedule F. The University's share of sublicense income shall not be less than the running royalty that would have been payable on direct sales by the Licensee.

5.6 Patent Prosecution Reimbursement. The Licensee shall reimburse the University for all out-of-pocket patent prosecution costs from the Effective Date forward, plus a portion of historical prosecution costs as set forth in Schedule G.

5.7 Royalty Reports. The Licensee shall provide the University with semi-annual royalty reports within sixty (60) days after June 30 and December 31, with running royalties paid concurrently.

5.8 Currency. All payments in United States Dollars.

ARTICLE 6 — RECORDS AND ROYALTY AUDIT

6.1 Records. The Licensee shall maintain complete books and records of the Licensed Products and Net Sales for at least six (6) years.

6.2 Audit Right. The University may, on at least thirty (30) days' notice and not more than {{LICENSE_AUDIT_FREQUENCY}}, engage an independent CPA to audit the Licensee's books for purposes of verifying royalty reports.

6.3 Audit Costs and Findings. The University bears the cost of the audit unless underpayment exceeds 5%, in which case the Licensee pays. Underreported royalties are paid plus interest.

ARTICLE 7 — CONFIDENTIALITY AND ACADEMIC PUBLICATION

7.1 Confidentiality. The Licensee shall hold University Confidential Information in strict confidence. The University and its researchers shall hold Licensee Confidential Information in confidence.

7.2 Academic Publication. The University and its faculty researchers retain the right to publish the results of research underlying the Licensed Patents in academic journals, scientific conferences, and theses. Before publication, the University shall provide the Licensee with at least thirty (30) days to identify any patentable subject matter for which the Licensee wishes the University to file additional patent applications. The Licensee shall not unreasonably delay academic publication and shall not block publication.

7.3 Government Reporting. Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, the University may disclose information about the Licensed Patents and the Licensee's commercialization activities to the Funding Agencies as required by Bayh-Dole reporting obligations.

7.4 Survival. Confidentiality obligations survive termination for ten (10) years.

8.3 Licensee Indemnification of University. The Licensee shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless the University, its trustees, officers, faculty, employees, students, agents, and the Funding Agencies from any third-party claim arising out of (a) the Licensee's use of the Licensed Patents, (b) the Licensee's manufacture, use, or sale of any Licensed Product, (c) any product liability claim, or (d) the Licensee's breach of this Agreement. ARTICLE 9 — TERM AND TERMINATION OF LICENSE

9.1 Term. The Term commences on the Effective Date and continues until expiration of the last Valid Claim of the Licensed Patents, unless terminated earlier under this Article 9.

9.2 Termination by Licensee. The Licensee may terminate this Agreement at any time on ninety (90) days' written notice.

9.3 Termination by University. The University may terminate this Agreement upon thirty (30) days' written notice for the Licensee's:

(a) failure to pay any fee or royalty within the applicable grace period;

(b) failure to maintain insurance;

(c) failure to meet a diligence milestone after extension under Section 3.4;

(d) failure to comply with the U.S. manufacturing preference (subject to a cure period and cooperation in seeking a waiver if appropriate);

(e) suppression or non-commercialization of the Licensed Patents in violation of Section 3.5;

(f) material breach of any other provision after thirty (30) days' notice and opportunity to cure; or

(g) bankruptcy, insolvency, or assignment for the benefit of creditors.

9.4 Conversion to Non-Exclusive. As an alternative to termination, the University may convert an exclusive license to non-exclusive if the Licensee fails to meet diligence requirements.

9.5 Effect of Termination. Upon termination:

(a) The Licensee shall cease all manufacture, use, and sale of Licensed Products, subject to a six-month sell-off period.

(b) The Licensee shall pay all amounts due through the end of the sell-off period.

(c) Sublicenses (if any) may, at the University's option, survive (with the University stepping into the Licensee's shoes for receiving sublicensee payments) or terminate.

(d) The Licensee shall return or destroy any University Confidential Information.

9.6 Survival. Articles 6 (post-termination audit), 7 (confidentiality), 8 (with respect to claims arising before termination), 9.5, 10, and 11 survive termination.

ARTICLE 11 — BAYH-DOLE COMPLIANCE AND MARCH-IN RIGHTS

11.1 Bayh-Dole Status. The Parties acknowledge that the Licensed Patents are subject inventions under Bayh-Dole, that the University has retained title under 35 U.S.C. § 202, and that this Agreement is intended to fulfill the University's obligation to commercialize the inventions for the benefit of the public.

11.2 March-In Rights. The Parties acknowledge that the U.S. government has march-in rights under 35 U.S.C. § 203, which authorize the U.S. government to require the University (or, through the University, the Licensee) to grant additional licenses on reasonable terms in the following circumstances:

(a) the University or the Licensee has not taken effective steps to achieve Practical Application of the Licensed Patents within a reasonable time;

(b) action is necessary to alleviate health or safety needs that are not reasonably satisfied by the University or the Licensee;

(c) action is necessary to meet requirements for public use specified by federal regulations and such requirements are not reasonably satisfied by the University or the Licensee; or

(d) action is necessary because the U.S. manufacturing preference under Section 4 has not been met or waived in accordance with Bayh-Dole.

11.3 Consequences of March-In Exercise. If the U.S. government exercises march-in rights with respect to the Licensed Patents:

(a) The exclusivity of the license under Article 2 may be reduced, modified, or terminated by operation of law to the extent necessary to give effect to the march-in determination.

(b) The Licensee shall reasonably cooperate with the University in any administrative or judicial proceeding relating to the march-in.

(c) The Licensee shall not have any claim for damages against the University, the U.S. government, or any third party arising from the U.S. government's exercise of march-in rights.

(d) The U.S. government's exercise of march-in rights shall not, by itself, terminate the Licensee's license, but the Licensee's exclusivity may be lost as a result of the exercise.

11.4 March-In Procedures. The Parties acknowledge that the U.S. government has not, as of the Effective Date, exercised march-in rights with respect to any subject invention. The procedures for an exercise of march-in are set forth in 37 C.F.R. § 401.6 and include written notice, an opportunity to respond, an opportunity for a hearing, and judicial review.

11.5 Government Use. The U.S. Government Reserved License under Section 2.2 entitles the U.S. government (and parties acting for or on behalf of the U.S. government) to practice the Licensed Patents for U.S. government purposes throughout the world without payment. This use is paramount to the Licensee's exclusive license, and the Licensee shall not assert the Licensed Patents against the U.S. government.

11.6 Reporting. The Licensee shall provide the University with all information the University needs to fulfill its reporting obligations to the Funding Agencies under 37 C.F.R. § 401.14, including iEdison reporting.
