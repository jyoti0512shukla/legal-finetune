INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT — PHARMACEUTICAL CONSULTANT

This INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Sponsor"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual residing at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Consultant"). The Sponsor and the Consultant are collectively the "Parties." This Agreement is the Sponsor's standard form for individual consultant engagements supporting clinical development and shall be governed by the Sponsor's negotiated terms.

ARTICLE 1 — ENGAGEMENT

1.1 Engagement of Consultant. The Sponsor hereby engages the Consultant to provide the consulting services described in Section 1.2 (the "Services") on the terms set forth in this Agreement.

1.2 Scope of Services. The Consultant shall provide professional consulting services to the Sponsor's Clinical Development organization, including: (a) advising on clinical trial protocol design, (b) reviewing investigator brochures and clinical study reports, (c) supporting regulatory interactions with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and equivalent foreign agencies (preparation of materials, attendance at meetings as observer or technical expert), (d) advising on the Sponsor's data and safety monitoring board procedures, and (e) such other clinical development advisory services as the Sponsor's Chief Medical Officer may reasonably request.

1.3 Personal Services. The Consultant shall personally perform the Services and shall not delegate or subcontract.

1.4 Best Efforts. The Consultant shall use the Consultant's best efforts in a professional manner consistent with ICH GCP E6(R2) and the standards of the Consultant's profession.

1.5 Reporting. The Consultant shall report to {{IC_REPORT_TO}} and shall provide written status reports on a monthly basis.

ARTICLE 2 — TERM AND TERMINATION

2.1 Term. The Term shall commence on the Effective Date and continue for {{IC_TERM_MONTHS}} months thereafter, unless earlier terminated.

2.2 Termination for Convenience. Either Party may terminate on {{IC_NOTICE_DAYS_CONVENIENCE}} days' written notice.

2.3 Termination for Cause. The Sponsor may terminate immediately for: (a) the Consultant's material breach, (b) willful misconduct, (c) loss of the Consultant's professional license, (d) exclusion from federal health care programs, (e) any conflict of interest the Consultant fails to disclose, (f) any breach of GCP or applicable clinical trial regulations, or (g) conviction of any felony involving healthcare fraud, scientific misconduct, or moral turpitude.

2.4 Effect of Termination. The Consultant returns work product, surrenders Sponsor materials and documents, submits a final invoice, and cooperates in transferring open matters.

ARTICLE 3 — FEES, EXPENSES, AND COMPENSATION

3.1 Hourly Rate. The Sponsor shall pay the Consultant at the hourly rate of {{IC_HOURLY_RATE}} per hour, subject to the not-to-exceed cap in Section 3.2.

3.2 Not-to-Exceed Cap. The cumulative compensation under this Agreement shall not exceed {{IC_FIXED_FEE}} (the "NTE Cap") without a written amendment.

3.3 Time Tracking. The Consultant shall maintain detailed time records.

3.4 Invoicing. Monthly, due net 60.

3.5 Expense Reimbursement. The Sponsor reimburses pre-approved travel and out-of-pocket expenses, subject to the Sponsor's standard policy. Any individual expense above Seven Hundred Fifty United States Dollars (US$750) requires written pre-approval.

3.6 Fair Market Value Compensation. The Consultant's compensation under this Agreement is set at fair market value for the Consultant's services and reflects the Consultant's specialized expertise. The compensation is not determined in any manner that takes into account the volume or value of any prescriptions or referrals between the Consultant and the Sponsor's products. This compensation arrangement is intended to comply with the personal services and management contracts safe harbor under the Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b) and the personal services arrangement exception under the Stark Law (42 U.S.C. § 1395nn).

3.7 Sunshine Act Reporting. The Sponsor shall report payments to the Consultant under this Agreement as required by the Physician Payment Sunshine Act (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7h) and the implementing regulations at 42 C.F.R. Part 403 Subpart I, including aggregate transfer-of-value reporting on the CMS Open Payments database.

3.8 No Additional Compensation. No equity, bonus, or severance.

ARTICLE 4 — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR RELATIONSHIP

4.1 No Employment Relationship.

4.2 No Withholding.

4.3 No Employee Benefits.

4.4 Form W-9; Form 1099-NEC. The Consultant shall provide a Form W-9. The Sponsor shall report compensation on Form 1099-NEC.

4.5 Self-Employment Taxes. The Consultant is solely responsible for all federal, state, and local income taxes, self-employment taxes, Social Security and Medicare contributions.

4.6 Tax Indemnification. The Consultant indemnifies the Sponsor against any reclassification claim or tax authority claim.

4.7 License and Eligibility Maintenance. The Consultant shall maintain (a) an active, unrestricted medical license, (b) board certification (where applicable), and (c) eligibility to participate in Medicare, Medicaid, and other federal health care programs (i.e., not on the OIG LEIE / SAM exclusion list). The Sponsor shall verify the Consultant's eligibility monthly.

ARTICLE 5 — CONFIDENTIALITY AND CLINICAL TRIAL DATA

5.1 Confidential Information. All non-public information of the Sponsor, including clinical trial data, investigator brochure content, regulatory submission materials, drug development strategy, business plans, and any other non-public information, constitutes Confidential Information.

5.2 Confidentiality Obligation. The Consultant shall hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence during the Term and at all times thereafter (and in any event for ten (10) years after the relevant Sponsor product receives marketing approval, in line with the Sponsor's records retention policy).

5.3 Clinical Trial Subject Privacy. To the extent the Consultant has access to any subject-level clinical trial data or any personally identifiable health information about clinical trial subjects, the Consultant shall handle it in accordance with HIPAA, applicable data protection laws (GDPR, UK GDPR, LGPD, etc.), and the Sponsor's data handling SOPs.

5.4 No Personal Devices. The Consultant shall not store Confidential Information on personal devices, in personal email, or in any non-Sponsor-approved storage. All access shall be through Sponsor-approved systems.

5.5 Return of Materials. On termination, the Consultant returns all Confidential Information.

5.6 Injunctive Relief. The Sponsor may seek injunctive relief without bond.

5.7 Cooperation with Regulators. The Consultant shall cooperate with any FDA, EMA, or other regulatory authority investigation of the Sponsor's clinical development program.

ARTICLE 6 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

6.1 Non-Competition During Engagement. During the Term, the Consultant shall not provide consulting services on the same therapeutic area or competing products to any other pharmaceutical company that competes directly with the Sponsor in the relevant therapeutic area, except as expressly permitted by the Sponsor in writing.

6.2 Continuing Practice Permitted. The Consultant remains free to engage in clinical practice in the Consultant's profession during and after the Term. The restriction in Section 6.1 applies only to advisory and consulting services, not clinical practice.

6.3 Non-Solicitation. The Consultant shall not solicit any Sponsor employee for twelve (12) months after termination.

6.4 Conflicts of Interest. The Consultant shall promptly disclose any actual or potential conflict of interest, including any consulting or advisory engagement with another pharmaceutical company, contract research organization, or relevant regulator.

6.5 Disclosure to Other Parties. The Consultant acknowledges that the Sponsor may be required to disclose the Consultant's identity and the existence of this Agreement to (a) the FDA or other regulatory authorities, (b) institutional review boards, (c) data safety monitoring boards, (d) clinical investigators at the Sponsor's clinical sites, and (e) academic publishers (as a financial relationship disclosure under journal authorship rules), and the Consultant consents to such disclosure.

ARTICLE 7 — REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES

7.1 Authority. The Consultant has full authority to enter this Agreement.

7.2 No Conflicts. The Consultant is not subject to any agreement that would prevent the Services.

7.3 Professional Qualifications. The Consultant has the professional qualifications, training, and experience to perform the Services.

7.4 No Disqualification or Debarment. The Consultant has not been debarred under Section 306 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (21 U.S.C. § 335a) or excluded from federal health care programs.

7.5 No Research Misconduct. The Consultant has not been the subject of any finding of research misconduct, fraud, or inappropriate behavior in connection with clinical trials or scientific research.

7.6 Compliance with Law. The Consultant will comply with all applicable laws and regulations, including ICH GCP E6(R2), 21 C.F.R. Parts 50, 54, 56, 312, and 314, and all other clinical trial regulations.

ARTICLE 8 — INDEMNIFICATION AND INSURANCE

8.1 Indemnification by Consultant. In addition to the tax indemnification in Section 4.6, the Consultant indemnifies the Sponsor for any breach of representations and warranties, willful misconduct, or gross negligence.

8.2 Limitation. The Consultant's aggregate liability under Article 4 and this Article 8 shall not exceed three (3) times the cumulative compensation, except for tax indemnification, willful misconduct, fraud, or research misconduct (uncapped).

8.3 Professional Liability Insurance. The Consultant shall maintain professional liability insurance with coverage of at least Three Million United States Dollars (US$3,000,000) per claim.

9.2 Exclusive Venue. Disputes shall be brought in the state or federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

9.3 Equitable Relief. The Sponsor may seek equitable relief to enforce confidentiality, restrictive covenants, and clinical trial regulatory provisions.
