INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT — FREELANCE DESIGNER

This Agreement is between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} located at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Company"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Designer"). It's effective on {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}.

The Company is hiring the Designer as an independent contractor to do design work. Here's how it works.

ARTICLE 1 — ENGAGEMENT

1.1 What the Designer is doing. The Company is engaging the Designer to provide visual design services — logos, brand identity, marketing materials, website design, and similar — on a project-by-project basis as agreed in writing between the parties for each project.

1.2 Project descriptions. Each project will be described in a written project brief signed (or accepted by email) by both parties. The brief will identify the deliverables, target dates, and the specific compensation for that project.

1.3 Best efforts. The Designer will do the work to a professional standard.

1.4 Personal services. The Designer will do the work personally. The Designer can use research assistants or production assistants for non-creative tasks, but the actual design work has to be done by the Designer.

ARTICLE 2 — TERM AND TERMINATION

2.1 Term. This Agreement starts on the Effective Date and continues until either party terminates it.

2.2 Termination for convenience. Either party can terminate this Agreement on {{IC_NOTICE_DAYS_CONVENIENCE}} days' written notice for any reason.

2.3 Termination for cause. The Company can terminate immediately if the Designer materially breaches the Agreement, commits willful misconduct, or fails to deliver work that meets professional standards.

2.4 Effect of termination. On termination, the Designer will (a) deliver any in-progress work the Company has paid for, (b) submit a final invoice, and (c) return any Company materials in the Designer's possession.

ARTICLE 3 — FEES, EXPENSES, AND COMPENSATION

3.1 Fee structure. Compensation depends on the project type, agreed in each project brief. The default options are:

(a) Hourly: {{IC_HOURLY_RATE}} per hour, with a not-to-exceed cap stated in each project brief.

(b) Fixed fee: a project-specific fixed fee stated in each project brief.

3.2 Invoicing. The Designer will invoice the Company monthly (for hourly work) or on milestone completion (for fixed-fee work). Invoices are due net 15.

3.3 Expense reimbursement. The Company will reimburse the Designer for pre-approved expenses (stock photography licenses, font licenses, print samples, courier costs). Any expense over Two Hundred Fifty United States Dollars (US$250) needs pre-approval in writing.

3.4 No additional compensation. The fees and expenses in this Article 3 are the Designer's entire compensation. No equity, no bonuses, no benefits.

3.5 Late payment. Invoices not paid within 45 days accrue interest at 1.5% per month or the maximum legal rate, whichever is less.

ARTICLE 4 — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR RELATIONSHIP

4.1 No employment relationship. The Designer is an independent contractor, not an employee. This Agreement does not create any employer-employee, partnership, joint venture, or agency relationship between the parties.

4.2 No withholding. The Company will not withhold taxes from the Designer's compensation. The Designer is responsible for all federal, state, and local income taxes, self-employment taxes, Social Security and Medicare contributions, and any other tax obligations.

4.3 No employee benefits. The Designer is not eligible for any benefits provided to Company employees: no medical, dental, vision, retirement, paid leave, workers' compensation, or unemployment insurance.

4.4 W-9 and 1099. The Designer will provide a completed Form W-9 to the Company. The Company will report compensation on Form 1099-NEC.

4.5 Designer's control. The Designer determines how, when, and where to do the work. The Designer can work for other clients, use the Designer's own equipment and software, and is not subject to Company supervision in the design process. The Company specifies what it wants delivered; the Designer decides how to deliver it.

4.6 Tax indemnification. If the Designer fails to pay required taxes, or if any taxing authority claims the Designer should have been classified as an employee, the Designer will indemnify the Company for any resulting claims, taxes, penalties, interest, and reasonable attorneys' fees.

ARTICLE 5 — WORK PRODUCT AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

5.1 Final Work Product owned by Company. All final deliverables the Designer produces under this Agreement (the "Work Product") will be owned by the Company once the Company has paid in full for the relevant project. Until full payment, the Designer retains ownership.

5.2 Work made for hire. The Work Product is "work made for hire" under Section 101 of the U.S. Copyright Act to the maximum extent allowed. To the extent the Work Product doesn't qualify as work made for hire, the Designer assigns it to the Company at the time of full payment.

5.3 Designer's pre-existing assets. The Designer retains all rights in any pre-existing fonts, brushes, illustrations, templates, source files, and other design assets that the Designer creates outside of and unrelated to a specific Company project. To the extent any such pre-existing assets are incorporated into the Work Product, the Designer grants the Company a non-exclusive, perpetual, royalty-free license to use them as part of the delivered Work Product.

5.4 Designer's portfolio rights. The Designer may include the Work Product in the Designer's professional portfolio (online or physical) and may credit the Designer's role on the Work Product, unless the Company expressly notes a project as confidential at the start of the project.

5.5 Original work. The Designer represents that the Work Product will be original or properly licensed and will not infringe anyone else's IP rights. The Designer warrants that any stock images, fonts, or third-party content incorporated into the Work Product will be properly licensed for the Company's intended use.

ARTICLE 6 — CONFIDENTIALITY

6.1 Confidential Information. "Confidential Information" means any non-public information of the Company, including business plans, customer lists, pricing, brand strategy, and any project-specific materials marked confidential.

6.2 Confidentiality obligation. The Designer will hold Confidential Information in strict confidence during this engagement and after. The Designer will not use or disclose Confidential Information except as needed to do the work.

6.3 Return of materials. On termination or on Company request, the Designer will return all Confidential Information to the Company.

6.4 Injunctive relief. The Designer agrees that breach of this Article 6 would cause irreparable harm and that the Company can seek injunctive relief without posting bond.

ARTICLE 7 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS

7.1 Free to work for others. The Designer is free to work for other clients during this engagement. The Designer can have multiple clients at once — that's part of being an independent contractor.

7.2 Non-solicit. During this engagement and for six (6) months after, the Designer will not solicit any Company employee or contractor to leave the Company.

7.3 No use of Confidential Information for other clients. The Designer will not use any Company Confidential Information when doing work for other clients.

ARTICLE 8 — INDEMNIFICATION

8.1 By Designer. In addition to the tax indemnification in Section 4.6, the Designer will indemnify the Company against any claim that the Work Product infringes someone else's IP, and against any claim arising from the Designer's gross negligence or willful misconduct.

8.2 Cap. The Designer's total liability under this Agreement is capped at the total fees paid to the Designer under this Agreement, except for the tax indemnification in Section 4.6, the IP infringement indemnification in this Section 8.1, and any willful misconduct (which are uncapped).

ARTICLE 9 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND GOVERNING LAW

9.1 Governing law. This Agreement is governed by the laws of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

9.2 Venue. Any dispute will be brought in the state or federal courts in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

9.3 Equitable relief. The Company can seek injunctive relief in any court to enforce confidentiality and IP provisions.

ARTICLE 10 — MISCELLANEOUS

10.1 Entire agreement. This Agreement, plus any project briefs signed by both parties, is the entire agreement between the parties.

10.2 Amendment. Any amendment must be in writing signed by both parties.

10.3 Assignment. The Designer cannot assign the Agreement. The Company can assign to a successor.

10.4 Survival. Articles 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 survive termination.

10.5 Counterparts. This Agreement can be signed in counterparts.
