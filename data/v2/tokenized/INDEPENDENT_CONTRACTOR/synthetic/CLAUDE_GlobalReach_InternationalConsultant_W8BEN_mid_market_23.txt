INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT — INTERNATIONAL CONSULTANT (FORM W-8BEN, IRC § 1442 / 1446 WITHHOLDING)

This INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Company"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual residing at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Foreign Consultant"). The Company and the Foreign Consultant are collectively the "Parties." The Foreign Consultant is not a U.S. citizen or U.S. lawful permanent resident, and the Foreign Consultant performs the Services from outside the United States.

ARTICLE 1 — ENGAGEMENT

1.1 Engagement of Foreign Consultant. The Company hereby engages the Foreign Consultant to provide the consulting services described in Section 1.2 (the "Services") on the terms set forth in this Agreement.

1.2 Scope of Services. The Foreign Consultant shall provide market entry consulting services for the Company's expansion into the European market, including: (a) market research on EU customer segments, (b) competitive landscape analysis, (c) regulatory landscape analysis (GDPR, EU consumer protection law, EU sectoral regulation), (d) introductions to potential channel partners and customers in the EU, and (e) related advisory services as the Company's VP of International may reasonably request.

1.3 Place of Performance. The Foreign Consultant shall perform the Services from outside the United States. The Foreign Consultant shall not perform any portion of the Services within the United States. If the Foreign Consultant travels to the United States for any business reason, the Foreign Consultant shall not perform any Services for the Company while physically present in the United States, in order to avoid creating a U.S. tax nexus.

1.4 Personal Services. The Foreign Consultant shall personally perform the Services and shall not delegate or subcontract.

1.5 Best Efforts. The Foreign Consultant shall use the Foreign Consultant's best efforts in a professional manner.

ARTICLE 2 — TERM AND TERMINATION

2.1 Term. The Term shall commence on the Effective Date and continue for {{IC_TERM_MONTHS}} months thereafter.

2.2 Termination for Convenience. Either Party may terminate at any time on {{IC_NOTICE_DAYS_CONVENIENCE}} days' written notice.

2.3 Termination for Cause. The Company may terminate immediately for material breach, willful misconduct, or any breach of the export control or sanctions provisions in Article 5.

2.4 Effect of Termination. The Foreign Consultant returns work product, submits a final invoice, and surrenders any Company materials.

ARTICLE 3 — FEES, EXPENSES, COMPENSATION, AND U.S. TAX TREATMENT

3.1 Hourly Rate. The Company shall pay the Foreign Consultant at the hourly rate of {{IC_HOURLY_RATE}} per hour for time spent performing the Services.

3.2 Cap. The cumulative compensation under this Agreement shall not exceed {{IC_FIXED_FEE}} (the "Cap") without a written amendment.

3.3 Currency. All payments shall be made in United States Dollars to the Foreign Consultant's bank account designated in writing.

3.4 Invoicing. Monthly, due net 30. Each invoice shall identify the Foreign Consultant's country of tax residence and shall be accompanied by a current Form W-8BEN (or, if the Foreign Consultant operates through a non-U.S. entity, Form W-8BEN-E) on file with the Company.

3.5 Form W-8BEN. The Foreign Consultant shall, promptly upon execution of this Agreement, deliver to the Company a completed and signed IRS Form W-8BEN (Certificate of Foreign Status of Beneficial Owner for United States Tax Withholding and Reporting). The Foreign Consultant shall update the Form W-8BEN promptly upon any change in the Foreign Consultant's tax residence or treaty position.

3.6 No U.S. Trade or Business. The Foreign Consultant represents that the Foreign Consultant does not have a U.S. trade or business and shall not perform any Services in the United States that would create a U.S. trade or business or a U.S. permanent establishment.

3.7 U.S. Tax Withholding. To the extent any compensation paid under this Agreement is U.S.-source income subject to withholding under IRC § 1441 or 1442, the Company shall withhold U.S. federal income tax at the statutory rate of thirty percent (30%) unless a lower rate applies under an applicable income tax treaty between the United States and the Foreign Consultant's country of tax residence (in which case the Company shall withhold at the treaty rate, supported by the Foreign Consultant's Form W-8BEN claim).

3.8 Treaty Position. Where the Foreign Consultant claims a reduced withholding rate or exemption under an income tax treaty, the Foreign Consultant shall provide a Form W-8BEN with the treaty position completed and shall represent and warrant that the Foreign Consultant is entitled to the claimed treaty benefits.

3.9 Form 1042-S Reporting. The Company shall report compensation paid to the Foreign Consultant on IRS Form 1042-S (Foreign Person's U.S. Source Income Subject to Withholding) annually.

3.10 No Form W-9, No Form 1099. Because the Foreign Consultant is not a U.S. person, the Foreign Consultant shall not provide a Form W-9, and the Company shall not report compensation on Form 1099-NEC.

3.11 Expense Reimbursement. The Company will reimburse pre-approved travel and out-of-pocket expenses, with any individual expense above Five Hundred United States Dollars (US$500) requiring written pre-approval. Expense reimbursements are not generally subject to U.S. withholding.

3.12 Foreign Tax Compliance. The Foreign Consultant is solely responsible for complying with all tax obligations in the Foreign Consultant's country of tax residence and any other country where the Foreign Consultant has a tax obligation. The Foreign Consultant shall indemnify the Company against any non-U.S. tax claim relating to the Foreign Consultant's compensation.

ARTICLE 4 — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR RELATIONSHIP

4.1 No Employment Relationship. The Foreign Consultant is an independent contractor, not an employee of the Company.

4.2 No U.S. Employee Benefits. The Foreign Consultant is not entitled to any U.S. employee benefits.

4.3 No Foreign Employment. The Parties expressly do not intend to create an employment relationship under the laws of the Foreign Consultant's country of residence. The Foreign Consultant represents that the Foreign Consultant operates as an independent professional under the laws of the Foreign Consultant's country and is not engaged in employment with the Company within the meaning of those laws.

4.4 Foreign Consultant's Foreign Tax Status. The Foreign Consultant is solely responsible for the Foreign Consultant's foreign tax status, including registering with foreign tax authorities, paying foreign income tax, social security contributions, value-added tax (VAT) where applicable, and any other foreign tax obligations.

4.5 Tax Indemnification. The Foreign Consultant indemnifies the Company against (a) any claim by any U.S. or foreign taxing authority that the Foreign Consultant is or should be classified as an employee of the Company, (b) any claim by any foreign taxing authority that the Company is liable for tax on the Foreign Consultant's compensation, and (c) any failure by the Foreign Consultant to comply with foreign tax obligations.

ARTICLE 5 — EXPORT CONTROL, SANCTIONS, AND FATCA

5.1 Sanctions Compliance. The Foreign Consultant represents that the Foreign Consultant is not located in, or a resident of, any country or region that is subject to comprehensive U.S. economic sanctions (including without limitation Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, the Crimea region, the so-called Donetsk and Luhansk People's Republics, and any other sanctioned destination), and that the Foreign Consultant is not a Specially Designated National or otherwise on any U.S. sanctions list. The Foreign Consultant shall promptly notify the Company if any change occurs.

5.2 Export Control. The Foreign Consultant shall comply with U.S. and foreign export control laws (including the Export Administration Regulations and the International Traffic in Arms Regulations) and shall not export, re-export, or transfer any controlled technology, software, or technical data without proper authorization.

5.3 FATCA. To the extent applicable, the Foreign Consultant shall comply with the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and shall provide any FATCA-related certifications required by the Company.

5.4 Anti-Bribery / FCPA. The Foreign Consultant shall comply with the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and any applicable foreign anti-bribery laws (including the UK Bribery Act). The Foreign Consultant shall not offer or pay any bribe, kickback, or improper payment to any government official or other person in connection with the Services.

ARTICLE 6 — CONFIDENTIALITY

6.1 The Foreign Consultant shall hold all Company Confidential Information in strict confidence during the Term and at all times thereafter.

6.2 Cross-border data transfer. The Foreign Consultant acknowledges that any Company Confidential Information shared with the Foreign Consultant may be subject to data protection laws in the Foreign Consultant's country of residence (e.g., GDPR if the Foreign Consultant is in the EU). The Foreign Consultant shall handle such information in accordance with applicable data protection laws.

6.3 Return of Materials. On termination, the Foreign Consultant returns or deletes all Company Confidential Information.

6.4 Injunctive Relief. The Company may seek injunctive relief to enforce this Article 6.

ARTICLE 7 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS

7.1 During the Term, the Foreign Consultant shall not provide consulting services to direct competitors of the Company in the same product category.

7.2 During the Term and for twelve (12) months after, the Foreign Consultant shall not solicit any Company employee.

7.3 The Foreign Consultant shall not use Company Confidential Information when providing services to other clients.

7.4 The Parties acknowledge that post-engagement non-compete restrictions for foreign consultants vary in enforceability across jurisdictions, and that the restrictions in this Article 7 shall be enforced only to the extent permitted by the law of the Foreign Consultant's country of residence and any applicable U.S. forum.

ARTICLE 8 — INDEMNIFICATION AND LIABILITY

8.1 Indemnification by Foreign Consultant. The Foreign Consultant indemnifies the Company for breach of representations, breach of confidentiality, willful misconduct, breach of sanctions/export control/FCPA, and tax matters (in addition to the tax indemnification in Article 4).

8.2 Limitation. The Foreign Consultant's aggregate liability shall not exceed two (2) times the cumulative compensation under this Agreement, except for tax indemnification, breach of sanctions/export control/FCPA, willful misconduct, and fraud (uncapped).

ARTICLE 9 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND GOVERNING LAW

9.1 Governing Law. This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, without regard to conflict of laws principles.

9.2 Exclusive Venue; Arbitration. Any dispute arising out of this Agreement shall be settled by binding arbitration in {{VENUE_CITY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}, in accordance with the International Arbitration Rules of the International Centre for Dispute Resolution (ICDR). The Parties select arbitration over court proceedings because the Parties' international relationship makes a New York Convention-enforceable arbitration award more practical to enforce in the Foreign Consultant's country of residence.

9.3 Equitable Relief. The Company may seek equitable relief in any court of competent jurisdiction (including in the Foreign Consultant's country of residence) to enforce confidentiality and IP provisions.

ARTICLE 10 — MISCELLANEOUS

10.1 Entire Agreement.

10.2 Amendment in writing.

10.3 No assignment by Foreign Consultant.

10.4 Survival of Articles 3 (tax provisions), 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10.

10.5 Counterparts and electronic signatures.
