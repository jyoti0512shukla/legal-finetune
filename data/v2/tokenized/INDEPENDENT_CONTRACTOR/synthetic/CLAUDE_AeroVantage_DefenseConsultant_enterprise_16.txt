INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT — DEFENSE CONTRACTOR CONSULTANT (FAR / DFARS / ITAR)

This INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Prime Contractor"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual residing at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Consultant"). The Prime Contractor and the Consultant are collectively the "Parties." This Agreement is the Prime Contractor's standard form for individual consultant engagements supporting defense contracts and shall be governed by the Prime Contractor's negotiated terms set forth herein.

ARTICLE 1 — ENGAGEMENT

1.1 Engagement of Consultant. The Prime Contractor hereby engages the Consultant to provide the consulting services described in Section 1.2 (the "Services") in support of the Prime Contractor's prime contract with the U.S. Government identified in Schedule A.

1.2 Scope of Services. The Consultant shall provide engineering and program management consulting services in support of the Prime Contractor's prime contract, including: (a) systems engineering analysis, (b) program management support, (c) technical interchange meeting participation, (d) test plan and test data review, and (e) such other related services as the Prime Contractor's program manager may reasonably request.

1.3 Personal Services. The Consultant shall personally perform the Services and shall not delegate or subcontract.

1.4 Best Efforts. The Consultant shall use the Consultant's best efforts in a professional manner consistent with the Consultant's areas of expertise.

1.5 Reporting. The Consultant shall report to {{IC_REPORT_TO}}.

ARTICLE 2 — TERM AND TERMINATION

2.1 Term. The Term shall commence on the Effective Date and continue for {{IC_TERM_MONTHS}} months thereafter, unless earlier terminated.

2.2 Termination for Convenience. The Prime Contractor may terminate at any time and for any reason on {{IC_NOTICE_DAYS_CONVENIENCE}} days' written notice. The Consultant may terminate on thirty (30) days' notice.

2.3 Termination for Cause. The Prime Contractor may terminate immediately for: (a) material breach, (b) willful misconduct, (c) loss of any required security clearance, (d) any export control violation, (e) any conflict of interest the Consultant fails to disclose, or (f) the U.S. Government end-customer's direction to terminate.

2.4 Termination for Convenience by U.S. Government. If the U.S. Government terminates the Prime Contractor's underlying prime contract for convenience under FAR 52.249-2 (or equivalent), the Prime Contractor may terminate this Agreement on the same basis. The Consultant shall be paid for time worked through the termination date plus any settlement costs the Prime Contractor recovers from the U.S. Government attributable to the Consultant's wind-down.

2.5 Effect of Termination. The Consultant returns work product, surrenders all controlled technical data, submits a final invoice, and surrenders any Prime Contractor or U.S. Government credentials.

ARTICLE 3 — FEES, EXPENSES, AND COMPENSATION

3.1 Hourly Rate. The Prime Contractor shall pay the Consultant at the hourly rate of {{IC_HOURLY_RATE}} per hour, subject to the not-to-exceed cap in Section 3.2.

3.2 Not-to-Exceed Cap. The cumulative compensation under this Agreement shall not exceed {{IC_FIXED_FEE}} (the "NTE Cap") without a written amendment.

3.3 Time Tracking. The Consultant shall maintain detailed time records consistent with the Prime Contractor's program time-keeping requirements (which may be subject to U.S. Government audit under FAR Subpart 42.7).

3.4 Invoicing. Monthly, with detail consistent with the Prime Contractor's program controls. Due net 60.

3.5 Expense Reimbursement. The Prime Contractor reimburses pre-approved expenses, subject to FAR cost principles and the Prime Contractor's standard policies. Any expense above Five Hundred United States Dollars (US$500) requires written pre-approval.

3.6 Audit Rights. The Consultant agrees that the Consultant's time records, invoices, and expense documentation are subject to audit by the Prime Contractor and by the Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) on the same terms as other costs charged to the prime contract.

3.7 No Additional Compensation. No equity, bonus, severance.

ARTICLE 4 — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR RELATIONSHIP

4.1 No Employment Relationship. The Consultant is an independent contractor, not an employee of the Prime Contractor.

4.2 No Withholding.

4.3 No Employee Benefits.

4.4 Form W-9; Form 1099-NEC. The Consultant shall provide a Form W-9. The Prime Contractor shall report compensation on Form 1099-NEC.

4.5 Self-Employment Taxes. The Consultant is solely responsible for all federal, state, and local income taxes, self-employment taxes, Social Security and Medicare contributions.

4.6 Tax Indemnification. The Consultant indemnifies the Prime Contractor against any reclassification claim or tax authority claim relating to the Consultant's compensation under this Agreement.

ARTICLE 5 — EXPORT CONTROL AND SECURITY

5.1 ITAR / EAR Compliance. The Consultant shall comply with the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR, 22 C.F.R. Parts 120-130) and the Export Administration Regulations (EAR, 15 C.F.R. Parts 730-774) in performing the Services. The Consultant shall not export, re-export, transfer, or release any controlled technical data, software, or hardware to any non-U.S. person or to any country embargoed by the U.S. Government, except as expressly authorized by an applicable export license.

5.2 U.S. Person Status. The Consultant shall be a "U.S. person" within the meaning of 22 C.F.R. § 120.62 (i.e., a U.S. citizen, U.S. lawful permanent resident, or person granted asylum or refugee status). If the Consultant's U.S. person status changes, the Consultant shall promptly notify the Prime Contractor and shall cease performance under this Agreement until the Parties agree on alternative arrangements.

5.3 Personnel Security Clearance. To the extent the Services require access to classified information, the Consultant shall hold the security clearance level identified in Schedule A. Loss or downgrade of the required security clearance constitutes Cause for termination under Section 2.3.

5.4 CMMC Compliance. The Consultant shall comply with the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) level identified in Schedule A and shall maintain that certification level throughout the Term. Loss of the required CMMC level shall be a material breach.

5.5 Background Check. The Consultant shall complete the Prime Contractor's standard background check, including any requirements imposed by the U.S. Government end-customer.

ARTICLE 6 — CONFIDENTIALITY AND CONTROLLED UNCLASSIFIED INFORMATION

6.1 Confidential Information. All non-public information of the Prime Contractor, the U.S. Government end-customer, and any other party with which the Prime Contractor has a confidential relationship constitutes Confidential Information.

6.2 Controlled Unclassified Information. The Consultant shall handle Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) in accordance with 32 C.F.R. Part 2002 and the Prime Contractor's CUI handling procedures.

6.3 No Personal Devices for CUI. The Consultant shall not store CUI on personal devices, in personal email, or in any non-Prime-Contractor-approved storage.

6.4 Confidentiality Obligation. The Consultant shall hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence during the Term and at all times thereafter.

6.5 Return of Materials. On termination, the Consultant shall return all Confidential Information, including all CUI, controlled technical data, and any Prime Contractor or U.S. Government property.

6.6 Cooperation with Government Investigations. The Consultant shall cooperate with any U.S. Government investigation of the Prime Contractor's program.

ARTICLE 7 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

7.1 Non-Competition During Engagement. During the Term, the Consultant shall not provide consulting services on the same program or competing program to any other prime contractor or subcontractor working on the U.S. Government end-customer's procurement.

7.2 Non-Solicitation. During the Term and for twelve (12) months after, the Consultant shall not solicit any Prime Contractor employee.

7.3 Conflicts of Interest. The Consultant shall promptly disclose any actual or potential organizational conflict of interest under FAR Subpart 9.5.

7.4 No Lobbying. The Consultant shall not lobby the U.S. Government on behalf of the Prime Contractor or any related party. The Consultant's role is technical, not legislative.

7.5 No Personal Procurement Influence. The Consultant shall not attempt to influence the U.S. Government's procurement decisions on any matter outside the Consultant's narrow technical role.

ARTICLE 8 — REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES

8.1 Authority. The Consultant has full authority to enter this Agreement.

8.2 No Conflicts. The Consultant is not subject to any agreement that would prevent the Services.

8.3 U.S. Person Status. The Consultant represents and warrants that the Consultant is a U.S. person within the meaning of 22 C.F.R. § 120.62.

8.4 Security Clearance. The Consultant holds the security clearance level identified in Schedule A.

8.5 No Debarment. The Consultant is not debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment from federal procurement under FAR Subpart 9.4 or any state or international debarment list.

8.6 Compliance with Law. The Consultant will comply with all applicable U.S. and State laws in performing the Services.

ARTICLE 9 — INDEMNIFICATION AND FAR FLOW-DOWNS

9.1 Indemnification by Consultant. In addition to the tax indemnification in Section 4.6, the Consultant indemnifies the Prime Contractor for any breach of representations and warranties, breach of export control laws, willful misconduct, or gross negligence.

9.2 Limitation. The Consultant's aggregate liability under Article 4 and this Article 9 shall not exceed three (3) times the cumulative compensation under this Agreement, except for tax indemnification, export control violations, willful misconduct, and fraud (uncapped).

9.3 FAR Flow-Down Clauses. The FAR and DFARS clauses identified in Schedule B are incorporated by reference into this Agreement and flow down to the Consultant. The Consultant shall comply with all such flow-down clauses, including without limitation those relating to equal employment opportunity, prevention of human trafficking, and counterfeit electronic parts.

9.4 Professional Liability Insurance. The Consultant shall maintain professional liability insurance with coverage of at least Two Million United States Dollars (US$2,000,000) per claim and provide a certificate of insurance.

ARTICLE 10 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND GOVERNING LAW

10.1 Governing Law. This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

10.2 Exclusive Venue. Disputes shall be brought in the state or federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

10.3 Equitable Relief. The Prime Contractor may seek equitable relief to enforce export control, confidentiality, and restrictive covenant provisions.

10.4 U.S. Government Audit Rights. The U.S. Government end-customer shall have audit rights as required by applicable FAR and DFARS clauses.

11.2 Amendment in writing.
