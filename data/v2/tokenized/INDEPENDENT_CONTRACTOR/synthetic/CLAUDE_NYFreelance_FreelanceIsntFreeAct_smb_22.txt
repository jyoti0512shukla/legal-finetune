INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT — NEW YORK FREELANCE ISN'T FREE ACT COMPLIANT

This INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} located at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Hiring Party"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual residing at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Freelance Worker"). The Hiring Party and the Freelance Worker are collectively the "Parties." This Agreement is intended to satisfy the written-contract requirement of the New York City Freelance Isn't Free Act (NYC Admin. Code §§ 20-927 to 20-935) and the New York State Freelance Isn't Free Act (NY Labor Law §§ 191-d, 198-c, and General Business Law § 199-d), each as applicable to engagements with freelance workers in New York.

ARTICLE 1 — ENGAGEMENT

1.1 Engagement of Freelance Worker. The Hiring Party hereby engages the Freelance Worker to provide the services described in Section 1.2 (the "Services") as an independent freelance worker.

1.2 Scope of Services. The Freelance Worker shall provide professional copywriting services, including blog posts, email newsletters, landing page copy, and white papers, on a per-deliverable basis. Each project will be specified in a written Project Order signed (or accepted by email) by both Parties identifying the deliverable, target completion date, word count or scope, and per-project fee.

1.3 Personal Services. The Freelance Worker shall personally perform the Services and shall not delegate or subcontract.

1.4 Best Efforts. The Freelance Worker shall use the Freelance Worker's best efforts in a professional manner.

ARTICLE 2 — TERM AND TERMINATION

2.1 Term. This Agreement starts on the Effective Date and continues until either Party terminates it.

2.2 Termination for Convenience. Either Party may terminate at any time on {{IC_NOTICE_DAYS_CONVENIENCE}} days' written notice. Projects already accepted under a Project Order will be completed under this Agreement.

2.3 Termination for Cause. The Hiring Party may terminate immediately for material breach.

ARTICLE 3 — FEES, EXPENSES, AND COMPENSATION

3.1 Per-Project Fees. The fee for each project is set forth in the Project Order.

3.2 Payment Timing — Freelance Isn't Free Act Compliance. Pursuant to NYC Admin. Code § 20-929 and NY Labor Law § 191-d, the Hiring Party shall pay the Freelance Worker on or before the date payment is due under each Project Order or, if no specific payment date is stated, no later than thirty (30) days after the Freelance Worker has completed the services described in the Project Order.

3.3 Default Payment Terms. Unless otherwise specified in a Project Order, the default payment term is thirty (30) days after the Freelance Worker delivers the completed deliverable.

3.4 Pre-completion Modification. The Hiring Party shall not, as a condition of timely payment, require the Freelance Worker to accept less compensation than the amount agreed in the Project Order. Any modification of the agreed compensation must be in writing signed by both Parties.

3.5 Late Payment. If the Hiring Party fails to make timely payment as required by Section 3.2, the Freelance Worker may, in addition to seeking the unpaid amounts, pursue the statutory remedies available under the New York City Freelance Isn't Free Act and the New York State Freelance Isn't Free Act, including double damages, statutory damages, and reasonable attorneys' fees.

3.6 No Retaliation. The Hiring Party shall not retaliate against the Freelance Worker for exercising any rights under the Freelance Isn't Free Act, including by terminating, refusing to engage in future projects with, or otherwise penalizing the Freelance Worker for asserting Freelance Isn't Free Act rights.

3.7 Expense Reimbursement. The Hiring Party will reimburse pre-approved out-of-pocket expenses (paid research subscriptions, paid stock images), with any individual expense above One Hundred United States Dollars (US$100) requiring written pre-approval.

ARTICLE 4 — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR RELATIONSHIP

4.1 No Employment Relationship. The Freelance Worker is an independent contractor, not an employee of the Hiring Party.

4.2 No Withholding. The Hiring Party shall not withhold any federal, state, or local income taxes, FICA contributions, or other employment-related withholdings.

4.3 No Employee Benefits. The Freelance Worker is not entitled to any benefits provided to Hiring Party employees.

4.4 Form W-9; Form 1099-NEC. The Freelance Worker shall provide a Form W-9. The Hiring Party shall report compensation on Form 1099-NEC.

4.5 Self-Employment Taxes. The Freelance Worker is responsible for all federal, state, and local income taxes, self-employment taxes, Social Security and Medicare contributions.

4.6 Tax Indemnification. The Freelance Worker indemnifies the Hiring Party against any reclassification claim or tax authority claim.

4.7 Free to Work for Other Clients. The Freelance Worker is and remains free to provide copywriting services to other clients during this engagement and after.

ARTICLE 5 — WORK PRODUCT AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

5.1 Work Product. The Hiring Party owns all completed copywriting deliverables (the "Work Product") once the Hiring Party has paid in full for the relevant project.

5.2 Work Made for Hire. The Work Product is "work made for hire" to the maximum extent allowed; to the extent it doesn't qualify, the Freelance Worker assigns it to the Hiring Party.

5.3 Background IP. The Freelance Worker retains rights in the Freelance Worker's pre-existing writing samples, frameworks, and templates, with a non-exclusive license to the Hiring Party for any incorporated into the Work Product.

5.4 Portfolio Rights. The Freelance Worker may include the Work Product in the Freelance Worker's professional portfolio with attribution to the Hiring Party, unless the Hiring Party expressly designates a project as confidential.

5.5 Original work. The Freelance Worker represents that the Work Product will be original.

ARTICLE 6 — CONFIDENTIALITY

6.1 The Freelance Worker will hold all Hiring Party Confidential Information in strict confidence during the engagement and after.

6.2 The Freelance Worker will not use Confidential Information for any purpose other than the work for the Hiring Party.

6.3 On termination, the Freelance Worker returns or deletes Confidential Information.

ARTICLE 7 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS

7.1 Free to Work for Others. As stated in Section 4.7, the Freelance Worker is free to work for other clients (including the Hiring Party's competitors) during and after this engagement.

7.2 Non-Solicitation. During the Term and for six (6) months after, the Freelance Worker shall not solicit any Hiring Party employee.

7.3 No Post-Engagement Non-Compete. There is no post-engagement non-compete. The Freelance Worker is free to write for any client after this engagement ends.

ARTICLE 8 — INDEMNIFICATION

The Freelance Worker indemnifies the Hiring Party for breach of confidentiality, IP infringement of the Work Product, and willful misconduct, in addition to the tax indemnification in Article 4.

The Freelance Worker's total liability under this Agreement is capped at the total fees paid by the Hiring Party.

ARTICLE 9 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND GOVERNING LAW

9.1 Governing Law. This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of New York.

9.2 Exclusive Venue. Disputes shall be brought in the state or federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, New York. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Freelance Worker may bring any claim under the Freelance Isn't Free Act in any court of competent jurisdiction in New York City or New York State, in accordance with the venue provisions of those statutes.

9.3 Statutory Remedies Preserved. Nothing in this Agreement limits the Freelance Worker's right to pursue statutory remedies under the Freelance Isn't Free Act.

9.4 Equitable Relief. The Hiring Party may seek equitable relief to enforce confidentiality and IP provisions.

ARTICLE 10 — MISCELLANEOUS

10.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement plus signed Project Orders constitutes the entire agreement. The minimum-contract requirement of the Freelance Isn't Free Act (for engagements valued at $800 or more between the same Hiring Party and Freelance Worker over a 120-day period) is satisfied by this Agreement.

10.2 Amendment in writing signed by both parties.

10.3 No assignment by Freelance Worker.

10.4 Survival of Articles 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10.

10.5 Counterparts and electronic signatures.
