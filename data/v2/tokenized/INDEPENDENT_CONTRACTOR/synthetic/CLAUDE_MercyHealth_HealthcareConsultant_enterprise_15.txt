INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT — HEALTHCARE CONSULTANT (HIPAA BUSINESS ASSOCIATE)

This INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} (the "Effective Date") by and between {{PROVIDER_NAME}}, a {{PROVIDER_STATE}} {{PROVIDER_ENTITY_TYPE}} having its principal place of business at {{PROVIDER_ADDRESS}} (the "Hospital System"), and {{CUSTOMER_NAME}}, an individual residing at {{CUSTOMER_ADDRESS}} (the "Consultant"). The Hospital System and the Consultant are collectively the "Parties." This Agreement is the Hospital System's standard form for individual healthcare consultant engagements and shall be governed by the Hospital System's negotiated terms set forth herein.

ARTICLE 1 — ENGAGEMENT

1.1 Engagement of Consultant. The Hospital System hereby engages the Consultant to provide the consulting services described in Section 1.2 (the "Services") on the terms set forth in this Agreement.

1.2 Scope of Services. The Consultant shall provide professional consulting services to the Hospital System's Quality and Patient Safety organization, including: (a) advising on the Hospital System's clinical quality improvement program, (b) facilitating root-cause analysis of selected adverse events and near-miss events, (c) supporting the Hospital System's Joint Commission re-accreditation preparation, (d) advising on patient safety culture survey methodology, and (e) such other quality and patient safety advisory services as the Hospital System's Chief Medical Officer may reasonably request.

1.3 Personal Services. The Consultant shall personally perform the Services and shall not delegate or subcontract without Hospital System prior written consent.

1.4 Best Efforts. The Consultant shall use the Consultant's best efforts to perform the Services in a professional manner consistent with the standards of the Consultant's profession.

1.5 Reporting. The Consultant shall report to {{IC_REPORT_TO}} and shall provide written status reports on a monthly basis.

ARTICLE 2 — TERM AND TERMINATION

2.1 Term. The Term shall commence on the Effective Date and shall continue for {{IC_TERM_MONTHS}} months thereafter, unless earlier terminated or extended.

2.2 Termination for Convenience. Either Party may terminate on {{IC_NOTICE_DAYS_CONVENIENCE}} days' prior written notice.

2.3 Termination for Cause. The Hospital System may terminate immediately for: (a) the Consultant's material breach, (b) willful misconduct or gross negligence, (c) loss of any professional license or board certification, (d) exclusion from federal health care programs (OIG LEIE / SAM), (e) any breach of HIPAA or the Business Associate Agreement, or (f) conviction of any felony or any health care fraud offense.

2.4 Effect of Termination. On termination, the Consultant returns work product, submits a final invoice, and surrenders Hospital System credentials and badges.

ARTICLE 3 — FEES, EXPENSES, AND COMPENSATION

3.1 Hourly Rate. The Hospital System shall pay the Consultant at the hourly rate of {{IC_HOURLY_RATE}} per hour, subject to the not-to-exceed cap in Section 3.2.

3.2 Not-to-Exceed Cap. The cumulative compensation under this Agreement shall not exceed {{IC_FIXED_FEE}} (the "NTE Cap") without a written amendment.

3.3 Time Tracking. The Consultant shall maintain detailed time records.

3.4 Invoicing. Monthly, due net forty-five (45) days.

3.5 Expense Reimbursement. The Hospital System reimburses pre-approved expenses, with any individual expense over Five Hundred United States Dollars (US$500) requiring written pre-approval.

3.6 Stark Law and Anti-Kickback Compliance. The Consultant's compensation under this Agreement is set at fair market value, is commercially reasonable, and is not determined in any manner that takes into account the volume or value of any referrals or business otherwise generated between the Consultant and the Hospital System. This Agreement is intended to comply with the personal services arrangement exception to the Physician Self-Referral Law (Stark Law, 42 U.S.C. § 1395nn) and the personal services and management contracts safe harbor under the Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b). For Stark Law compliance, the Consultant's compensation is set in advance, does not exceed fair market value for the Services, and is not determined by reference to the value or volume of referrals.

3.7 No Additional Compensation. The fees set forth in this Article 3 are the Consultant's entire compensation. No equity, bonus, severance, or other compensation.

ARTICLE 4 — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR RELATIONSHIP

4.1 No Employment Relationship. The Parties expressly agree they are creating an independent contractor relationship. The Consultant is not an employee of the Hospital System and is not eligible for any benefits.

4.2 No Withholding. The Hospital System shall not withhold any taxes from the Consultant's compensation.

4.3 No Employee Benefits. No medical, dental, retirement, paid leave, workers' compensation, or unemployment insurance.

4.4 Form W-9; Form 1099-NEC. The Consultant shall provide a Form W-9. The Hospital System shall report compensation on Form 1099-NEC.

4.5 Self-Employment Taxes. The Consultant is solely responsible for all federal, state, and local income taxes, self-employment taxes, Social Security and Medicare contributions.

4.6 Tax Indemnification. The Consultant shall indemnify the Hospital System against any reclassification claim or tax authority claim relating to the Consultant's compensation.

4.7 License and Eligibility Maintenance. The Consultant shall maintain (a) an active, unrestricted license in the Consultant's profession in the state in which the Consultant is licensed, (b) board certification (where applicable), and (c) eligibility to participate in Medicare, Medicaid, and other federal health care programs (i.e., not on the OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities or the GSA SAM exclusion list). The Hospital System shall verify the Consultant's eligibility against the OIG LEIE and the SAM exclusion list monthly during the Term, and the Consultant agrees to such verification.

ARTICLE 5 — HIPAA AND BUSINESS ASSOCIATE OBLIGATIONS

5.1 Business Associate Status. To the extent the Consultant has access to or creates Protected Health Information (PHI) on behalf of the Hospital System in performing the Services, the Consultant is a Business Associate of the Hospital System within the meaning of HIPAA, and the Parties shall execute a separate Business Associate Agreement on the Hospital System's standard form. That BAA shall be incorporated into this Agreement by reference, and any conflict between this Agreement and the BAA shall be resolved in favor of the BAA on PHI matters.

5.2 Minimum Necessary. The Consultant shall access only the minimum PHI necessary to perform the Services.

5.3 No PHI in Personal Devices. The Consultant shall not store PHI on the Consultant's personal devices, in personal email accounts, or in any non-Hospital-System-approved storage. All PHI access shall be through the Hospital System's approved systems.

5.4 Breach Notification. If the Consultant becomes aware of any potential breach of unsecured PHI, the Consultant shall notify the Hospital System immediately and cooperate in the Hospital System's breach response.

5.5 HHS OCR Cooperation. The Consultant shall cooperate with any U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights investigation relating to the Hospital System's PHI handling.

ARTICLE 6 — CONFIDENTIALITY

6.1 Confidential Information. All non-public information of the Hospital System, including business plans, financial information, customer lists, regulatory examination materials, peer review and quality improvement materials, and any other non-public information, constitutes Confidential Information.

6.2 Confidentiality Obligation. The Consultant shall hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence during the Term and at all times thereafter.

6.3 Peer Review Privilege. To the extent any of the Consultant's work involves peer review activities, the Consultant agrees that materials and communications generated in connection with such activities are intended to be protected under the applicable State peer review privilege. The Consultant shall handle peer review materials in accordance with the Hospital System's peer review protocols.

6.4 Return of Materials. On termination, the Consultant returns all Confidential Information.

6.5 Injunctive Relief. The Hospital System may seek injunctive relief without bond.

ARTICLE 7 — RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS

7.1 Non-Competition During Engagement. During the Term, the Consultant shall not provide consulting or advisory services on quality improvement or patient safety to any other hospital or health system that competes directly with the Hospital System in the Hospital System's primary geographic market.

7.2 Non-Solicitation. The Consultant shall not solicit any Hospital System employee or medical staff member during the Term and for twelve (12) months thereafter.

7.3 Conflicts of Interest. The Consultant shall promptly disclose any actual or potential conflicts.

7.4 Continuing Practice Permitted. Notwithstanding Section 7.1, the Consultant remains free to practice the Consultant's profession (medicine, nursing, etc.) within the restricted geographic area, and the restriction in Section 7.1 applies only to advisory and consulting services, not clinical practice.

ARTICLE 8 — INDEMNIFICATION AND INSURANCE

8.1 Indemnification by Consultant. In addition to the tax indemnification in Section 4.6, the Consultant shall indemnify the Hospital System for any breach of HIPAA or the BAA, breach of representations or warranties, willful misconduct, or gross negligence.

8.2 Limitation. The Consultant's aggregate liability under Article 4 and this Article 8 shall not exceed three (3) times the cumulative compensation under this Agreement, except for tax indemnification, breach of HIPAA, willful misconduct, and fraud (uncapped).

8.3 Professional Liability Insurance. The Consultant shall maintain professional liability insurance with coverage limits of at least Three Million United States Dollars (US$3,000,000) per claim and in the aggregate, covering the Consultant's professional services as a healthcare consultant, and shall provide the Hospital System with a certificate of insurance.

ARTICLE 9 — DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND GOVERNING LAW

9.2 Exclusive Venue. Disputes shall be brought in the state or federal courts located in {{VENUE_COUNTY}}, {{GOVERNING_LAW_STATE}}.

9.3 Equitable Relief. The Hospital System may seek equitable relief to enforce HIPAA, confidentiality, and restrictive covenant provisions.
